<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can change a *Subdomain's* hosting and HTTPS settings and check its DNS.
</comp.Notice>

Every <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings, whether it serves <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm> or redirects visitors to another domain. The settings belong to the *Subdomain*, not to each *Site*, so any change applies to every *Site* on the *Subdomain*. For more information on the hosting options and when to choose each one, please read our documentation on [what a Subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [added a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/).

## Open the page for a Subdomain

Each *Subdomain* has its own page, with a *Hosting*, *HTTPS*, and *DNS* section.

To open the page for a *Subdomain*:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
4. Click the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain you want to configure. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
5. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will open the list of *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*.
6. Click the *Subdomain* in the list.

CloudCannon will open the page for the *Subdomain*.

You can also reach the same settings from the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/context-menu.yml">Context Menu</comp.GlossaryTerm> of each *Subdomain* in the list, which has options to configure its hosting or HTTPS, check its DNS now, and view its check history. To change the hosting or HTTPS of a *Subdomain* that serves a *Site*, you can also use the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* for that *Site*.

## Change the hosting option for a Subdomain

Every new *Subdomain* starts on <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>. You can switch it to <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm> or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Each hosting option needs a different DNS record, so changing the hosting option can mean updating the record at your DNS provider.

To change the hosting option for a *Subdomain*:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Configure* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
3. Select the hosting option you want. CloudCannon will show the DNS record the option needs, and warn you if the record has to change.
4. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will change how visitors reach the *Subdomain*. If the record has to change, update it at your DNS provider. For the records each hosting option needs, and for setting up *Cloudflare Proxy*, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

### The hosting diagram

The *Hosting* section shows how visitors reach the *Subdomain* as a diagram. The diagram depends on the hosting option:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — *Visitors*, the *CloudCannon CDN*, and the *CloudCannon origin*.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — *Visitors*, *Your Cloudflare*, and the *CloudCannon origin*.
* **Single Origin** — *Visitors* and the *CloudCannon origin*. Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> uses this diagram too, because its A records point straight at the CloudCannon origin.

Each connection in the diagram is labeled with its state, such as *Reaching CloudCannon*, *Not reaching CloudCannon*, *Waiting for DNS to update*, *Not checked yet*, or *Connected securely*. A green line means the connection works, and a red line with an exclamation mark shows where visitors stop reaching CloudCannon. Click a connection to see what CloudCannon found, including the DNS records, the result of its test request, and when it last checked, along with what to change if the connection isn't working. The *View the DNS record* button (*View the DNS records* when there is more than one) scrolls to the *DNS* section, the *Configure hosting* button opens the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal, the *Check DNS now* button runs a new check, and the *View check history* button opens the history of checks. Press Escape or click the close button to close the details.

## Change how a Subdomain gets its certificate

Each *Subdomain* has its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm>. The *Configure* button in the *HTTPS* section opens the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal, where you choose whether CloudCannon issues and renews the certificate or uses a certificate you upload, and whether to redirect http:// visitors to https://. The *HTTPS* section also explains the state of the certificate, and what to do if it needs attention.

For instructions, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/), [adding a custom SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-ssl-certificate/), and [resolving SSL certificate errors](/documentation/developer-articles/resolve-ssl-certificate-errors/).

## Check the DNS for a Subdomain

The *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page says whether the *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon, and shows the record the *Subdomain* *Needs*, the records CloudCannon *Found*, the *Status* of the record, and when CloudCannon last checked it. The status is one of:

* **Connected** — The *Subdomain* reaches CloudCannon.
* **Not pointing at CloudCannon** — A record for the *Subdomain* points somewhere else.
* **No DNS record** — CloudCannon found no record for the *Subdomain*.
* **Not checked yet** — CloudCannon hasn't checked the *Subdomain* yet.
* **Waiting for DNS** — You added the *Subdomain* or marked its record as updated in the last 48 hours, and CloudCannon is still looking for the record. The 48 hours also start again when you change the *Subdomain's* hosting or your domain's DNS provider. After 48 hours, the status shows what CloudCannon found. CloudCannon checks daily, and hourly for up to seven days after you mark the record as updated.
* **Waiting for nameservers** — On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, the nameservers of your domain don't point at CloudCannon DNS yet. The record answers once they do.

If the *Subdomain* isn't connected, the sentence above the record says what to do:

* **CloudCannon DNS** — Click the *Fix* button, and CloudCannon replaces or removes the records that stop the *Subdomain* from reaching CloudCannon. Fixing the record also requires the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. For more information, please read our documentation on [fixing the DNS record for a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/#fix-the-dns-record-for-a-subdomain).
* **External DNS** — Add the record at your DNS provider, using the *Copy* button next to its value, then click the *I've updated it* button. CloudCannon checks hourly for up to seven days, and emails you when the *Subdomain* is live. If CloudCannon still can't find the record 48 hours after you marked it as updated, the section lists what usually fixes it. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding the records](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#add-the-records).

When the *Subdomain* has TXT records to add for its certificate, the *DNS* section points you to them under *HTTPS*.

To check the DNS for a *Subdomain*:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section.
3. (Optional.) Open the *DNS checks for [Subdomain]* modal with the *History* button.

CloudCannon will check the *Subdomain's* DNS and update the *DNS* section.

The *DNS checks for [Subdomain]* modal lists earlier checks, most recent first. Back-to-back checks with the same result share a row. Each row shows the result of the check, the DNS answer CloudCannon received, whether a test request reached CloudCannon, and when the check ran. A check that couldn't complete is marked *Couldn't check*, and doesn't change the *Subdomain's* status. Click the *Load older results* button to see more. CloudCannon removes results older than 90 days, except the latest complete check.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-Checks-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the DNS checks for blog.example.com modal shows the record the Subdomain needs, a list of checks with their result, DNS answer, test request outcome, and time, including a Connected check and a check that couldn't complete, and the Load older results button." title="The DNS checks modal" type="screenshot"/>

You can check the DNS for each *Subdomain* at most once a minute. CloudCannon also checks every *Subdomain* daily, and, on external DNS, every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button. If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the section says when the check failed, and keeps the last known status.
