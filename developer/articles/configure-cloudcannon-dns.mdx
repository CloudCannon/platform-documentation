<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions, can view and change the DNS of a *Base Domain*.
</comp.Notice>

CloudCannon DNS allows you to manage your DNS records within CloudCannon for each [Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/) connected to your [Organization](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-an-organization/). CloudCannon adds the records each subdomain needs, and the TXT records for your certificates, for you.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

## Enable CloudCannon DNS

*CloudCannon DNS* is the default DNS provider when you connect a *Custom Domain*. If your domain uses *External DNS*, you can switch it to *CloudCannon DNS* on the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Updating your DNS provider can cause downtime for anything connected to your domain, such as your website or custom email address. Please make sure you have a copy of your existing DNS records before you change providers.

  If you need help updating your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, your current DNS provider, and a copy of your existing DNS records.
</comp.Notice>

To switch to CloudCannon DNS:

1. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Domain Settings* tab. CloudCannon will open the *Details* section.
4. Under *DNS provider*, select the *CloudCannon DNS* option.
5. Click the *Switch to CloudCannon DNS* button.

CloudCannon will create a DNS zone for your domain and add the records CloudCannon needs. While you choose, CloudCannon labels the provider you are switching from *(current)* and lists *What happens when you switch*.

Nothing changes for visitors until you change the nameservers at your domain registrar. Before you change them, add your other records, such as mail and site verification records, on the *DNS* tab.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Settings-Details-Switch-To-CloudCannon-DNS" alt="A screenshot of the Details section on the Domain Settings tab shows CloudCannon DNS selected under DNS provider, External DNS marked (current), and the What happens when you switch steps." title="The Details section on the Domain Settings tab" type="screenshot" />

You can also open the *Details* section from the *DNS* tab, by clicking the *change the DNS provider* link below the *CloudCannon DNS nameservers* heading at the top of the tab.

## Move from the legacy CloudCannon DNS

Some older *Custom Domains* use the legacy CloudCannon DNS, where you can't add or change DNS records. Moving your domain to the new CloudCannon DNS creates a DNS zone with a copy of its current records, where you can add and edit them. Nothing changes for visitors until you update the nameservers at your domain registrar.

Until you move, the setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page shows *Move [Base Domain] to the new CloudCannon DNS*. On the *DNS* tab, the *CloudCannon DNS nameservers* heading has a *How do I move from the legacy CloudCannon DNS?* link and says *The legacy CloudCannon DNS answers for [Base Domain], and its records cannot be changed.* Below that, the tab shows the same setup guide with the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button, and lists the current records, which are read-only.

Moving requires permission to edit both the settings and the DNS of the *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions). Without both, the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button is disabled, and hovering over it shows which permission you need.

To move to the new CloudCannon DNS:

1. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain you want to move by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button in the setup guide.

CloudCannon will create a DNS zone for your domain and copy in its current records. Check the copied records on the *DNS* tab, then [point your domain at CloudCannon DNS](#point-your-domain-at-cloudcannon-dns). You can also click the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button on the *DNS* tab.

[docshot]

You can also move from the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab, where *DNS provider* shows *Legacy CloudCannon DNS* as the current option. Select the *CloudCannon DNS* option and click the *Switch to CloudCannon DNS* button.

If you need a record changed urgently before you can move your domain, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include your *Base Domain* and the record you want added, changed, or removed.

## Point your domain at CloudCannon DNS

Once you enable CloudCannon DNS, you need to replace the nameservers at your domain registrar with CloudCannon's nameservers. Until you do, a setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page walks you through each step. The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same guide for any *Site* on the domain, and notes that the *Site* stays on its *Testing Domain* until your domain goes live.

The setup guide shows a three-step tracker (*Update your registrar*, *Propagation*, and *[Base Domain] goes live*) and a heading for the step you are on:

* **Getting [Base Domain] ready** — CloudCannon is creating the DNS zone for your domain. Your nameservers show once the zone is ready, usually in a moment.
* **CloudCannon could not set up the DNS zone for [Base Domain]** — CloudCannon couldn't create the DNS zone, and shows Cloudflare's reason. Click the *Retry zone creation* button to try again.
* **Point [Base Domain] at CloudCannon DNS** — Update the nameservers at your domain registrar.
* **[Base Domain] is on its way** — Your registrar is handing your domain over to CloudCannon DNS. Most handovers finish within an hour, and almost all within 48 hours.
* **Your registrar still lists the old nameservers for [Base Domain]** — CloudCannon still can't see your new nameservers 48 hours after you updated them.
* **[Base Domain]'s nameservers changed** — Your domain was live on CloudCannon DNS, but its nameservers no longer point at CloudCannon.

If the zone still can't be created after you retry, contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include your *Base Domain* and the reason Cloudflare gave in the setup guide.

### Add your existing records

Unless you moved from the legacy CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon doesn't copy records from your current DNS provider. Records at your current DNS provider stop working once you switch nameservers, so add them to CloudCannon DNS first. These usually include MX records for email and TXT records for site verification.

The first step of the setup guide, *1. Add your existing records*, has an *Open DNS records* button on the *Summary* tab, which opens the *DNS* tab. On the *DNS* tab, the same step has a *Jump to DNS records* button, which scrolls down to the *DNS records* section. For the steps to add a record, please read the section on [adding DNS records](#add-dns-records).

### Update your nameservers

The second step of the setup guide, *2. Update your nameservers*, shows a table with one row for your domain. It compares the full list of nameservers your domain *Needs* with the list CloudCannon *Found* at your registrar, and gives one *Status* for the domain. The check passes only when your registrar lists exactly CloudCannon's nameservers. If your registrar lists all of CloudCannon's nameservers alongside others, hover over the status to see which nameservers to remove.

Configuring your nameservers requires you to use the services provided by your domain registrar. For support, please contact your domain registrar.

CloudCannon DNS doesn't support DNSSEC. If DNSSEC is turned on for your domain at your registrar, turn it off before you update your nameservers. Otherwise, resolvers that check DNSSEC reject the answers from CloudCannon DNS, and your domain stops resolving for many visitors.

To update your nameservers:

1. Log in to your third-party domain registrar with the account details used when you purchased your domain name. If you don't know who your domain registrar is, click the *WHOIS* link in the setup guide.
2. Replace every nameserver at your domain registrar with the nameservers listed under *Needs*. Click the *Copy* icon next to each nameserver to copy it. The icon will turn into a tick and show *Copied!*.
3. Save your changes at your domain registrar.
4. Click the *I've updated my nameservers* button in the nameservers table.

CloudCannon will check your nameservers every hour, and email you when your domain is live. There's no need to keep the page open. To check again immediately, click the *Check now* button below the nameservers table.

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  Most registrars hand your domain over to CloudCannon DNS within an hour, and almost all within 48 hours.
</comp.Notice>

If CloudCannon still can't see your new nameservers 48 hours after you clicked *I've updated my nameservers*, the setup guide lists what usually fixes it:

* Check that the change was saved at your registrar, not only entered.
* Replace all of the old nameservers. Adding CloudCannon's nameservers alongside them doesn't work.
* If DNSSEC is on at your registrar, turn it off first.

Once you have fixed the problem, click the *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button. CloudCannon will check every hour again.

If CloudCannon still can't see your nameservers after you have checked each of these, contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include your *Base Domain*, the name of your domain registrar, and the nameservers your registrar lists.

When your nameservers switch while you have the *Summary* tab open, the setup guide shows that your domain is live. Click the *Add a subdomain* button to add a subdomain, or the *Dismiss* button to close the guide. If you open the *Summary* tab after your domain goes live, CloudCannon shows the normal *Summary* tab.

### If your nameservers change

If the nameservers of a live domain stop pointing at CloudCannon, the setup guide shows when CloudCannon noticed the change. As DNS caches expire, the subdomains on your domain stop resolving.

* If you didn't mean to change your nameservers, change them back at your domain registrar, then click the *I've changed them back* button.
* If you moved your DNS on purpose, click the *switch to External DNS* link to open the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

Cloudflare deletes your domain's CloudCannon DNS zone if the nameservers stay changed for seven days. CloudCannon keeps a copy of your DNS records when it notices the change. If you change the nameservers back after Cloudflare deletes the zone, CloudCannon creates the zone again and restores your records. Cloudflare may give the new zone different nameservers, in which case the setup guide shows the nameservers to set at your registrar.

The *I've updated my nameservers*, *Keep checking for another 48 hours*, *I've changed them back*, and *Retry zone creation* buttons require permission to edit the DNS of the *Base Domain*. Without it, the buttons are disabled. Hover over a disabled button to see which permission you need. Ask someone in your *Organization* who has the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. If you can't view the DNS of the *Base Domain*, the *Summary* tab shows only a notice asking you to finish setting up DNS.

### How often CloudCannon checks your nameservers

CloudCannon checks your nameservers daily. It checks every hour until your domain is live, for up to 48 hours after the latest of these: you connected the domain, you set up CloudCannon DNS for it, or you clicked the *I've updated my nameservers* or *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button. If your domain still isn't live after 48 hours, CloudCannon emails you and goes back to checking daily. If you had clicked *I've updated my nameservers*, the setup guide then shows the *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button, which starts the hourly checks again.

The footer of the nameservers table, in the setup guide and on the *DNS* tab, says how often CloudCannon checks and when it last checked. Click the *Check now* button in the footer to check immediately. For a minute after a check, the button is disabled, and hovering over it shows *Checked less than a minute ago. You can check again in a minute.* If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the footer shows *Could not check* with how long ago the check failed, and the status stays at the last known result.

### View the nameserver check history

To see every recent check of your nameservers, click the *View nameserver check history* icon in the footer of the nameservers table. CloudCannon will open the *Nameserver checks for [Base Domain]* modal, which lists the nameservers your domain needs and the checks, most recent first. Back-to-back checks with the same result share a row.

Each row shows which DNS provider the nameservers CloudCannon found belong to (*CloudCannon DNS*, *Legacy CloudCannon DNS*, or *Another provider*), *Nothing found*, or *Couldn't check*, along with the nameservers found, any error, and when the checks ran. A *Couldn't check* row doesn't change the status of your nameservers. Click the *Load older results* button to see older checks.

You can also list the checks with the [CloudCannon API](/documentation/developer-reference/api/), at `GET /api/v0/base-domains/{base_domain_uuid}/dns/checks`.

#### [Base Domain] is live on CloudCannon DNS

## Add DNS records

[DNS records](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/#what-are-dns-records) are instructions for a domain name managed by an authoritative nameserver. By enabling CloudCannon DNS, you allow CloudCannon's servers to manage your DNS records.

To add a DNS record:

1. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *DNS* tab.
4. Click the *Add record* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add DNS record* modal.
5. In the *Add DNS record* modal, use the *Type* dropdown to select the type of record. Valid options include *A*, *AAAA*, *CNAME*, *MX*, *SRV*, *TXT*, and *NS*. CloudCannon will update the available fields depending on the record type.
6. Enter the details for your DNS record. 
7. (Optional.) Add a *Comment* to your DNS record to help your Organization Members understand the purpose of the record.
8. Click the *Add record* button.

CloudCannon will add the DNS record to your DNS settings and list it in the *DNS records* table.

The *Add record* button is disabled until the DNS zone for your domain is ready, or if you don't have the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Hover over the disabled button to see why.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-Add-Record-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Add DNS record modal on the DNS tab shows an MX record with fields for Type, Name, Mail server, Priority, and TTL, a Preview of the record, a Comment, and the Add record button." title="The Add DNS record modal" type="screenshot" />

The *DNS records* table lists the *Type*, *Name*, *Content*, and *TTL* of each record. Type in the *Filter* field to find a record. You can edit or delete existing DNS records by clicking the *Context Menu* button for a record and selecting the *Edit* or *Delete* option.

### CloudCannon CNAME records

When you enable CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon adds two CNAME records to your DNS records:

* `*.example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`
* `example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`

These records instruct servers completing a DNS lookup for your *Base Domain* (and all its subdomains) to search for the IP address of `sites.cloudcannon.com`. These records let visitors' browsers find the IP address of the CloudCannon server that hosts your *Site*.

If a certificate needs TXT records for validation, CloudCannon also adds those records for you, including at each renewal of the wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain*. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

## Fix the DNS record for a subdomain

The *Required records* section on the *DNS* tab lists the subdomains on your *Custom Domain* whose DNS record still needs a change. For each of these subdomains, the table shows:

* **Subdomain** — The subdomain. Click it to open the subdomain page.
* **Needs** — The record the subdomain needs to reach CloudCannon. If the wildcard record for your *Base Domain* serves the subdomain, this column shows *Served by the \*.example.com wildcard*.
* **Found** — The records CloudCannon found for the subdomain.
* **Status** — *Not pointing at CloudCannon* if a record points somewhere else, *No DNS record* if CloudCannon found nothing, or *Not checked yet*.

When every subdomain reaches CloudCannon, the section shows *[Subdomain] points at CloudCannon*, *Both subdomains point at CloudCannon*, or *All [Number] subdomains point at CloudCannon*, and says there is nothing to add. The *DNS* section of each subdomain page shows the record for that subdomain, whether or not it needs a change.

The footer of the table says when CloudCannon last checked the records. Click the *Check now* button in the footer to check every listed subdomain again.

The *Required records* section is hidden until your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS. Until then, the *DNS* section of each subdomain page shows *Waiting for nameservers*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-CloudCannon" alt="A screenshot of the DNS tab on the Domain page shows the CloudCannon DNS nameservers heading, the setup guide with example.com is live and the closed Nameservers row noting that the nameservers match, and the start of the Required records table with its Subdomain, Needs, Found, and Status columns." title="The Required records section on the DNS tab" type="screenshot" />

If a record in CloudCannon DNS stops a subdomain from reaching CloudCannon, CloudCannon can replace or remove that record for you. For example, a record for the subdomain can override the wildcard record for your *Base Domain*. The *Fix* button appears once your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS.

To fix the DNS record for a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the *DNS* tab of the *Domain* page, or to the *DNS* section of the subdomain page.
2. Click the *Fix* button in the row for the subdomain. CloudCannon will open the *Fix DNS for [Subdomain]?* modal, which lists the records it will remove and add.
3. Click the *Replace record*, *Remove record*, or *Add record* button to confirm the change. When more than one record changes, the button label is plural, such as *Replace records*.

CloudCannon will update the records in CloudCannon DNS. Visitors reach CloudCannon once the change spreads, usually within a few minutes.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-Fix-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Fix DNS for www.example.com modal lists removing an A record and adding a CNAME record that points at CloudCannon, with a Replace record button." title="The Fix DNS modal" type="screenshot" />

The *Fix* button requires the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Without it, the button is disabled, and hovering over it shows which permission you need.

CloudCannon checks the record of each subdomain daily. Click the *Check now* button below the *Required records* table, or in the *DNS* section of a subdomain page, to check again. For a minute after a check, the button is disabled. Checking a subdomain's record requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission.
