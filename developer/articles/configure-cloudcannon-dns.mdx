<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions, can view and change the DNS of a *Base Domain*.
</comp.Notice>

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> allows you to manage your DNS records within CloudCannon for each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> connected to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon adds the records that point your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> and its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomains</comp.GlossaryTerm> at CloudCannon, and the TXT records for your certificates, for you.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

## Enable CloudCannon DNS

*CloudCannon DNS* is the default DNS provider when you connect a *Custom Domain*. If your domain uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you can switch it to *CloudCannon DNS* on the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Updating your DNS provider can cause downtime for anything connected to your domain, such as your website or custom email address. Please make sure you have a copy of your existing DNS records before you change providers.

  If you need help updating your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, your current DNS provider, and a copy of your existing DNS records.
</comp.Notice>

To switch to CloudCannon DNS:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
4. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Domain Settings* tab. CloudCannon will open the *Details* section.
6. Under *DNS provider*, select the *CloudCannon DNS* option. CloudCannon will label the provider you are switching from *(current)* and list *What happens when you switch*.
7. Click the *Switch to CloudCannon DNS* button.

CloudCannon will create a DNS zone for your domain and add the records CloudCannon needs.

Nothing changes for visitors until you change the nameservers at your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Before you change them, add your other records, such as mail and site verification records, on the *DNS* tab.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Settings-Details-Switch-To-CloudCannon-DNS" alt="A screenshot of the Details section on the Domain Settings tab shows CloudCannon DNS selected, External DNS marked (current), the What happens when you switch steps, and the Switch to CloudCannon DNS button." title="The Details section on the Domain Settings tab" type="screenshot" />

You can also open the *Details* section by clicking the *Change DNS provider* link on the *DNS* tab.

## Move from the legacy CloudCannon DNS

Some older *Custom Domains* use the legacy CloudCannon DNS, where you can't add or change DNS records. Moving your domain to the new CloudCannon DNS creates a DNS zone with a copy of its current records, where you can add and edit them. Nothing changes for visitors until you update the nameservers at your domain registrar.

Until you move, the setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab and the *DNS* tab of your *Domain* page shows *Move [Base Domain] to the new CloudCannon DNS*.

Moving requires permission to edit both the settings and the DNS of the *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions). Without both, the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button is disabled, and the setup guide says which permission you need.

To move to the new CloudCannon DNS:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
4. Select the domain you want to move by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Move to the new CloudCannon DNS* button in the setup guide.

CloudCannon will create a DNS zone for your domain and copy in its current records. Check the copied records on the *DNS* tab, then [point your domain at CloudCannon DNS](#point-your-domain-at-cloudcannon-dns).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Overview-Setup-Guide-Legacy" alt="A screenshot of the setup guide on the Summary tab shows the Move example.org to the new CloudCannon DNS step, which explains that records on the legacy CloudCannon DNS cannot be changed, and the Move to the new CloudCannon DNS button." title="The setup guide on the legacy CloudCannon DNS" type="screenshot" />

You can also move from the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab, where *DNS provider* shows *Legacy CloudCannon DNS* as the current option. Select the *CloudCannon DNS* option and click the *Switch to CloudCannon DNS* button.

If you need a record changed urgently before you can move your domain, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include your *Base Domain* and the record you want added, changed, or removed.

## Point your domain at CloudCannon DNS

Once you enable CloudCannon DNS, you need to replace the nameservers at your domain registrar with CloudCannon's nameservers. Until you do, a setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page walks you through each step. The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same guide for any <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> on the domain, and notes that the *Site* stays on its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> until your domain goes live.

The setup guide shows a three-step tracker (*Update your registrar*, *Propagation*, and *[Base Domain] goes live*) and a heading for the step you are on:

* **Getting [Base Domain] ready** — CloudCannon is creating the DNS zone for your domain. Your nameservers show once the zone is ready, usually in a moment.
* **CloudCannon could not set up the DNS zone for [Base Domain]** — CloudCannon couldn't create the DNS zone. Click the *Retry zone creation* button to try again.
* **Point [Base Domain] at CloudCannon DNS** — Update the nameservers at your domain registrar.
* **[Base Domain] is on its way** — Your registrar is handing your domain over to CloudCannon DNS. Most handovers finish within an hour, and almost all within 48 hours.
* **[Base Domain] has not switched over yet** — CloudCannon still can't see your new nameservers 48 hours after you updated them.
* **[Base Domain]'s nameservers changed** — Your domain was live on CloudCannon DNS, but its nameservers no longer point at CloudCannon.

### Bring over your other records

Unless you moved from the legacy CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon doesn't copy records from your old DNS provider. Records at your old provider, such as mail and site verification records, stop working once you switch nameservers, so add them to CloudCannon DNS first. Click the *Open DNS records* button in the setup guide to open the *DNS* tab.

If CloudCannon DNS has no MX records for your domain, the setup guide warns that mail will stop arriving once you switch nameservers. If you send or receive email on your domain, click the *Add MX records* button to open the *Add DNS record* modal with the *MX* type selected.

### Update your nameservers

The *Nameservers* table in the setup guide lists the nameservers your domain *Needs*, the nameservers CloudCannon *Found*, and the *Status* of each one.

Configuring your nameservers requires you to use the services provided by your domain registrar. For support, please contact your domain registrar.

CloudCannon DNS doesn't support DNSSEC. If DNSSEC is turned on for your domain at your registrar, turn it off before you update your nameservers. Otherwise, resolvers that check DNSSEC reject the answers from CloudCannon DNS, and your domain stops resolving for many visitors.

To update your nameservers:

1. Log in to your third-party domain registrar with the account details used when you purchased your domain name. If you don't know who your domain registrar is, click the *WHOIS* link in the setup guide.
2. Replace every nameserver at your domain registrar with the nameservers listed under *Needs*. Click the *Copy* button next to each nameserver to copy it.
3. Save your changes at your domain registrar.
4. Click the *I've updated my nameservers* button in the setup guide.

CloudCannon will check your nameservers every hour, and email you when your domain is live. There's no need to keep the page open. Click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section of the *Summary* tab, or the refresh button in the header of the *Nameservers* table, to check again immediately.

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  Most registrars hand your domain over to CloudCannon DNS within an hour, and almost all within 48 hours.
</comp.Notice>

* Check that the change was saved at your registrar, not only entered.
* Replace all of the old nameservers. Adding CloudCannon's nameservers alongside them doesn't work.
* If DNSSEC is on at your registrar, turn it off first.

Once you have fixed the problem, click the *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button. CloudCannon will check every hour again.

Click the *Add a subdomain* button to add a *Subdomain*, or the *Dismiss* button to close the guide. If you open the *Summary* tab after your domain goes live, CloudCannon shows the normal *Summary* tab.

### If your nameservers change

If the nameservers of a live domain stop pointing at CloudCannon, the setup guide shows when CloudCannon noticed the change. As DNS caches expire, the *Subdomains* on your domain stop resolving.

* If you didn't mean to change your nameservers, change them back at your domain registrar, then click the *I've changed them back* button.
* If you moved your DNS on purpose, click the *switch to External DNS* link to open the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

Cloudflare deletes your domain's CloudCannon DNS zone if the nameservers stay changed for seven days. CloudCannon keeps a copy of your DNS records when it notices the change. If you change the nameservers back after Cloudflare deletes the zone, CloudCannon creates the zone again and restores your records. Cloudflare may give the new zone different nameservers, in which case the setup guide shows the nameservers to set at your registrar.

If they are disabled, ask someone in your *Organization* who has the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Without that permission, the setup guide also hides the *Add MX records* button.

### Check your nameservers on the DNS tab

The *DNS* tab shows the same setup guide at the top of the tab, with the *Nameservers* table and the same buttons. Unlike the *Summary* tab, the *DNS* tab keeps the guide once your domain is live: it shows *[Base Domain] is live*, and each nameserver in the table shows *Matches*. To check your nameservers again, click the refresh button in the header of the *Nameservers* table.

### How often CloudCannon checks your nameservers

CloudCannon checks your nameservers daily. It checks every hour until your domain is live, for up to 48 hours after the latest of these: you connected the domain, you set up CloudCannon DNS for it, or you clicked the *I've updated my nameservers* or *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button. If your domain still isn't live after 48 hours, CloudCannon emails you and goes back to checking daily. If you had clicked *I've updated my nameservers*, the setup guide then shows the *Keep checking for another 48 hours* button, which starts the hourly checks again.

You can click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section of the *Summary* tab, or the refresh button in the header of the *Nameservers* table, at most once a minute. If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, it shows *Could not check* with how long ago the check failed, and keeps the last known status.

### Nameserver emails

CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) about the nameservers of your domain.

#### [Base Domain] is live on CloudCannon DNS

CloudCannon sends this email about 15 minutes after your nameservers switch to CloudCannon, if an earlier check found nameservers that didn't match. It lists what is live on up to four *Subdomains*, then, if they apply, the *Subdomains* whose records need a change, the certificates still issuing, and a reminder that mail records are still missing.

CloudCannon sends this email when it still can't see your new nameservers more than 48 hours after you clicked *I've updated my nameservers*. If you didn't click it, CloudCannon counts the 48 hours from when you connected the domain or set up CloudCannon DNS for it, whichever is later, so switching a domain to CloudCannon DNS starts the 48 hours again. CloudCannon only sends the email within seven days of that starting point, and doesn't send it once your domain uses external DNS.

The email lists the nameservers your registrar still has and what usually fixes it, with a *Check the nameservers* button that opens the *DNS* tab of your *Domain* page. ## Add DNS records

[DNS records](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/#what-are-dns-records) are instructions for a domain name managed by an authoritative nameserver. By enabling CloudCannon DNS, you allow CloudCannon's servers to manage your DNS records.

To add a DNS record:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
4. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *DNS* tab.
6. Click the *Add record* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add DNS record* modal.
7. Use the *Type* dropdown to select the type of record. Valid options include *A*, *AAAA*, *CNAME*, *MX*, *SRV*, *TXT*, and *NS*. CloudCannon will update the available fields depending on the record type.
8. Enter the details for your DNS record.
9. (Optional.) Add a *Comment* to your DNS record to help your *Organization Members* understand the purpose of the record.
10. Click the *Add record* button.

CloudCannon will add the DNS record to your DNS settings.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-Add-Record-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Add DNS record modal on the DNS tab shows an MX record with fields for Type, Name, Mail server, Priority, and TTL, a Preview of the record, a Comment, and the Add record button." title="The Add DNS record modal" type="screenshot" />

The *DNS records* table lists the *Type*, *Name*, *Content*, and *TTL* of each record. Type in the *Filter* field to find a record. You can edit or delete existing DNS records by clicking the *Context Menu* button for a record and selecting the *Edit* or *Delete* option.

### CloudCannon CNAME records

When CloudCannon creates the DNS zone for your domain, it adds two CNAME records to your DNS records:

* `*.example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`
* `example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`

These records instruct servers completing a DNS lookup for your *Base Domain* (and its *Subdomains*) to search for the IP address of `sites.cloudcannon.com`. These records let visitors' browsers find the IP address of the CloudCannon server that hosts your *Site*. The wildcard record covers one level of *Subdomain* (e.g., `docs.example.com`, but not `api.docs.example.com`). A *Subdomain* the wildcard record doesn't serve needs its own record, which you can add with the *Fix* button.

If you moved from the legacy CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon copies your existing records into the new zone as well. If CloudCannon is restoring a zone that Cloudflare deleted, it restores the copy of your records instead of adding these two records.

If a certificate needs TXT records for validation, CloudCannon also adds those records for you, including at each renewal of the wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain*. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

## Fix the DNS record for a Subdomain

Fixing a record requires the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Without it, the *Fix* button is disabled, and the table says which permission you need.

The *Required records* table on the *DNS* tab lists every *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain*. For each *Subdomain*, the table shows:

* **Subdomain** — The *Subdomain*. Click it to open the *Subdomain* page.
* **Needs** — The record the *Subdomain* needs to reach CloudCannon. If the wildcard record for your *Base Domain* serves the *Subdomain*, this column shows *Served by the \*.example.com wildcard*.
* **Found** — The records CloudCannon found for the *Subdomain*.
* **Status** — *Connected* if the *Subdomain* reaches CloudCannon, *Not pointing at CloudCannon* if a record points somewhere else, *No DNS record* if CloudCannon found nothing, *Waiting for DNS* while a new record is still spreading, or *Not checked yet*.

Below the heading, the table shows how many *Subdomains* need a change, and when CloudCannon last checked them. Click the refresh button next to the heading to check every *Subdomain* again. The *DNS* section of each *Subdomain* page shows the same record for that *Subdomain*.

Until your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS, *Subdomains* that CloudCannon hasn't checked yet show *Waiting for nameservers*, and the table notes that the records answer once your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Network-Nameserver-Setup" alt="A screenshot of the DNS tab on the Domain page shows the setup guide with Point pending-dns.com at CloudCannon DNS, the Nameservers table with both nameservers marked Update needed and the I've updated my nameservers button, and the Required records table with one subdomain Waiting for nameservers." title="The DNS tab before the nameservers switch" type="screenshot" />

If a record in CloudCannon DNS stops a *Subdomain* from reaching CloudCannon, CloudCannon can replace or remove that record for you. For example, a record for the *Subdomain* can override the wildcard record for your *Base Domain*. The *Fix* button appears once your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS.

To fix the DNS record for a *Subdomain*:

1. Navigate to the *DNS* tab of the *Domain* page, or to the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
2. Click the *Fix* button in the row for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the *Fix DNS for [Subdomain]?* modal, which lists the records it will remove and add.
3. Click the *Replace record*, *Remove record*, or *Add record* button to confirm the change. When the change covers more than one record, the button reads *Replace records*, *Remove records*, or *Add records*.

CloudCannon will update the records in CloudCannon DNS. Visitors reach CloudCannon once the change spreads, usually within a few minutes.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-Fix-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Fix DNS for www.example.com modal lists removing an A record and adding a CNAME record that points at CloudCannon, with a Replace record button." title="The Fix DNS modal" type="screenshot" />

CloudCannon checks the record of each *Subdomain* daily. Click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section of a *Subdomain* page to check it again, at most once a minute. Checking a *Subdomain's* record requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission.
