<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions, can view and change the DNS Records of a *Base Domain*.
</comp.Notice>

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-records.yml">DNS Records</comp.GlossaryTerm> are instructions for a domain name, served by its authoritative nameservers. When a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you manage its DNS Records on the *DNS* tab of its *Domain* page.

This article covers *Custom Domains* on CloudCannon DNS. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">External DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you manage your DNS Records at your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-provider.yml">DNS Provider</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring External DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

Before we begin, this article assumes your *Custom Domain* uses CloudCannon DNS. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/).

## Add DNS Records

The *Add record* button is disabled until the DNS zone for your domain is ready, or if you don't have the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Hover over the disabled button to see why.

If you are a member of multiple <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organizations</comp.GlossaryTerm>, make sure you select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to first. For more information, please read our documentation on [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

To add a DNS Record:

1. Click the *Custom Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Select the domain for which you want to add a record by clicking the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *DNS* tab. CloudCannon will open the *DNS* tab, which lists your records in the *DNS records* section.
4. Click the *Add record* button in the top right of the *DNS records* section. CloudCannon will open the *Add DNS record* modal.
5. Use the *Type* dropdown to select the type of record. Valid options include *A*, *AAAA*, *CNAME*, *MX*, *SRV*, *TXT*, and *NS*. CloudCannon will update the available fields depending on the record type.
6. Enter the details for your DNS Record.
7. (Optional.) Enter a description of the record in the *Comment* field.
8. Click the *Add record* button.

CloudCannon will add the DNS Record to the DNS zone for your domain and list it in the *DNS records* section.

## Edit or delete DNS Records

The *DNS records* section on the *DNS* tab lists the *Type*, *Name*, *Content*, and *TTL* of each record. Type in the *Filter* field to find a record.

To edit or delete a DNS Record, click the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/context-menu.yml">Context Menu</comp.GlossaryTerm> button for the record and select the *Edit* or *Delete* option. Without the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission, the *Context Menu* only has the *View* option.

## Records CloudCannon adds

When CloudCannon creates the DNS zone for your domain, it adds two CNAME Records:

* `*.example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`
* `example.com` is an alias for `sites.cloudcannon.com`

These records instruct servers completing a DNS lookup for your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (and its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomains</comp.GlossaryTerm>) to search for the IP address of `sites.cloudcannon.com`. These records let visitors' browsers find the IP address of the CloudCannon server that hosts your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm>. The wildcard record covers one level of *Subdomain* (e.g., `docs.example.com`, but not `api.docs.example.com`). A *Subdomain* the wildcard record doesn't serve needs its own record, which you can add with the *Fix* button.

If you migrated from the legacy CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon copies your existing records into the new zone as well. If CloudCannon is restoring a zone that Cloudflare deleted, it restores the copy of your records instead of adding these two records.

If an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> needs TXT Records for validation, CloudCannon also adds those records for you, including at each renewal of the wildcard *SSL Certificate* for your *Base Domain*. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL Certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

## Fix the DNS Record for a Subdomain

Fixing a record requires the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission. Without it, the *Fix* button is disabled, and hovering over it shows *You do not have permission to edit the DNS for [Base Domain].*

The *Required records* section on the *DNS* tab lists the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain* whose DNS Record still needs a change. For each of these *Subdomains*, the table shows:

* **Subdomain** — The *Subdomain*. Click it to open the *Subdomain* page.
* **Record** — The record the *Subdomain* needs to reach CloudCannon, followed by the records CloudCannon found for it. If the wildcard record for your *Base Domain* serves the *Subdomain*, the needed record reads *Served by the \*.example.com wildcard*. The needed record is marked *Configured* once CloudCannon finds it, a found record that points somewhere else is marked *Points elsewhere*, and other records at the same name are marked *Existing*.
* **Status** — What CloudCannon found, such as *Not pointing at CloudCannon* or *No DNS Record*. For what each status means, please read our documentation on [how CloudCannon checks your DNS Records](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-cloudcannon-check-your-dns-records/#what-each-dns-record-status-means).

When every *Subdomain* reaches CloudCannon, the section shows *[Subdomain] points at CloudCannon*, *Both subdomains point at CloudCannon*, or *All [Number] subdomains point at CloudCannon*, and says there is nothing to add. The *DNS* section of each *Subdomain* page shows the record for that *Subdomain*, whether or not it needs a change.

The footer of the table says when CloudCannon last checked the records. Click the *Check now* button in the footer to check every listed *Subdomain* again.

The *Required records* section is hidden until your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/n/nameserver.yml">nameservers</comp.GlossaryTerm> point at CloudCannon DNS. Until then, the *DNS* section of each *Subdomain* page marks records CloudCannon hasn't checked yet as *Waiting for nameservers*, and has no *Check now* button.

If a record in CloudCannon DNS stops a *Subdomain* from reaching CloudCannon, CloudCannon can replace or remove that record for you. For example, a record for the *Subdomain* can override the wildcard record for your *Base Domain*. The *Fix* button appears once your nameservers point at CloudCannon DNS.

To fix the DNS Record for a *Subdomain*:

1. Navigate to the *DNS* tab of the *Domain* page, or to the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
2. Click the *Fix* button in the row for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the *Fix DNS for [Subdomain]?* modal, which lists the records it will remove and add.
3. Click the *Replace record*, *Remove record*, or *Add record* button to confirm the change. When the change covers more than one record, the button reads *Replace records*, *Remove records*, or *Add records*.

CloudCannon will update the records in CloudCannon DNS. Visitors reach CloudCannon once the change spreads, usually within a few minutes.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-Fix-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Fix DNS for www.example.com? modal lists removing an A Record and adding a CNAME Record that points at CloudCannon, with a Replace record button." title="The Fix DNS modal" type="screenshot" />

CloudCannon checks the record of each *Subdomain* daily. Click the *Check now* button below the *Required records* table, or in the *DNS* section of a *Subdomain* page, to check again. For a minute after a check, the button is disabled. Checking a *Subdomain's* record requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission. Without it, the button is disabled, and hovering over it shows *You do not have permission to edit the Domain Settings for [Base Domain].*
