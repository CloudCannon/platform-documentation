<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can add, change, and delete *Redirect Subdomains*.
</comp.Notice>

A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> sends every visitor to another domain. The redirect keeps the path and query string, so a visitor to `www.example.com/pricing?ref=ad` arrives at `example.com/pricing?ref=ad`. Redirects send visitors to the target over HTTPS when the target has an *SSL Certificate*.

You can redirect to another <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> or to any other domain, including a domain you have moved away from (e.g., an old brand name that now redirects to your new domain). If the destination is on a *Custom Domain* connected to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, it must already be a *Subdomain* there. For example, to redirect to `shop.example.com` when `example.com` is connected to your *Organization*, add `shop.example.com` as a *Subdomain* first. Destinations on domains that aren't connected to your *Organization* have no such requirement. *Redirect Subdomains* do not count toward the number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites* that your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> includes.

You can redirect to a *Subdomain* that is itself a redirect, everywhere you enter a destination. Visitors are then redirected twice, so CloudCannon shows the final destination below the *Redirect visitors to* field (e.g., *Avoid a double redirect by entering example.com instead.*), but still lets you save. CloudCannon refuses three kinds of destination, and shows why below the *Redirect visitors to* field. It refuses the *Subdomain* itself (*Cannot select this Subdomain as it would redirect to itself.*). It refuses a subdomain that isn't set up yet on one of the *Custom Domains* connected to your *Organization*, for example *Cannot select this Subdomain until you configure it on example.com.* And it refuses any change that would send visitors in a loop (*Cannot select this Subdomain as it would create a redirect loop.*). For how loops form and how to fix one, see [fixing a redirect loop](#fix-a-redirect-loop) below.

When you add the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., `example.com`) as a *Site Subdomain*, or change it from a redirect to serve *Sites*, CloudCannon also adds `www.example.com` as a redirect to it, unless `www.example.com` already exists. Adding `www.example.com` as a *Site Subdomain* adds `example.com` as a redirect in the same way. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/).

For more information on how *Subdomains* work, please read our documentation on [what a Subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).

Each *Subdomain* has its own page, which you open by clicking the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the *Subdomain* in the list on the *Subdomains* tab of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>. You can also find the *Change redirect target*, *Change type to Site*, *Change type to Redirect*, and *Delete Subdomain* options in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/context-menu.yml">Context Menu</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each *Subdomain* on the *Subdomains* tab.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

## Redirect status codes

Each *Redirect Subdomain* has its own status code, which you choose in the *Status code* dropdown when you add or change the redirect. New *Redirect Subdomains* use *301 permanent redirect*, including the redirect CloudCannon adds for `www.example.com` when you add `example.com`.

* **301 permanent redirect** — Search engines move to the target. Browsers can change a form POST to a GET.
* **302 temporary redirect** — Search engines keep the original address. Browsers can change a form POST to a GET.
* **307 temporary redirect, keeping the method** — Search engines keep the original address. Browsers repeat the same method, such as a form POST.
* **308 permanent redirect, keeping the method** — Search engines move to the target. Browsers repeat the same method, such as a form POST.

Search engines treat a permanent redirect (301 or 308) as a permanent move. If you might later serve a *Site* on the *Subdomain*, or swap which of `example.com` and `www.example.com` redirects to the other, choose a temporary redirect (302 or 307) until you are sure.

The status code of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">Fallback Redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a separate setting. For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the Fallback Redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/).

## Add a Redirect Subdomain

To add a *Redirect Subdomain*, please read our documentation on [adding a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/).

## Redirect a whole domain to another domain

To send every visitor on one domain to another domain (e.g., from an old brand name, `old-example.com`, to `example.com`), you don't need a second *Site*. Add *Redirect Subdomains* on the old domain instead. Before you begin, [connect the old domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/). If the new domain is also connected to your *Organization*, the destination you redirect to (e.g., `example.com`) must already be a *Subdomain* on it, or CloudCannon refuses the redirect with *Cannot select this Subdomain until you configure it on example.com.*

Each of the first two steps below adds a *Redirect Subdomain*. For the full steps, please read our documentation on [adding a Redirect Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/#add-a-redirect-subdomain).

To redirect a whole domain to another domain:

1. Add a *Redirect Subdomain* on the old domain with the *Subdomain* field left empty.
2. Enter the new domain (e.g., `example.com`) in the *Redirect visitors to* field.
3. Add a second *Redirect Subdomain* on the old domain for `www`, with the same destination.
4. Point the DNS for both *Subdomains* at CloudCannon. On external DNS, add the records listed on the *DNS* tab of the old domain's *Domain* page.

CloudCannon will redirect visitors to `old-example.com` and `www.old-example.com` to the same path on `example.com`. On CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon adds the DNS records for you. CloudCannon only adds `www` automatically when you add the *Base Domain* as a *Site Subdomain* or change it to serve *Sites*, not when you add it as a redirect, so add the `www` redirect yourself.

To redirect only some paths to another domain, or to send each old path to a different new path, use a `routing.json` file on the *Site* that serves the old domain instead. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring custom routing](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-custom-routing/).

## Change where a redirect goes

You can change the destination of a *Redirect Subdomain*, its status code, or both. Browsers that have already followed a redirect may keep using the old target for up to an hour after you change it.

To change where a *Redirect Subdomain* sends visitors:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Redirect Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Change Target* button in the *Redirect* section. CloudCannon will open the *Change [Subdomain]'s Target* modal.
3. Enter the new domain in the *Redirect visitors to* field.
4. (Optional.) Select a different status code from the *Status code* dropdown.
5. Click the *Save Redirect* button.

CloudCannon will redirect visitors to the new domain with the status code you selected.

The *Redirect* section of the *Subdomain* page shows the redirect as a tree: the *Subdomain* with a description of its status code (e.g., *301 permanent redirect, keeping the path and query string.*), then the destination and, if the destination is on the same *Custom Domain*, the *Sites* it serves or the domain to which it redirects.

## Change a Subdomain that serves Sites to a redirect

Changing a *Subdomain* that serves *Sites* to a redirect also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for each of those *Sites*.

When you change a *Subdomain* that serves *Sites* into a redirect:

* The *Sites* on the *Subdomain* lose their *Custom Domain*. Each *Site* keeps its [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/).
* Any redirects on the same *Custom Domain* that pointed at the *Subdomain* now point at the new target.

If you redirect a *Subdomain* to another *Subdomain* that already redirects back to it, the two *Subdomains* swap. For example, if `www.example.com` redirects to `example.com` and you redirect `example.com` to `www.example.com`, `www.example.com` takes the *Sites* from `example.com` at the same paths. If `example.com` was your *Fallback Redirect*, `www.example.com` also becomes the fallback.

To change a *Subdomain* that serves *Sites* to a redirect:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Change Subdomain Type* button next to *Change Subdomain type to Redirect* in the *Danger zone* section. CloudCannon will open the *Change [Subdomain] to a Redirect Subdomain* modal.
3. Enter the domain to which you want to send visitors in the *Redirect visitors to* field.
4. (Optional.) Select a status code from the *Status code* dropdown.
5. Click the *Change type to Redirect* button.

CloudCannon will redirect every visitor on the *Subdomain* to the domain you entered.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Change-To-Redirect-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Change example.com to a Redirect Subdomain modal shows www.example.com in the Redirect visitors to field, a note that CloudCannon will swap the two so www.example.com serves example.com's Sites and becomes the Fallback Redirect, 301 permanent redirect under Status code, and the Cancel and Change type to Redirect buttons." title="The Change to a Redirect Subdomain modal" type="screenshot"/>

You can also change your *Site's* *Subdomain* to a redirect from the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings*, using the *Change Subdomain Type* button next to *Change Subdomain type to Redirect* in the *Danger zone* section under *Subdomain settings*. Your *Site* then leaves the *Subdomain*, and CloudCannon opens the *[Site Name] is no longer on [Subdomain]* modal, with the *Stay here* and *Go to [Subdomain] settings* buttons.

## Change a redirect to serve a Site

Changing a *Redirect Subdomain* to serve a *Site* also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for that *Site*.

When you change a *Redirect Subdomain* to serve a *Site*, the *Subdomain* stops redirecting. Its hosting and certificate stay as they are.

Once the *Subdomain* serves a *Site*, it counts toward the number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites* that your *Subscription Plan* includes. If serving a *Site* takes your *Organization* over that number, CloudCannon will ask you to confirm the extra cost first. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Add-Ons and Overages are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-add-ons-and-overages/).

In a *Client Organization* on its extended six-month *Free Trial*, serving a *Site* on a *Subdomain* that had no *Sites* shortens the trial so that it ends five days later, whether or not the *Client Organization* is still within the limits of its *Subscription Plan*. CloudCannon asks you to confirm before it shortens the trial. For more information, please read our documentation on [how billing works in the Partner Program](/documentation/partner-articles/how-does-billing-work-in-the-partner-program/).

To change a *Redirect Subdomain* to serve a *Site*:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Redirect Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Change Subdomain Type* button next to *Change Subdomain type to Site* in the *Danger zone* section. CloudCannon will open the *Change [Subdomain] to a Site Subdomain* modal.
3. (Optional.) Select the *Site* you want to serve from the *Site* dropdown. Leave this field empty to set up the *Subdomain* with no *Site* yet.
4. (Optional.) Enter a path in the *Subpath* field to serve the *Site* under that path, so other *Sites* can share the *Subdomain*.
5. Click the *Change type to Site* button.

CloudCannon will stop redirecting the *Subdomain* and serve the *Site* you selected. If you did not select a *Site*, visitors will see a 404 page until you add one.

If the *Site* you select is the last *Site* on another *Subdomain*, the modal also asks what happens to that *Subdomain*. For more information, please read our documentation on [moving a Site from another Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/#move-a-site-from-another-subdomain).

## Delete a Redirect Subdomain

When you delete a *Redirect Subdomain*, CloudCannon stops redirecting it and removes its certificate. Visitors to the deleted *Subdomain* go to your *Fallback Redirect*.

To delete a *Redirect Subdomain*:

1. Navigate to the page for the *Redirect Subdomain*.
2. Click the *Delete Subdomain* button in the *Danger zone* section. CloudCannon will open the *Delete [Subdomain]?* modal.
3. (Optional.) If other *Subdomains* redirect to this one, uncheck the *Delete this other Redirect Subdomain* or *Delete these other Redirect Subdomains* checkbox to keep them.
4. Type the *Subdomain* in the *Type [Subdomain] to confirm* field.
5. Click the *Delete Subdomain* button.

CloudCannon will delete the *Redirect Subdomain*.

For more information on deleting *Subdomains* that serve *Sites*, please read our documentation on [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/#delete-a-subdomain).

## Fix a redirect loop

A redirect loop sends visitors from one *Subdomain* through a chain of redirects and back to where they started, so their browser never reaches a page. CloudCannon checks for loops whenever you add, change, or delete a *Subdomain*, and refuses any change that would create one.

To work out where visitors end up, CloudCannon follows each step of the chain:

* **Redirect Subdomain** — Visitors go to its destination.
* **Site Subdomain** — The chain ends, whether or not the *Site Subdomain* has a *Site* yet.
* **Unconfigured subdomain** — A subdomain you haven't configured on one of the *Custom Domains* connected to your *Organization*. Visitors go to the *Fallback Redirect* for that *Custom Domain*.
* **Any other domain** — The chain ends, because CloudCannon doesn't follow redirects on domains that aren't connected to your *Organization*.

A loop forms in one of two ways:

* **A redirect points back** — A *Redirect Subdomain* sends visitors to another *Redirect Subdomain* that leads back to it, directly or through other redirects. For example, `a.example.com` redirects to `b.example.com`, and `b.example.com` redirects to `a.example.com`.
* **The Fallback Redirect points back** — The chain reaches an unconfigured subdomain, which sends visitors to your *Fallback Redirect*, and the *Fallback Redirect* leads back. For example, your *Fallback Redirect* is `blog.example.com`, which redirects to `news.example.com`. If you delete `news.example.com` and keep `blog.example.com`, visitors to `news.example.com` would go to `blog.example.com`, which sends them back to `news.example.com`.

When a destination you enter in the *Redirect visitors to* field would create a loop, CloudCannon shows *Cannot select this Subdomain as it would create a redirect loop.* below the field. If you enter the *Subdomain* itself, CloudCannon shows *Cannot select this Subdomain as it would redirect to itself.* instead. The *Redirect visitors to* field appears in:

* The *Add a Subdomain* modal, when you select the *Redirect* type.
* The *Change [Subdomain]'s Target* modal.
* The *Change [Subdomain] to a Redirect Subdomain* modal. If the destination already redirects back to the *Subdomain*, this modal doesn't show the error, because CloudCannon swaps the two *Subdomains* instead. For more information, see [changing a Subdomain that serves Sites to a redirect](#change-a-subdomain-that-serves-sites-to-a-redirect) above.
* The *Redirect visitors* option, when you remove or move the last *Site* on a *Subdomain*.

When you change your *Fallback Redirect* to a *Subdomain* that would lead back to itself, the *Change Fallback* modal and the *Use as Fallback Redirect* option show *You cannot select [Subdomain] as your Fallback Redirect, because website visitors would go around in a loop ([Subdomain] → [Subdomain] → [Subdomain]). Select a different Subdomain.* The message lists the *Subdomains* in the loop.

To fix a loop, find the redirect in the chain that points back. On the *Subdomains* tab of the *Domain* page, each *Redirect Subdomain* shows its destination (e.g., *Redirects visitors to news.example.com.*). Start from the destination you entered, and follow each redirect until you reach the *Subdomain* you were changing. Then do one of the following before you try your change again:

* [Change where the redirect goes](#change-where-a-redirect-goes).
* [Change the redirect to serve a Site](#change-a-redirect-to-serve-a-site).
* [Delete the redirect](#delete-a-redirect-subdomain).
* For a loop through your *Fallback Redirect*, [make a different Subdomain your Fallback Redirect](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/), or, when you delete a *Subdomain*, leave the *Delete this other Redirect Subdomain* or *Delete these other Redirect Subdomains* checkbox checked so the redirects that point at it are deleted too.
