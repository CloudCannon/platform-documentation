External hosting is when a service other than CloudCannon serves your website to visitors, while your team edits the content in CloudCannon. Hosting your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon is optional.

CloudCannon saves every content change to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so any hosting provider that builds from your *Git Repository* or a branch of it can publish those changes. External hosting may be right for you if your website needs server-side rendering, your organization already runs its own hosting, or you want features your hosting provider offers.

For more information on hosting through CloudCannon instead, please read our documentation on [what web hosting is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-web-hosting/).

## Ways to host your Site externally

You can host your *Site* externally in two ways. The difference is which service builds your *Site*.

### Your hosting provider builds your Site

You connect your hosting provider (e.g., Netlify or Vercel) to the same *Git Repository* and branch as your CloudCannon *Site*. When your team saves changes in CloudCannon, CloudCannon commits them to the branch, and your hosting provider builds and publishes the new version.

CloudCannon can still build your *Site* for its own use, such as the *Visual Editor* and your *Testing Domain*. If you don't need those, you can turn off building and hosting on CloudCannon with *Headless Mode*. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Headless Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-headless-mode/) and [Netlify hosting with CloudCannon editing](/documentation/developer-articles/netlify-hosting-with-cloudcannon-editing/).

### CloudCannon builds your Site and deploys the output

<comp.Notice info_type="pricing">
  ***Build Deploys* are available on our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/pricing/">Standard, Team, or Enterprise Plan</a>.**
</comp.Notice>

CloudCannon builds your *Site*, then a *Build Deploy* commits the built files to a branch of a *Git Repository* after each successful build. Your hosting provider serves the files from that branch without building them again. This suits hosting providers that serve static files, and keeps one build for both editing and hosting. For more information, please read our documentation on [outputting a built Site from CloudCannon to an external provider](/documentation/developer-articles/output-a-built-site-from-cloudcannon-to-an-external-provider/).

## What changes when you host externally

CloudCannon's hosting features only apply to the *Sites* CloudCannon hosts. When you host externally, configure these features with your hosting provider instead:

* Serving your *Site* on a [Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/), and its SSL certificates.
* Redirects and headers in a `routing.json` file. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring custom routing](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-custom-routing/).
* Password, user account, and SAML authentication for visitors. For more information, please read our documentation on [what authentication is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-authentication/).

You can still manage your domain's DNS records with [CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/) and point them at your hosting provider. A *Site* that CloudCannon doesn't serve on a *Custom Domain* doesn't count toward the number of *Custom Domains* in your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
