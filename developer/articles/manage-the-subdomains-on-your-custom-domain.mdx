<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can add, configure, and delete subdomains. Adding, swapping, or removing a *Site* on a subdomain, or changing the path at which it is served, also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for that *Site*. This applies to the subdomain page and the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* alike. Deleting a subdomain that serves *Sites*, or changing it to a redirect, also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for each of those *Sites*.
</comp.Notice>

Every address you use on a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a subdomain in CloudCannon, including the *Base Domain* itself (e.g., example.com). Each subdomain has its own page, where you can see and change what it serves, how visitors reach it, its HTTPS certificate, and its DNS.

A subdomain either serves *Sites* or redirects visitors to another domain. This article covers subdomains that serve *Sites*. For subdomains that redirect, please read our documentation on [managing redirect subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-redirect-subdomains/). For more information on how subdomains work, please read our documentation on [what a Custom Domain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/).

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

## View the subdomains on your Custom Domain

The *Subdomains* tab of the *Domain* page lists every subdomain on your *Custom Domain*. For each subdomain, the list shows:

* **Subdomain** — The address of the subdomain, with the *Sites* it serves, the domain it redirects to, or *No site yet. Visitors get the fallback* below it. If the subdomain is your fallback and has no *Site*, it shows *No site yet. Visitors get a not found page* instead. CloudCannon labels the subdomain that is your fallback redirect with *Fallback*.
* **Hosting** — How visitors reach the subdomain: *CloudCannon Hosting*, *Cloudflare Proxy*, or *Single Origin*.
* **HTTPS** — The status of the subdomain's certificate, such as *Active*, *Issuing*, *Renewing*, *Expiring*, *Expired*, or *Failed*. Hover over a status to see more detail, and open the subdomain page for the full explanation.
* **DNS** — Whether the subdomain's record points at CloudCannon: *Connected*, *Not pointing at CloudCannon*, *No DNS record*, *Not checked yet*, or *Waiting for DNS* while CloudCannon looks for the record of a subdomain that, in the last 48 hours, you added, changed the hosting of, or marked as updated, or whose domain changed DNS provider.

To view the subdomains on your *Custom Domain*:

1. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Identify the domain you want to view and click the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Subdomains* tab.
4. (Optional.) Use the sort dropdown or the *Add Filter* button to change which subdomains the list shows, and in what order.
5. Click a subdomain in the list to open its page.

CloudCannon will open the subdomain page, which shows the *Sites* on the subdomain and its *Hosting*, *HTTPS*, *DNS*, and *Danger zone* sections.

Each subdomain in the list also has a *Context Menu*, with options to open the subdomain page, configure its hosting or HTTPS, check its DNS now or view its check history, use it as the fallback, change it to a redirect or to serve a *Site*, change the target of a redirect, and delete it.

The *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page counts your subdomains and names the subdomains that need attention: subdomains that don't reach CloudCannon, subdomains with no *Site* yet, and subdomains with certificate problems. Click the *View them* button to open the *Subdomains* tab with only those subdomains listed. To list every subdomain again, remove the *Subdomain needs attention* filter. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

### Sort and filter the subdomains

Click the sort dropdown to the left of the search field, which shows the current sort (e.g., *Name*), to sort the list by:

* **Name** — Subdomains with fewer levels first (e.g., example.com before www.example.com), then alphabetically. This is the default.
* **Name (Z-A)** — The reverse of *Name*.
* **Needs attention first** — Subdomains that don't reach CloudCannon, have no *Site* yet, or have a certificate problem, first.
* **Recently added** — The newest subdomains first.

Click the *Add Filter* button to show only the subdomains that match one or more values:

* **Attention** — *Needs attention* or *Doesn't need attention*.
* **Type** — *Site* or *Redirect*.
* **Hosting** — *CloudCannon Hosting*, *Cloudflare Proxy*, or *Single Origin*.
* **HTTPS** — *Active*, *Issuing*, *Renewing*, *Expired*, *Failed*, or *Missing*.
* **DNS** — *Connected*, *Not pointing at CloudCannon*, *No DNS record*, or *Not checked yet*.

You can also type in the search field to find a subdomain by name.

## Add a subdomain

You can add a subdomain with or without a *Site*. A subdomain with no *Site* yet keeps its hosting and certificate ready, and CloudCannon sends its visitors to your [fallback redirect](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/) until you add a *Site*.

When you add the *Base Domain* (e.g., example.com) as a subdomain that serves *Sites*, with or without a *Site* yet, CloudCannon also adds www.example.com as a redirect to it, if www.example.com does not already exist. When you add www.example.com to serve *Sites*, CloudCannon adds example.com as a redirect to www.example.com in the same way.

To add a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the *Subdomains* tab of the *Domain* page for your *Custom Domain*.
2. Click the *Add subdomain* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add subdomain* modal.
3. Enter the subdomain name in the *Subdomain* field (e.g., docs). Leave this field empty to add the *Base Domain* itself.
4. Select the *Site* option from the *Type* dropdown.
5. (Optional.) Choose the *Site* you want the subdomain to serve from the *Site to serve* dropdown.
6. (Optional.) Enter a path in the *Path* field to serve the *Site* under that path (e.g., `/docs/`). Leave this field empty to serve the *Site* at the root of the subdomain.
7. Click the *Add subdomain* button.

CloudCannon will add the subdomain to the list on the *Subdomains* tab. The setup guide on the *Summary* tab lists any records the new subdomain needs at your DNS provider. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#follow-the-setup-guide).

If the *Site* you select already has a *Custom Domain*, the modal shows where the *Site* moves from and to. For more information, please read the section on [moving a Site from another subdomain](#move-a-site-from-another-subdomain).

You can also click the *Add subdomain* button on the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.

If you select a *Site* and the new subdomain takes your *Organization* over the number of *Custom Domains* in its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>, CloudCannon will ask you to confirm the extra cost before adding it. Click the *Accept Billing Increase* button to continue. Subdomains with no *Site* yet and redirect subdomains do not count toward this number, so CloudCannon asks when you add the first *Site* to them instead. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Add-Ons and Overages are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-add-ons-and-overages/).

In a *Client Organization* on its extended six-month *Free Trial*, serving a *Site* on a subdomain that had no *Sites* shortens the trial so that it ends five days later, whether or not the *Client Organization* is still within the limits of its *Subscription Plan*. CloudCannon asks you to confirm before it shortens the trial. For more information, please read our documentation on [how billing works in the Partner Program](/documentation/partner-articles/how-does-billing-work-in-the-partner-program/).

## Add a Site to a subdomain

A subdomain can serve several *Sites*, each at its own path. For example, example.com can serve your marketing *Site* at the root (`/`) and your documentation *Site* at `/docs/`. Every *Site* on a subdomain shares the subdomain's hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings.

If the *Site* you add already has a *Custom Domain*, CloudCannon moves the *Site* to the new address. For more information, please read the section on [moving a Site from another subdomain](#move-a-site-from-another-subdomain).

You can also add a *Custom Domain* from the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings*. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/).

To add a *Site* to a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Add site* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add a site to [Subdomain]* modal.
3. Select the *Site* you want to add from the *Site to serve* dropdown.
4. (Optional.) Enter a path in the *Path* field. Leave this field blank to serve the *Site* at the root of the subdomain.
5. Click the *Add site* button.

CloudCannon will serve the *Site* on the subdomain at the path you entered.

## Swap the Site at a path

You can replace the *Site* at a path with another *Site* in one step, so the path is never left without a *Site*. The *Site* you replace keeps its [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/).

The *Swap for another site* option is disabled unless you can both change the hosting of the *Site* and edit the settings of the *Base Domain*. Hover over the option to see which permission you're missing, then ask someone in your *Organization* who has it.

To swap the *Site* at a path:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Context Menu* for the *Site* you want to replace and select the *Swap for another site* option. CloudCannon will open the *Swap the site at [Subdomain and path]* modal.
3. Select the *Site* that takes over from the *New site* dropdown.
4. Click the *Swap site* button.

CloudCannon will serve the new *Site* at that path. The *New site* dropdown only lists *Sites* whose hosting you can change.

## Change the path of a Site

You can change the path at which a subdomain serves a *Site*. Each path on a subdomain is served by one *Site*, so the new path can't be a path another *Site* already serves. Before you save, CloudCannon shows the paths on the subdomain after the change. If links to the old path would show a not found page or another *Site*, CloudCannon warns you so you can update those links or keep the old path.

The *Change path* option is disabled unless you can both change the hosting of the *Site* and edit the settings of the *Base Domain*. Hover over the option to see which permission you're missing, then ask someone in your *Organization* who has it.

To change the path of a *Site*:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Context Menu* for the *Site* you want to move and select the *Change path* option. CloudCannon will open the *Change [Site Name]'s path* modal.
3. Enter the new path in the *Path* field (e.g., `/guides/`).
4. Review the paths on the subdomain after the change.
5. Click the *Change path* button.

CloudCannon will serve the *Site* at the new path.

Your *Site's* build output needs a folder that matches the new path. For more information, please read our documentation on [building your Site for a path](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/#build-your-site-for-a-path).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Change-Path-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Change path modal for the Documentation Site shows the new path /guides/, a preview of the paths on example.com after the change, and a warning that links to the old path will be served by the Marketing site instead." title="The Change path modal" type="screenshot"/>

You can also change the path of a *Site* from the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings*. For more information, please read our documentation on [reviewing your Site's Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/#review-your-site-s-custom-domain).

## Remove a Site from a subdomain

When you remove a *Site* from a subdomain, CloudCannon stops serving that *Site* on the subdomain. The *Site* keeps its *Testing Domain*.

The *Remove from [Subdomain]* option is disabled unless you can both change the hosting of the *Site* and edit the settings of the *Base Domain*. Hover over the option to see which permission you're missing, then ask someone in your *Organization* who has it.

If you remove the last *Site* on a subdomain, CloudCannon asks what happens to the subdomain:

* **Leave it set up** — The subdomain stays set up with no *Site*. Its hosting and certificate stay connected, so you can add a *Site* back without waiting for DNS or a new certificate. Until then, CloudCannon sends visitors to your fallback redirect, or shows a not found page if the subdomain is the fallback. This is the default. Choose this option to take your website down temporarily.
* **Redirect** — The subdomain becomes a redirect that sends every visitor to another domain, keeping the path and query string.
* **Delete it** — CloudCannon deletes the subdomain and its certificate.

If other subdomains redirect to the subdomain you are emptying, CloudCannon lists them and deletes them too, unless you uncheck the *Delete this redirect too* or *Delete these redirects too* checkbox. The checkbox appears when you leave the subdomain set up or delete it, and only covers redirects on domains whose settings you can edit. CloudCannon notes the destination of visitors to the redirects that stay.

If you leave the subdomain set up, CloudCannon sends its visitors to your fallback redirect. If your fallback sends visitors back to this subdomain, directly or through other redirects, CloudCannon refuses to leave it set up, because visitors would go round in a loop. This can happen when your fallback is one of the redirects that point at the subdomain, and you uncheck the checkbox to keep it. The modal says what to do instead, for example *Visitors would go round in a loop: blog.example.com -> news.example.com -> blog.example.com. Delete the redirect on news.example.com, or redirect or delete blog.example.com instead.*

If you redirect the subdomain, enter its new destination in the *Send visitors to* field. If the destination itself redirects, CloudCannon warns that visitors are redirected twice, and suggests sending them to the final destination instead. CloudCannon refuses a destination that would send visitors in a loop, a destination that is the subdomain itself (*[Subdomain] cannot redirect to itself.*), and a destination on one of your *Custom Domains* that isn't a subdomain yet (*[Destination] is not set up yet. Add it as a subdomain first.*).

To remove a *Site* from a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Context Menu* for the *Site* you want to remove and select the *Remove from [Subdomain]* option. CloudCannon will open the *Remove [Subdomain] from [Site Name]?* modal.
3. If this is the last *Site* on the subdomain, select the *Leave it set up*, *Redirect*, or *Delete it* option. If you select *Redirect*, enter the domain to which you want to send visitors in the *Send visitors to* field.
4. (Optional.) Uncheck the *Delete this redirect too* or *Delete these redirects too* checkbox to keep the subdomains that redirect to this one.
5. Click the *Remove from [Subdomain]* button. If this is the last *Site* on the subdomain, click the *Remove domain*, *Remove and redirect*, or *Remove and delete* button instead.

CloudCannon will stop serving the *Site* on the subdomain.

## Move a Site from another subdomain

If the *Site* you add to a subdomain already has a *Custom Domain*, CloudCannon moves the *Site* from its old subdomain to the new one. This applies when you add a subdomain with a *Site*, add a *Site* to a subdomain, swap the *Site* at a path, or change a redirect subdomain to serve a *Site*. The modal shows where the *Site* is now and where it moves to.

If the *Site* is the last *Site* on its old subdomain, the modal also asks what happens to the old subdomain, with the same choices as when you [remove a Site from a subdomain](#remove-a-site-from-a-subdomain):

* **Leave it set up** — The old subdomain stays set up with no *Site*. This is the default.
* **Redirect** — The old subdomain becomes a redirect. Enter its destination in the *Send visitors to* field. Other redirects that pointed at the old subdomain redirect to the new destination instead.
* **Delete it** — CloudCannon deletes the old subdomain and its certificate.

If you leave the old subdomain set up or delete it, CloudCannon also deletes the subdomains that redirect to it, unless you uncheck the *Delete this redirect too* or *Delete these redirects too* checkbox.

Choosing what happens to the old subdomain requires permission to edit the settings of its *Base Domain*. Without that permission, the modal shows what happens to the old subdomain, and CloudCannon leaves it set up.

## Configure hosting, HTTPS, and DNS for a subdomain

Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS belong to the subdomain, so any change applies to every *Site* on it. Each section of the subdomain page has its own buttons next to its heading:

* **Hosting** — Click the *Configure* button to choose how visitors reach the subdomain in the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal. *CloudCannon Hosting* is the default. The modal shows the DNS record the selected option needs, and warns you if the record has to change. If you proxy the subdomain through your own Cloudflare zone or another provider, please read our documentation on [choosing how visitors reach a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#choose-how-visitors-reach-a-subdomain).
* **HTTPS** — Click the *Configure* button to choose between an automatic certificate and an uploaded certificate, and whether to redirect HTTP visitors to HTTPS, in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. The section explains the state of the certificate, and what to do if it needs attention. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/), [adding a custom SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-ssl-certificate/), and [resolving SSL certificate errors](/documentation/developer-articles/resolve-ssl-certificate-errors/).
* **DNS** — Review whether the subdomain points at CloudCannon, and the record it needs. Click the *Check now* button to check again, or the *History* button to see earlier checks.

### The hosting diagram

The *Hosting* section shows how visitors reach the subdomain as a diagram. The diagram depends on the hosting option:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — *Visitors*, the *CloudCannon CDN*, and the *CloudCannon origin*.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — *Visitors*, *Your Cloudflare*, and the *CloudCannon origin*.
* **Single Origin** — *Visitors* and the *CloudCannon origin*. Your *Base Domain* on external DNS uses this diagram too, because its A records point straight at the CloudCannon origin.

Each connection in the diagram is labeled with its state, such as *Reaching CloudCannon*, *Not reaching CloudCannon*, *Waiting for DNS to update*, *Not checked yet*, or *Connected securely*. A green line means the connection works, and a red line with an exclamation mark shows where visitors stop reaching CloudCannon. Click a connection to see what CloudCannon found, including the DNS records, the result of its test request, and when it last checked, along with what to change if the connection isn't working. The *View the DNS record* button scrolls to the *DNS* section, the *Check DNS now* button runs a new check, and the *View check history* button opens the history of checks. Press Escape or click the close button to close the details.

## Check the DNS for a subdomain

The *DNS* section of the subdomain page says whether the subdomain points at CloudCannon, and shows the record the subdomain *Needs*, the records CloudCannon *Found*, the *Status* of the record, and when CloudCannon last checked it. The status is one of:

* **Connected** — The subdomain reaches CloudCannon.
* **Not pointing at CloudCannon** — A record for the subdomain points somewhere else.
* **No DNS record** — CloudCannon found no record for the subdomain.
* **Not checked yet** — CloudCannon hasn't checked the subdomain yet.
* **Waiting for DNS** — You added the subdomain or marked its record as updated in the last 48 hours, and CloudCannon is still looking for the record. The 48 hours also start again when you change the subdomain's hosting or your domain's DNS provider. After 48 hours, the status shows what CloudCannon found. CloudCannon checks daily, and hourly for up to seven days after you mark the record as updated.
* **Waiting for nameservers** — On CloudCannon DNS, the nameservers of your domain don't point at CloudCannon DNS yet. The record answers once they do.

If the subdomain isn't connected, the sentence above the record says what to do:

* **CloudCannon DNS** — Click the *Fix* button, and CloudCannon replaces or removes the records that stop the subdomain from reaching CloudCannon. For more information, please read our documentation on [fixing the DNS record for a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/#fix-the-dns-record-for-a-subdomain).
* **External DNS** — Add the record at your DNS provider, using the *Copy* button next to its value, then click the *I've updated it* button. CloudCannon checks hourly for up to seven days, and emails you when the subdomain is live. If CloudCannon still can't find the record 48 hours after you marked it as updated, the section lists what usually fixes it. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding the records](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#add-the-records).

When the subdomain has TXT records to add for its certificate, the *DNS* section points you to them under *HTTPS*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-External-Records" alt="A screenshot of the DNS section for www.example.com shows that its record points somewhere else, the CNAME record it needs with a Copy button, the A record CloudCannon found, the Not pointing at CloudCannon status, and the I've updated it button." title="The DNS section on a subdomain page" type="screenshot"/>

To check the DNS for a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section.
3. (Optional.) Open the *DNS checks for [Subdomain]* modal with the *History* button.

CloudCannon will check the subdomain's DNS and update the *DNS* section.

The *DNS checks for [Subdomain]* modal lists earlier checks, most recent first. Back-to-back checks with the same result share a row. Each row shows the result of the check, the DNS answer CloudCannon received, whether a test request reached CloudCannon, and when the check ran. A check that couldn't complete is marked *Couldn't check*, and doesn't change the subdomain's status. Click the *Load older results* button to see more. CloudCannon removes results older than 90 days, except the latest complete check.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-DNS-Checks-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the DNS checks for blog.example.com modal shows the record the subdomain needs, a list of checks with their result, DNS answer, test request outcome, and time, including a Connected check and a check that couldn't complete, and the Load older results button." title="The DNS checks modal" type="screenshot"/>

You can check the DNS for each subdomain at most once a minute. CloudCannon also checks every subdomain daily, and, on external DNS, every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button. If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the section says when the check failed, and keeps the last known status.

## Delete a subdomain

When you delete a subdomain, CloudCannon stops serving it and removes its certificate. Any *Sites* on the subdomain keep their *Testing Domains*. If the subdomain was your fallback redirect, another subdomain becomes the fallback. You can choose which one under *Fallback* on the *Summary* tab. Visitors to the deleted subdomain go to your fallback redirect.

To delete a subdomain:

1. Navigate to the page for the subdomain.
2. Click the *Delete subdomain* button in the *Danger zone* section. CloudCannon will open the *Delete [Subdomain]?* modal.
3. (Optional.) Uncheck the *Delete this redirect too* or *Delete these redirects too* checkbox to keep the subdomains that redirect to this one.
4. Type the subdomain in the *Type [Subdomain] to confirm* field.
5. Click the *Delete subdomain* button.

CloudCannon will delete the subdomain.
