A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a domain you own and connect to your CloudCannon <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so visitors reach your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> at your own address instead of its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For example, CloudCannon's domain is <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>. The domain is the part of your URL that visitors are most likely to remember. You can purchase a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-name.yml">domain name</comp.GlossaryTerm> from a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

* **Domain Name** — The unique address used to access a website (e.g., <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>). This is made of a name of your choice (normally your brand name) and a top-level domain (TLD) from the <a href="https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/tlds-2012-02-25-en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">available list</a> (e.g., .com, .org, .site, .io).
* **Domain Registrar** — A third-party company that sells and renews domain names. The registrar records who owns each domain, and which nameservers answer DNS questions about it. You can purchase a domain name from a registrar such as <a href="https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cloudflare Registrar</a> or <a href="https://www.namecheap.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Namecheap</a>.

## Do you need a Custom Domain?

A *Custom Domain* can help with brand recognition and search engine optimization.

You don't need to purchase a *Custom Domain* to create and edit a *Site* on CloudCannon, as CloudCannon automatically provides a free [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) to all *Sites*. However, using your *Testing Domain* as your primary URL has two drawbacks.

One, you cannot change the automatically generated *Testing Domain* name without creating a new *Site* on CloudCannon. The automatically generated domain may not be right for you or your *Site*. You can generate a new one by creating a new *Site* with the same files; however, recreating a *Site* takes time.

Two, CloudCannon asks search engines not to index *Testing Domains*, so they don't appear in search results.

## How Custom Domains work

To use a *Custom Domain* on CloudCannon you can [connect a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), then [add a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/). When CloudCannon [builds your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/), it serves the new output on your *Custom Domain*.

You manage each *Custom Domain* from its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which you open from the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

A *Custom Domain* belongs to one *Organization*, along with every <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on it. You can't connect a domain that another *Organization* has already connected, or split its *Subdomains* between *Organizations*. When you transfer a *Site* to another *Organization*, its *Custom Domain* and every other *Site* on it move too. For more information, please read our documentation on [resolving ownership over a Custom Domain](/documentation/user-articles/resolve-ownership-over-a-custom-domain/) and [transferring a Site between Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/transfer-a-site-between-organizations/).

Your *Site* must be in *Hosted Mode* to serve it on a *Custom Domain*. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Site Building Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-site-building-mode/).

When you connect a *Custom Domain*, you choose its DNS provider. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, CloudCannon manages the DNS records for each *Subdomain*. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you add the records CloudCannon lists at another DNS provider, such as your domain registrar or Cloudflare. For more information, please read our documentation on [what DNS is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/).

Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> sets how many *Custom Domains* your *Organization* includes. The *Lite Plan* includes 1, the *Standard Plan* 5, and the *Team Plan* 10, and you can add more with <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/add-on.yml">Add-Ons</comp.GlossaryTerm>. The *Enterprise Plan* doesn't set a limit. CloudCannon counts each *Subdomain* that serves *Sites*. *Subdomains* with no *Site* yet and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomains</comp.GlossaryTerm> are free. If serving a *Site* on a *Subdomain* takes your *Organization* over the number of *Custom Domains* in its *Subscription Plan*, CloudCannon asks you to confirm the extra cost first. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Add-Ons and Overages are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-add-ons-and-overages/).

In a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> on its extended six-month <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/free-trial.yml">Free Trial</comp.GlossaryTerm>, serving a *Site* on a *Subdomain* that had no *Sites* shortens the trial so that it ends five days later, whether or not the *Client Organization* is still within the limits of its *Subscription Plan*. CloudCannon asks you to confirm before it shortens the trial. For more information, please read our documentation on [how billing works in the Partner Program](/documentation/partner-articles/how-does-billing-work-in-the-partner-program/).

## Subdomains

Every address you host on a *Custom Domain* is a *Subdomain* in CloudCannon, including the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> itself (e.g., example.com). Each *Subdomain* either serves one or more *Sites* or redirects visitors to another domain, and has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings. CloudCannon sends visitors to subdomains you haven't set up to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">fallback redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which is one of the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*. On external DNS, visitors only reach a subdomain you haven't set up if you add a wildcard record (e.g., `*.example.com`) at your DNS provider. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).

## The default robots.txt file

A `robots.txt` file tells search engines which pages of your website not to crawl. If your *Site* doesn't have its own `robots.txt` file, CloudCannon serves this default file at `/robots.txt` on your *Custom Domain*:

<comp.CodeBlock language="plaintext" source="robots.txt">
```

User-agent: *
Disallow: /cc-connection-test
Disallow: /update
Disallow: /login
Disallow: /logout

```
</comp.CodeBlock>

CloudCannon uses these paths on *Sites* it hosts:

* **`/cc-connection-test`** — CloudCannon answers this path itself to check that your domain reaches CloudCannon.
* **`/update`** — The default path of the Guest Login Page, which sends visitors to CloudCannon to edit the *Site*. You can change this path. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring the Guest Login Page](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-the-guest-login-page/).
* **`/login`** and **`/logout`** — CloudCannon uses these paths to sign visitors in and out of a *Site* that requires authentication.

CloudCannon doesn't serve a page of your own at `/cc-connection-test`, or at the path of your Guest Login Page. While CloudCannon serves the default `robots.txt` file, search engines also skip any page of your own at `/login`, `/logout`, or `/update`. The default file always lists `/update`, even if you change the path of the Guest Login Page.

If your *Site* has its own `robots.txt` file in its build output, CloudCannon serves your file instead of the default. Add the lines above to your file to keep search engines away from the reserved paths.

Your [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) always serves a different `robots.txt` file, which asks search engines not to crawl any page.
