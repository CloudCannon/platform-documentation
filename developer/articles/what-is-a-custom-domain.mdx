A *Custom Domain* is a unique address used to identify a website. For example, CloudCannon's domain is <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>. The domain is the part of your URL that visitors are most likely to remember. You can purchase a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-name.yml">domain name</comp.GlossaryTerm> from a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

* **Domain Name** — The unique address used to access a website (e.g., <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>). This is made of a name of your choice (normally your brand name) and a TLD from the <a href="https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/tlds-2012-02-25-en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">available list</a> (e.g., .com, .org, .site, .io).
* **Domain Registrar** — A third-party company that sells and renews domain names. The registrar records who owns each domain, and which nameservers answer DNS questions about it. Most CloudCannon users purchase a domain name from a registrar (e.g., <a href="https://www.cloudflare.com/en-gb/products/registrar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cloudflare Registrar</a>, <a href="https://www.namecheap.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Namecheap</a>).

<comp.DocsImage path="assets/diagrams/CloudCannon-Documentation-URL-Structure-Diagram.png" alt="A diagram of a URL shows labels for protocol, subdomain, domain, top-level domain, subdirectory, slug, and path." title="The parts of a URL" type="ui-snippet" />

To use a *Custom Domain* on CloudCannon you can [connect a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), then [add a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/). Every time you [save changes](/documentation/user-articles/save-your-changes/) to your *Site's* content, CloudCannon [builds your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/) and serves the new content on your *Custom Domain*.

Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> sets how many *Custom Domains* your *Organization* includes. The *Lite Plan* includes 1, the *Standard Plan* 5, and the *Team Plan* 10, and you can add more with *Add-Ons*. The *Enterprise Plan* doesn't set a limit. CloudCannon counts each subdomain that serves *Sites*. Subdomains with no *Site* yet and redirect subdomains are free. If your *Organization* uses more *Custom Domains* than it has available, CloudCannon serves an unavailable page to visitors instead of your content. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Add-Ons and Overages are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-add-ons-and-overages/).

In a *Client Organization* on its extended six-month *Free Trial*, serving a *Site* on a subdomain that had no *Sites* shortens the trial so that it ends five days later, whether or not the *Client Organization* is still within the limits of its *Subscription Plan*. CloudCannon asks you to confirm before it shortens the trial. For more information, please read our documentation on [how billing works in the Partner Program](/documentation/partner-articles/how-does-billing-work-in-the-partner-program/).

## Do you need a Custom Domain?

A *Custom Domain* can help with brand recognition and search engine optimization.

You don't need to purchase a *Custom Domain* to create and edit a *Site* on CloudCannon, as CloudCannon automatically provides a free [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) to all *Sites*. However, using your *Testing Domain* as your primary URL has two drawbacks.

One, you cannot change the automatically generated *Testing Domain* name without creating a new *Site* on CloudCannon. The automatically generated domain may not be right for you or your *Site*. You can generate a new one by creating a new *Site* with the same files; however, recreating a *Site* takes time.

Two, by default, CloudCannon automatically de-indexes *Testing Domains* so that they do not appear in search engines.

## How subdomains work

Every address you use on a *Custom Domain* is a subdomain in CloudCannon, including the *Base Domain* itself (e.g., example.com). The *Subdomains* tab of your *Domain* page lists every subdomain on your *Custom Domain*, along with what it serves and the status of its hosting, HTTPS, and DNS. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

* **Base Domain** — The domain you purchased from a domain registrar, including its top-level domain (e.g., example.com). Also called the root or apex domain.
* **Subdomain** — A name in front of your *Base Domain* (e.g., www.example.com or docs.example.com). In CloudCannon, every address you use is a subdomain, including the *Base Domain* itself.
* **Path** — The part of the URL after the domain (e.g., /docs/ in example.com/docs/). Also called a subpath or subdirectory. Several *Sites* can share a subdomain, each at its own path.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domains-Network-Tab" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomains tab on the Domain page shows the list of subdomains with Subdomain, Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS columns, and the Fallback section." title="The Subdomains tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

Each subdomain has its own page and does one of two things:

* **Serves Sites**: The subdomain serves one or more *Sites*, each at its own path. For example, example.com can serve your marketing *Site* at the root (`/`) and your documentation *Site* at `/docs/`. A subdomain can also serve only *Sites* at paths, in which case visitors to any other path on the subdomain see a not found page.
* **Redirects**: The subdomain sends every visitor to another domain, keeping the path and query string. Each redirect subdomain has its own status code, which is a permanent (301) redirect unless you choose another. Redirects use HTTPS, unless the target is a subdomain on CloudCannon that does not have a certificate yet.

A subdomain that serves *Sites* can also exist with no *Site* yet. Its hosting and certificate stay set up, and CloudCannon sends its visitors to your fallback redirect until you add a *Site*.

Separating your content onto different CloudCannon *Sites* can improve clarity for your *Organization Members* and visitors, and lets you use separate *Git Repositories* for distinct areas of your website. For example, you could serve your marketing *Site* on example.com and your documentation *Site* on docs.example.com, or serve both on example.com with the documentation at example.com/docs/. Whether you use subdomains or paths is a matter of personal preference.

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  Having your website content on separate CloudCannon *Sites* means you can specify which content different teams are allowed to edit using [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/).
</comp.Notice>

For more information, please read our documentation on [managing the subdomains on your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-subdomains-on-your-custom-domain/) and [managing redirect subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-redirect-subdomains/).

### Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS

Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS belong to the subdomain, not to each *Site*, so they apply to every *Site* on the subdomain. You can choose one of three hosting options for each subdomain:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — Visitors reach CloudCannon through Cloudflare's global network, which caches your *Site* close to them. This is the default and recommended option.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — Your own Cloudflare account proxies the subdomain to CloudCannon.
* **Single Origin** — DNS points straight at CloudCannon's origin servers, or your own proxy sits in front of them.

For more information on the route visitors take with each hosting option, please read our documentation on [how hosting works on CloudCannon](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-hosting-work-on-cloudcannon/#how-visitors-reach-cloudcannon). For the records each option needs, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

Each subdomain gets its own certificate. The domain's wildcard certificate covers subdomains you haven't set up yet, so visitors to them reach your fallback redirect over HTTPS. CloudCannon can issue and renew each certificate automatically, or you can upload your own. For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/).

### www and the Base Domain

When you add the *Base Domain* (e.g., example.com) as a subdomain that serves *Sites*, with or without a *Site* yet, CloudCannon also adds www.example.com as a redirect to it, if www.example.com does not already exist. When you add www.example.com to serve *Sites*, CloudCannon adds example.com as a redirect to www.example.com in the same way.

### The fallback redirect

CloudCannon sends visitors to your fallback redirect when they visit a subdomain on your *Custom Domain* that is not set up (e.g., a misspelled subdomain), or a subdomain with no *Site* yet. Visitors to the fallback redirect itself see a not found page if it has no *Site*. Your fallback redirect can be any subdomain on your *Custom Domain*, including a redirect subdomain. The fallback uses a temporary (307) redirect unless you choose another status code.

For example, suppose you have one *Site* on example.com and another *Site* on docs.example.com, and example.com is your fallback redirect. CloudCannon sends anyone trying to access an unconfigured subdomain (e.g., app.example.com) to example.com.

For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the fallback redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/).

### The default robots.txt file

A `robots.txt` file tells search engines which pages of your website not to crawl. If your *Site* doesn't have its own `robots.txt` file, CloudCannon serves this default file at `/robots.txt` on your *Custom Domain*:

<comp.CodeBlock language="plaintext" source="robots.txt">
```

User-agent: *
Disallow: /cc-connection-test
Disallow: /update
Disallow: /login
Disallow: /logout

```
</comp.CodeBlock>

CloudCannon uses these paths on *Sites* it hosts:

* **`/cc-connection-test`**: CloudCannon answers this path itself to check that your domain reaches CloudCannon.
* **`/update`**: The default path of the Guest Login Page, which sends visitors to CloudCannon to edit the *Site*. You can change this path. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring the Guest Login Page](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-the-guest-login-page/).
* **`/login`** and **`/logout`**: CloudCannon uses these paths to sign visitors in and out of a *Site* that requires authentication.

CloudCannon doesn't serve a page of your own at `/cc-connection-test`, or at the path of your Guest Login Page. While CloudCannon serves the default `robots.txt` file, search engines also skip any page of your own at `/login`, `/logout`, or `/update`. The default file always lists `/update`, even if you change the path of the Guest Login Page.

If your *Site* has its own `robots.txt` file in its build output, CloudCannon serves your file instead of the default. Add the lines above to your file to keep search engines away from the reserved paths.

Your [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) always serves a different `robots.txt` file, which asks search engines not to crawl any page.

### Who can change a subdomain

Changing a subdomain's hosting, HTTPS, or redirect requires permission to edit the settings of the *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission). Adding, swapping, or removing a *Site* on a subdomain, or changing the path at which it is served, requires that permission and the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for the *Site*, from the subdomain page and the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* alike. Deleting a subdomain that serves *Sites*, or changing it to a redirect, also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for each of those *Sites*.
