A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a domain you own and connect to your CloudCannon <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so visitors reach your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> at your own address instead of its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For example, CloudCannon's domain is <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>. The domain is the part of your URL that visitors are most likely to remember. You can purchase a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-name.yml">domain name</comp.GlossaryTerm> from a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

* **Domain Name** — The unique address used to access a website (e.g., <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>). This is made of a name of your choice (normally your brand name) and a top-level domain (TLD) from the <a href="https://www.icann.org/resources/pages/tlds-2012-02-25-en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">available list</a> (e.g., .com, .org, .site, .io).
* **Domain Registrar** — A third-party company that sells and renews domain names. The registrar records who owns each domain, and which nameservers answer DNS questions about it. You can purchase a domain name from a registrar such as <a href="https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cloudflare Registrar</a> or <a href="https://www.namecheap.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Namecheap</a>.

## Do you need a Custom Domain?

A *Custom Domain* can help with brand recognition and search engine optimization.

You don't need to purchase a *Custom Domain* to create and edit a *Site* on CloudCannon, as CloudCannon provides a free [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) to every *Site* in <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosted-mode.yml">Hosted Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>. However, using your *Testing Domain* as your primary URL has two drawbacks.

First, CloudCannon sets the *Testing Domain* name, a `cloudvent.net` address, when you create the *Site*, and you can't change it afterwards. To use a different name, you need to create a new *Site* with the same files.

Second, CloudCannon asks search engines not to index *Testing Domains*, so they don't appear in search results.

## How Custom Domains work

To serve a *Site* on a *Custom Domain*, you [connect the domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), then [add the Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/). Your *Site* must be in *Hosted Mode*, the default [Site Building Mode](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-site-building-mode/). Each time CloudCannon [builds your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/), it serves the new output on your *Custom Domain*.

When you connect a *Custom Domain*, you choose its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-provider.yml">DNS Provider</comp.GlossaryTerm>. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, CloudCannon manages the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-records.yml">DNS Records</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you add the records CloudCannon lists at another *DNS Provider*, such as your domain registrar or Cloudflare. For more information, please read our documentation on [what DNS is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/).

You manage each *Custom Domain* from its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which you open from the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

## Subdomains

Every address you host on a *Custom Domain* is a *Subdomain* in CloudCannon, including the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> itself (e.g., example.com). Each *Subdomain* either serves one or more *Sites* or redirects visitors to another domain, and has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings. CloudCannon sends visitors to subdomains you haven't configured to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">Fallback Redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which is one of the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*, as long as your DNS sends them to CloudCannon. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).

## Who owns a Custom Domain

A *Custom Domain* belongs to one *Organization*, along with every *Subdomain* on it. You can't connect a domain that another *Organization* has already connected, or split its *Subdomains* between *Organizations*. When you transfer a *Site* to another *Organization*, its *Custom Domain* and every other *Site* on it move too. For more information, please read our documentation on [resolving ownership over a Custom Domain](/documentation/user-articles/resolve-ownership-over-a-custom-domain/) and [transferring a Site between Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/transfer-a-site-between-organizations/).

## How Custom Domains are billed

Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> includes a set number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites*. The <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/l/lite-plan.yml">Lite Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> includes 1, the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/standard-plan.yml">Standard Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> 5, and the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/team-plan.yml">Team Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> 10, and you can add more with <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/add-on.yml">Add-Ons</comp.GlossaryTerm>. The <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/enterprise-plan.yml">Enterprise Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> doesn't set a limit. The *Plan resources* section of the *Your Subscription Plan* page lists these *Subdomains* as *Custom Domains*, with how many your *Subscription Plan* includes and how many you're using. *Subdomains* with no *Site* yet and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomains</comp.GlossaryTerm> are free. If serving a *Site* on a *Subdomain* takes your *Organization* over that number, CloudCannon asks you to confirm the extra cost first. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Add-Ons and Overages are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-add-ons-and-overages/).

## Reserved paths and the default robots.txt file

A `robots.txt` file tells search engines which pages of your website not to crawl. If your *Site* doesn't have its own `robots.txt` file, CloudCannon serves this default file at `/robots.txt` on your *Custom Domain*:

<comp.CodeBlock language="plaintext" source="robots.txt">
```
User-agent: *
Disallow: /cc-connection-test
Disallow: /update
Disallow: /login
Disallow: /logout
```
</comp.CodeBlock>

CloudCannon reserves these paths on *Sites* it hosts because some tasks only work at an address on your own domain. Checking that your DNS sends visitors to CloudCannon needs a request to your domain, and signing visitors in to your *Site* needs to start from your *Site's* address. CloudCannon uses each path for one of these tasks:

* **`/cc-connection-test`** — CloudCannon answers this path itself to check that your domain reaches CloudCannon.
* **`/update`** — The default path of the Guest Login Page, which sends visitors to CloudCannon to edit the *Site*. You can change this path. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring the Guest Login Page](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-the-guest-login-page/).
* **`/login`** and **`/logout`** — CloudCannon uses these paths to sign visitors in and out of a *Site* that requires authentication.

CloudCannon doesn't serve a page of your own at `/cc-connection-test`, or at the path of your Guest Login Page. While CloudCannon serves the default `robots.txt` file, search engines also skip any page of your own at `/login`, `/logout`, or `/update`. The default file always lists `/update`, even if you change the path of the Guest Login Page.

If your *Site* has its own `robots.txt` file in its build output, CloudCannon serves your file instead of the default. Add the lines above to your file to keep search engines away from the reserved paths.

Your [Testing Domain](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) always serves a different `robots.txt` file, which asks search engines not to crawl any page.
