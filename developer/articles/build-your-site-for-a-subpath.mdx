A *Site* served at a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subpath.yml">subpath</comp.GlossaryTerm> of a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., `example.com/docs/`) needs its build output to match that subpath. This article covers building your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> for a subpath. A *Site* served at the root of a *Subdomain* (`/`) doesn't need any of these steps.

To serve a *Site* at a subpath, please read our documentation on [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) or [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/).

## How CloudCannon serves a Site at a subpath

CloudCannon doesn't remove the subpath from a request before it looks for the file, so it serves `example.com/docs/about/` from `docs/about/index.html` in your build output. Your *Site's* build output needs a folder that matches the subpath, and your *Site's* links also need to include the subpath, so that a link to `/about/` becomes `/docs/about/`.

Many SSGs have a setting that adds a subpath to your links, but not all of them also build your files into a matching folder. After you add a subpath, check that your build output has a folder with that name and that every page of your *Site* is inside it.

If your Jekyll *Site* sets `baseurl` in its `_config.yml` file, Jekyll adds the subpath to your links but still builds your pages at the top of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/output-path.yml">Output Path</comp.GlossaryTerm>, and CloudCannon doesn't read your `_config.yml` file. Check that your build output has a folder that matches the subpath, as described above.

## Use Reseed to build for a subpath

<a href="https://github.com/CloudCannon/reseed" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reseed</a> is an open-source tool that copies your build output into a folder named after the subpath and rewrites the links in your pages to include it. You can run Reseed after your build in a postbuild script:

<comp.CodeBlock language="shell" source="/.cloudcannon/postbuild">
```
#!/bin/bash

npx reseed -s _site -d _reseed -b docs
rm -rf _site
mv _reseed _site
```
</comp.CodeBlock>

Replace `_site` with the *Output Path* of your *Site*, and `docs` with your subpath. CloudCannon uploads the files in your *Output Path* once the postbuild script finishes, so the script must leave the rewritten files there. For more information, please read our documentation on [extending your build process with hooks](/documentation/developer-articles/extending-your-build-process-with-hooks/) and [configuring your command line options](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-your-command-line-options/).

CloudCannon can also run Reseed as a build step for you.

To run Reseed as a build step:

1. Contact our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a> with the name of your *Site* and the subpath.
2. Enter the subpath in the *Baseurl* field on the *Reseed* page under *Site Settings*.
3. Click the *Update Reseed Details* button.

CloudCannon will rebuild your *Site* with the new subpath.
