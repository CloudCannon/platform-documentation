A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a name in front of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>, for example the `www` in `www.example.com` or `docs` in `docs.example.com`. In CloudCannon, every address you host on a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a *Subdomain*, including the *Base Domain* itself (i.e., `example.com` is an empty *Subdomain*).

<comp.DocsImage path="assets/diagrams/CloudCannon-Documentation-URL-Structure-Diagram.png" alt="A diagram of a URL shows labels for protocol, subdomain, domain, top-level domain, subdirectory, slug, and path." title="The parts of a URL" type="ui-snippet" />

After you [connect a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), you can [add a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) and choose whether each one serves one or more <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or redirects visitors to another domain. Each *Subdomain* has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings, which apply to every *Site* it serves. You can also configure a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">fallback redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm> for visitors to subdomains you haven't set up (e.g., `typo.example.com`) and to *Site Subdomains* with no *Site* yet.

CloudCannon lists all the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain* on the *Subdomains* tab of its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domains-Network-Tab" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomains tab on the Domain page shows the list of Subdomains with Subdomain, Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS columns, and the Fallback section." title="The Subdomains tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

This article covers the *Subdomains* on a *Custom Domain*. For the *Custom Domain* itself, including who owns it and its DNS provider, please read our documentation on [what a Custom Domain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/).

## Why use a Subdomain?

*Subdomains* let you give each area of your website its own address on one *Custom Domain*. You might use a *Subdomain* to:

* Serve a separate *Site* for each area. Each *Site* can use its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or several *Sites* can share one *Git Repository*, each with its own build settings and configured to show different content.
* Give an area its own hosting option or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm>, such as putting one *Subdomain* behind your own Cloudflare account.
* Send an old or alternative address, such as `www.example.com`, to your main *Site* with a redirect.

You can also serve several *Sites* on one *Subdomain*, each at its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subpath.yml">subpath</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., `/docs/` in `example.com/docs/`). To choose between them:

* **Subdomain** — Has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, it needs its own DNS record. Each *Subdomain* that serves *Sites* counts toward the number of *Custom Domains* in your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
* **Subpath** — Shares the hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings of its *Subdomain*, and doesn't count as another *Custom Domain*.

For more information, please read our documentation on [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/) and [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/).

## Subdomain types

When you add a *Subdomain* to your *Custom Domain*, you must select its *Type*. The *Type* decides what the *Subdomain* does:

* **Site** — The *Subdomain* serves one or more *Sites*, each at its own subpath.
* **Redirect** — The *Subdomain* sends every visitor to another domain.

You can switch your *Subdomain* *Type* at any time. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) and [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/).

### Site Subdomains

A *Site Subdomain* serves built files to website visitors from one or more CloudCannon *Sites*. CloudCannon <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/build.yml">builds</comp.GlossaryTerm> your *Site* each time it <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/syncing.yml">syncs</comp.GlossaryTerm> a change from your *Git Repository*, including the changes you save in CloudCannon, and when you start a build yourself, such as with the *Rebuild* button. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Site build is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/).

To build and serve *Site* files on a *Subdomain*, that *Site* must be in *Hosted Mode*. This is the default setting for *Sites* in CloudCannon, but you can configure the *Site Building Mode* on the *Details* page under *Site Settings*. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Site Building Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-site-building-mode/) and [enabling Headless Mode](/documentation/developer-articles/enable-headless-mode/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Site-Settings-Details" alt="A screenshot of the Details page under Site Settings shows the Site Name field and the Site Building Mode options, with Hosted Mode selected and Headless Mode below it." title="The Details page under Site Settings" type="screenshot"/>

You can serve several *Sites* on one *Subdomain*, each at its own subpath. For example, `example.com` can serve a marketing *Site* at `/` and a documentation *Site* at `/docs/`:

* A visit to `example.com/docs/setup/` goes to the documentation *Site*, because CloudCannon serves the *Site* with the longest subpath that matches the request.
* A visit to `example.com/about/` goes to the marketing *Site*, the only *Site* whose subpath matches.
* If `example.com` had no *Site* at `/`, a visit to a path that doesn't match any *Site*, such as `example.com/about/`, would show a not found page.

A *Site* at a subpath needs to build its files into a folder that matches the subpath (e.g., a `docs` folder for `/docs/`). For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) and [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Site" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomain page for example.com shows the Sites section with the Add site button and a Card for each of two Sites, Marketing site at / and Documentation at /docs/, each with its Git Repository and branch, build and sync status, and Context Menu, and the Hosting section with a diagram of Visitors reaching the CloudCannon Origin." title="The Subdomain page" type="screenshot"/>

A *Subdomain* belongs to your *Custom Domain*, not to any *Site*, so removing a *Site* doesn't remove its *Subdomain*. When you remove the last *Site* from a *Subdomain*, CloudCannon asks whether to leave the *Subdomain* set up with no *Site*, turn it into a redirect, or delete it. A *Subdomain* with no *Site* keeps its hosting and certificate, and CloudCannon sends its visitors to your fallback redirect until you add a *Site*. For more information, please read our documentation on [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/) and [removing a Custom Domain from your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/remove-a-custom-domain-from-your-site/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Remove-Last-Site-Leave-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Remove blog.example.com from Engineering blog? modal shows the Leave it set up, Redirect, and Delete it options, with Leave it set up selected and a Delete this redirect too checkbox for news.example.com, which redirects here." title="The Remove modal" type="screenshot"/>

### Redirect Subdomains

A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> sends every visitor to another domain, keeping the path and query string. For example, if `www.example.com` redirects to `example.com`, a visit to `www.example.com/about/` goes to `example.com/about/`. The destination can be a *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain* or any other domain. Each *Redirect Subdomain* also has its own status code, which is *301 Permanent* unless you choose another.

Redirects send visitors to the destination over HTTPS when the destination has an SSL certificate. A *Redirect Subdomain* still needs DNS records that point at CloudCannon, and its own SSL certificate, but it doesn't count toward the number of *Custom Domains* in your *Subscription Plan*. CloudCannon won't save a redirect that points at its own *Subdomain* or sends visitors around in a loop.

You change the destination and status code with the *Change target* button on the *Subdomain* page. For more information, please read our documentation on [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Redirect" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomain page for www.example.com shows the Redirect section with the Change target button and a tree from www.example.com, described as a 301 permanent redirect, to its destination, example.com, and the Sites it serves, above the Hosting and HTTPS sections." title="The Subdomain page for a Redirect Subdomain" type="screenshot"/>

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  When you add a plain *Base Domain* (e.g., `example.com`) as a *Site Subdomain*, CloudCannon also adds `www.example.com` as a redirect to it, unless `www.example.com` already exists. Adding `www.example.com` as a *Site Subdomain* adds `example.com` as a redirect in the same way. This happens whether or not you choose a *Site* yet, whether you add the *Subdomain* from the *Domain* page or from *Site Settings*, and when you change a *Redirect Subdomain* to serve *Sites*. CloudCannon doesn't add the pair when you add a *Redirect Subdomain*.
</comp.Notice>

## The fallback redirect

Subdomains you haven't set up (e.g., a misspelled subdomain), and *Site Subdomains* with no *Site* yet, send visitors to your fallback redirect. On external DNS, visitors only reach a subdomain that isn't set up if you add a wildcard DNS record at your DNS provider. Your fallback redirect can be any *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain*, including a *Redirect Subdomain*. If your fallback redirect is a *Site Subdomain* with no *Site* yet, its visitors see a not found page until you add one.

For example, suppose you have one *Site* on `example.com` and another *Site* on `docs.example.com`, and `example.com` is your fallback redirect. CloudCannon sends anyone trying to access an unconfigured subdomain (e.g., `app.example.com`) to `example.com`.

The fallback redirect uses a *307 Temporary, keeps the method* status code by default, because a temporary redirect stops search engines from treating an unconfigured subdomain as moved. You can choose any of the status codes that *Redirect Subdomains* use, which are described in our documentation on [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/). You change the fallback redirect and its status code with the *Change fallback* button in the *Fallback* section of the *Subdomains* tab.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domains-Network-Tab-Fallback-Element" alt="A screenshot of the Fallback section on the Subdomains tab shows the Change fallback button and a 307 temporary redirect that sends visitors from any unconfigured subdomain of example.com to example.com." title="The Fallback section" type="screenshot"/>

For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the fallback redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/). For the wildcard DNS record, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS

Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS belong to the *Subdomain*, not to each *Site*, so they apply to every *Site* on the *Subdomain*. Each has its own section on the *Subdomain* page. The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same sections under the *Subdomain settings* heading, for the *Subdomain* that serves that *Site*. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring a Subdomain's hosting, HTTPS, and DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-a-subdomains-hosting-https-and-dns/).

### Hosting

You can choose one of three hosting options for each *Subdomain*: <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>. To choose between them:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — CloudCannon serves your *Site* from its global network, so pages load quickly everywhere. This is the default and recommended option, and suits most *Subdomains*. If your *Custom Domain* uses external DNS, your *Base Domain* (e.g., `example.com`) can't use a CNAME record, so it uses two A records instead. Visitors to your *Base Domain* reach CloudCannon's origin servers directly rather than through the global network, and your *Site* works the same way.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — Your own Cloudflare account proxies the *Subdomain* to CloudCannon. Choose this if your DNS is on Cloudflare and you want to keep Cloudflare in front of your *Site*. *Cloudflare Proxy* isn't available while your *Custom Domain* uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Cloudflare may serve cached pages, so your *Site* won't show updates until you clear your Cloudflare cache.
* **Single Origin** — Visitors reach CloudCannon's origin servers directly, without going through CloudCannon's global network. Choose this if you run your own proxy or firewall in front of CloudCannon. Your *Base Domain* uses two A records, and other *Subdomains* use a CNAME record. CloudCannon can only validate the certificate once DNS points at CloudCannon.

The hosting option decides which DNS records the *Subdomain* needs, so changing it can mean updating your records. For more information, please read our documentation on [how hosting works on CloudCannon](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-hosting-work-on-cloudcannon/) and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

### HTTPS

Each *Subdomain* gets its own SSL certificate, which covers every *Site* on that *Subdomain*. Subdomains you haven't set up yet are covered by the domain's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/w/wildcard-certificate.yml">wildcard certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> instead, which covers one level of subdomain (e.g., `typo.example.com`, but not `a.typo.example.com`). On external DNS, the wildcard certificate needs records at your DNS provider to be issued and renewed, and without them HTTPS stops working for subdomains you haven't set up.

Each *Subdomain* also has its own minimum TLS version, which can't be lower than the minimum for your *Custom Domain*. The minimum you set on your *Base Domain's* own *Subdomain* also applies to subdomains you haven't set up. For more information, please read our documentation on [increasing the minimum TLS version for your domains](/documentation/developer-articles/increase-the-minimum-tls-version-for-your-domains/). For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/), [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/), [renewing the wildcard certificate on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-certificate-on-external-dns), and [adding a custom SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-ssl-certificate/).

### DNS

Each *Subdomain* needs a DNS record that points at CloudCannon. On *CloudCannon DNS*, the wildcard record CloudCannon adds for your *Base Domain* covers every *Subdomain* one level below it (e.g., `docs.example.com`) on *CloudCannon Hosting*, unless you add a record for a *Subdomain* that points somewhere else. A *Subdomain* on *Single Origin*, or one nested more than one level deep (e.g., `api.docs.example.com`), needs its own record. On external DNS, you add a record for each *Subdomain* at your DNS provider. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/) and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Who can change a Subdomain

Changing a *Subdomain's* hosting, HTTPS, or redirect, or your fallback redirect, requires permission to edit the settings of the *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission). Adding, swapping, or removing a *Site* on a *Subdomain*, or changing the subpath at which it is served, requires that permission and the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for the *Site*, from the *Subdomain* page and the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* alike. Deleting a *Subdomain* that serves *Sites*, or changing it to a redirect, also requires the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for each of those *Sites*.

Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), and of the *Partner* group in a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, hold both permissions. On the *Team Plan* or *Enterprise Plan*, you can also grant them to a [Custom Permission Group](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/). If you don't have permission, the buttons and *Context Menu* options for these changes are disabled on the *Subdomain* page, and on the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings*, a sentence that explains which permission you need replaces the *Subdomain settings*. Hover over a disabled option in a *Site's* *Context Menu* to see which permission you're missing. To get access, ask a member of the *Owners* group in your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
