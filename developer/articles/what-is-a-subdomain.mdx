A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a name in front of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>, for example the `www` in `www.example.com` or `docs` in `docs.example.com`. In CloudCannon, every address you host on a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a *Subdomain*, including the *Base Domain* itself (i.e., `example.com` is an empty *Subdomain*).

<comp.DocsImage path="assets/diagrams/CloudCannon-Documentation-URL-Structure-Diagram.png" alt="A diagram of a URL shows labels for protocol, subdomain, domain, top-level domain, subdirectory, slug, and path." title="The parts of a URL" type="ui-snippet" />

After you [connect a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), you can [add a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) and choose whether each one serves one or more <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or redirects visitors to another domain. Each *Subdomain* has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings, which apply to every *Site* it serves. You can also configure a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">Fallback Redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm> for visitors to subdomains you haven't set up (e.g., `typo.example.com`).

CloudCannon lists all the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain* on the *Subdomains* tab of its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Domain page is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Subdomains-Tab" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomains tab on the Domain page shows the list of Subdomains with Subdomain, Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS columns, and the Fallback Redirect section." title="The Subdomains tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

This article covers the *Subdomains* on a *Custom Domain*. For the *Custom Domain* itself, including who owns it and its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-provider.yml">DNS Provider</comp.GlossaryTerm>, please read our documentation on [what a Custom Domain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/).

## Why use a Subdomain?

*Subdomains* let you give each area of your website its own address on one *Custom Domain*. You might use a *Subdomain* to:

* Serve a separate *Site* for each area. Each *Site* can use its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or several *Sites* can share one *Git Repository*, each with its own build settings and configured to show different content.
* Give an area its own hosting option or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm>, such as putting one *Subdomain* behind your own Cloudflare account.
* Send an old or alternative address, such as `www.example.com`, to your main *Site* with a redirect.

You can also serve several *Sites* on one *Subdomain*, each at its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subpath.yml">subpath</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., `/docs/` in `example.com/docs/`). To choose between them:

* **Subdomain** — Has its own hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, it needs its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-records.yml">DNS Record</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Each *Subdomain* that serves *Sites* counts toward the number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites* that your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> includes.
* **Subpath** — Shares the hosting, HTTPS, and DNS settings of its *Subdomain*, and doesn't add to the number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites*.

For more information, please read our documentation on [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/) and [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/).

## Subdomain types

When you add a *Subdomain* to your *Custom Domain*, you must select its *Type*. The *Type* decides what the *Subdomain* does:

* **Site** — The *Subdomain* serves one or more *Sites*, each at its own subpath.
* **Redirect** — The *Subdomain* sends every visitor to another domain.

You can switch your *Subdomain* *Type* at any time. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) and [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/).

### Site Subdomains

A *Site Subdomain* serves the latest <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/build.yml">build</comp.GlossaryTerm> output of one or more CloudCannon *Sites*. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Site build is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/).

To be served on a *Subdomain*, a *Site* must be in <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosted-mode.yml">Hosted Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>, the default <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-building-mode.yml">Site Building Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>. While a *Site* has a *Custom Domain*, you can't switch it to <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/headless-mode.yml">Headless Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Site Building Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-site-building-mode/).

You can serve several *Sites* on one *Subdomain*, each at its own subpath. For example, `example.com` can serve a marketing *Site* at `/` and a documentation *Site* at `/docs/`:

* A visit to `example.com/docs/setup/` goes to the documentation *Site*, because CloudCannon serves the *Site* with the longest subpath that matches the request.
* A visit to `example.com/about/` goes to the marketing *Site*, the only *Site* whose subpath matches.
* If `example.com` had no *Site* at `/`, a visit to a path that doesn't match any *Site*, such as `example.com/about/`, would show a 404 page.

A *Site* at a subpath needs to build its files into a folder that matches the subpath (e.g., a `docs` folder for `/docs/`). For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) and [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Site" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomain page for example.com shows the Sites section with Cards for Marketing site at / and Documentation at /docs/, and the Hosting section with a diagram of Visitors reaching the CloudCannon Origin." title="The Subdomain page" type="screenshot"/>

A *Subdomain* belongs to your *Custom Domain*, not to any *Site*, so removing a *Site* doesn't remove its *Subdomain*. A *Subdomain* with no *Site* keeps its hosting and certificate, and its visitors see a 404 page until you add a *Site*. For more information, please read our documentation on [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/).

### Redirect Subdomains

A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> sends every visitor to another domain, keeping the path and query string. For example, if `www.example.com` redirects to `example.com`, a visit to `www.example.com/about/` goes to `example.com/about/`. The destination can be a *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain* or any other domain.

A *Redirect Subdomain* still needs a DNS Record that points at CloudCannon, and its own *SSL Certificate*, but it doesn't count toward the number of *Subdomains* serving *Sites* that your *Subscription Plan* includes. For status codes, changing where a redirect goes, and redirect loops, please read our documentation on [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Subdomain-Page-Redirect" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomain page for www.example.com shows the Redirect section with the Change Target button and a tree from www.example.com, a 301 permanent redirect, to example.com and the Sites it serves." title="The Subdomain page for a Redirect Subdomain" type="screenshot"/>

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  When you add a plain *Base Domain* (e.g., `example.com`) as a *Site Subdomain*, CloudCannon also adds `www.example.com` as a redirect to it, unless `www.example.com` already exists. Adding `www.example.com` as a *Site Subdomain* adds `example.com` as a redirect in the same way. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Subdomain to your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/).
</comp.Notice>

## The Fallback Redirect

Subdomains you haven't configured (e.g., a misspelled subdomain), including *Subdomains* you have deleted, send visitors to your *Fallback Redirect*, which can be any *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain*. A *Site Subdomain* with no *Site* yet shows its own visitors a 404 page instead. Until you add your first *Subdomain*, there is no *Fallback Redirect*, so visitors to any subdomain see a 404 page. On external DNS, visitors only reach a subdomain you haven't configured if you add a wildcard DNS Record at your *DNS Provider*.

For example, suppose you have one *Site* on `example.com` and another *Site* on `docs.example.com`, and `example.com` is your *Fallback Redirect*. CloudCannon sends anyone trying to access an unconfigured subdomain (e.g., `app.example.com`) to `example.com`.

The *Fallback Redirect* has its own status code, which is *307 temporary redirect, keeping the method* unless you choose another.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Subdomains-Tab-Fallback-Element" alt="A screenshot of the Fallback Redirect section on the Subdomains tab shows the Change Fallback button and a 307 temporary redirect that sends visitors from any unconfigured Subdomain of example.com to example.com." title="The Fallback Redirect section" type="ui-snippet"/>

For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the Fallback Redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/). For the wildcard DNS Record, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS

Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS belong to the *Subdomain*, not to each *Site*, so they apply to every *Site* on the *Subdomain*. Each has its own section on the *Subdomain* page. The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same sections under the *Subdomain settings* heading, for the *Subdomain* that serves that *Site*. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring a Subdomain's hosting, HTTPS, and DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-a-subdomains-hosting-https-and-dns/).

### Hosting

You can choose one of three hosting options for each *Subdomain*: <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>. *CloudCannon Hosting* is the default and recommended option, and serves your *Site* through CloudCannon's global network. *Cloudflare Proxy* keeps your own Cloudflare account in front of CloudCannon, and isn't available while your *Custom Domain* uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>. *Single Origin* sends visitors straight to CloudCannon's origin servers, for when you run your own proxy or firewall in front of CloudCannon.

The hosting option decides which DNS Records the *Subdomain* needs, so changing it can mean updating your records. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the hosting options for a Subdomain are](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-the-hosting-options-for-a-subdomain/), [how hosting works on CloudCannon](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-hosting-work-on-cloudcannon/), and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

### HTTPS

Each *Subdomain* has its own *SSL Certificate*, which covers every *Site* on that *Subdomain*, and its own minimum <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/tls.yml">TLS</comp.GlossaryTerm> version. Subdomains you haven't configured are covered by the domain's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/w/wildcard-certificate.yml">wildcard SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> instead. On external DNS, the wildcard *SSL Certificate* needs records at your *DNS Provider* to be issued and renewed. For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL Certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/), [adding an auto-generated SSL Certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/), and [increasing the minimum TLS version for your domains](/documentation/developer-articles/increase-the-minimum-tls-version-for-your-domains/).

### DNS

Each *Subdomain* needs a DNS Record that points at CloudCannon. On *CloudCannon DNS*, the wildcard record CloudCannon adds for your *Base Domain* covers every *Subdomain* one level below it (e.g., `docs.example.com`) on *CloudCannon Hosting*, unless you add a record for a *Subdomain* that points somewhere else. A *Subdomain* on *Single Origin*, or one nested more than one level deep (e.g., `api.docs.example.com`), needs its own record. On external DNS, you add a record for each *Subdomain* at your *DNS Provider*. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/) and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Who can change a Subdomain

Changing a *Subdomain* requires permission to edit the settings of its *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission). Changes to which *Sites* a *Subdomain* serves, or where it serves them, also require the `site:settings:hosting:write` permission for each of those *Sites*.

Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), and of the *Partner* group in a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, hold both permissions. On the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/team-plan.yml">Team Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/enterprise-plan.yml">Enterprise Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you can also grant them to a [Custom Permission Group](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/). If you don't have permission, the controls for these changes are disabled, and hovering over one shows which permission you need. To get access, ask someone who manages *Permission Groups* in your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
