When a visitor enters a web address into their internet browser, such as https://docs.example.com/setup/, their browser uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns.yml">DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> to find the web server for that website. If you host through CloudCannon, their browser will connect to CloudCannon, or to the proxy you put in front of CloudCannon, and check the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the web address the visitor requested.

Depending on how you configure hosting in CloudCannon, CloudCannon can return a built <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> to the visitor, redirect the visitor to a different domain, or send them to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">Fallback Redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>, your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-provider.yml">DNS Provider</comp.GlossaryTerm>, and your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> and *Site* settings in CloudCannon each control one part of this process.

## Your domain registrar and DNS Provider

Website domains are purchased from a third-party domain registrar, such as Cloudflare Registrar or Namecheap. Owning the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com) means you also own every <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., www.example.com or docs.example.com) and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subpath.yml">subpath</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com/staff/) on it.

Your registrar records who owns the domain, and which <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/n/nameserver.yml">nameservers</comp.GlossaryTerm> manage its DNS Records. When you host through CloudCannon, you can choose whether you manage your DNS Records through CloudCannon as well, or use an external provider. You make this choice when you connect your domain to CloudCannon, and you can change it later on the *Domain Settings* tab of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

* **CloudCannon DNS** — You set the nameservers in your domain registrar to CloudCannon, and CloudCannon manages the DNS Records for each *Subdomain*.
* **External DNS** — Your nameservers point at a *DNS Provider* other than CloudCannon, such as your domain registrar or Cloudflare, and you add the DNS Records there for each *Subdomain* you host through CloudCannon.

For more information, please read our documentation on [what DNS is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/), [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/), and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Your Custom Domain in CloudCannon

To serve your *Sites* on a domain you own, you must [connect it to your Organization as a Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domains-Browser-Connect-a-Domain-Page" alt="A screenshot of the Connect a Custom Domain page shows the Base Domain text field containing example.org and the CloudCannon DNS (recommended) and External DNS options under DNS Provider, next to the App Sidebar with the Custom Domains link." title="The Connect a Custom Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

Each *Custom Domain* you connect gets its own [Domain page](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/), where you can see the status of each *Subdomain* and which *Sites* it serves.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Summary" alt="A screenshot of the Summary tab on the Domain page shows the Subdomains section with 3 of 6 Subdomains need attention and the Fallback Redirect, beside the DNS section, where three Subdomains' DNS Records do not match." title="The Summary tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

CloudCannon sets up hosting for each *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain*, not for the domain as a whole. Each *Subdomain* has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain-type.yml">Type</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which decides what visitors get, a hosting option, which decides how visitors reach CloudCannon, and an *SSL Certificate*, which lets their browser load the address over HTTPS. These settings apply to every *Site* the *Subdomain* serves. The *Subdomains* tab on the *Domain* page lists every *Subdomain* with its hosting, HTTPS, and DNS status.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Subdomains-Tab" alt="A screenshot of the Subdomains tab on the Domain page shows the list of Subdomains with Subdomain, Hosting, HTTPS, and DNS columns, and the Fallback Redirect section." title="The Subdomains tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

### Subdomains

In CloudCannon, every web address you host on is a *Subdomain* (e.g., www.example.com, docs.example.com), including the *Base Domain* itself (e.g., example.com is an empty *Subdomain*). When you add a *Subdomain*, you choose its *Type*, which decides what visitors to the *Subdomain* get once they reach CloudCannon:

* **Site** — CloudCannon serves one or more *Sites*, each at its own subpath. Each *Subdomain* that serves a *Site* counts toward your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>, but extra *Sites* at subpaths on the same *Subdomain* don't. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).
* **Redirect** — CloudCannon sends the visitor to another domain. *Redirect Subdomains* don't count toward your *Subscription Plan*. For more information, please read our documentation on [managing your Redirect Subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-your-redirect-subdomains/).

Visitors to a subdomain you haven't configured (e.g., typo.example.com) go to your *Fallback Redirect*, as long as your DNS sends them to CloudCannon, and visitors to a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-subdomain.yml">Site Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> with no *Site* yet see a 404 page. For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the Fallback Redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/).

### Hosting options

Once you've added a *Subdomain*, its hosting option decides the route visitors take from the DNS Record to CloudCannon. Every new *Subdomain* starts on <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, where visitors reach CloudCannon through its global network, except your *Base Domain* on <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which uses A Records that point straight at CloudCannon's origin servers.

You can change the hosting option from the *Hosting* section of the *Subdomain* page. The hosting option decides which DNS Records the *Subdomain* needs, so changing it can mean updating your records. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, your own Cloudflare account proxies the *Subdomain* to CloudCannon. *Cloudflare Proxy* isn't available while your *Custom Domain* uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>, DNS points straight at CloudCannon's origin servers, or at your own proxy in front of them.

For more information on each hosting option, please read our documentation on [what the hosting options for a Subdomain are](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-the-hosting-options-for-a-subdomain/).

### SSL Certificates

Each *Subdomain* has its own *SSL Certificate*, which covers every *Site* on that *Subdomain*. Your *Custom Domain* can use three kinds:

* An <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/auto-generated-ssl-certificate.yml">auto-generated SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> is issued once the *Subdomain's* DNS Record points at CloudCannon, and CloudCannon renews it for you. On external DNS, you can add TXT Records at your *DNS Provider* first, so the certificate is ready before visitors arrive. Adding TXT Records first isn't available for your *Base Domain* or on *Single Origin*.
* A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-ssl-certificate.yml">Custom SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> is one you buy from a third-party provider and upload to the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon can't renew it, so you upload a new one before it expires.
* A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/w/wildcard-certificate.yml">wildcard SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> covers subdomains you haven't configured (e.g., typo.example.com). CloudCannon issues and renews it, but on external DNS it needs records at your *DNS Provider*.

For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL Certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/), [adding an auto-generated SSL Certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/), [adding a Custom SSL Certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-ssl-certificate/), and [resolving SSL Certificate errors](/documentation/developer-articles/resolve-ssl-certificate-errors/).

## Your Site in CloudCannon

CloudCannon can only serve a *Site* on your *Custom Domain* if the *Site* is in <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosted-mode.yml">Hosted Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>, the default <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-building-mode.yml">Site Building Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>, where CloudCannon builds and hosts it and gives it a free <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. In <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/headless-mode.yml">Headless Mode</comp.GlossaryTerm>, CloudCannon doesn't build or host your *Site*, so you can't serve it on a *Custom Domain* through CloudCannon, but you can still edit its *Site* files. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Site Building Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-site-building-mode/).

### Your Site on a Subdomain

You add a *Site* to a *Site Subdomain* at a subpath, such as `/` or `/docs/`. You can do this from the *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* of the *Site* you want to serve, or from the *Subdomain* page. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) and [managing the Sites on a Subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-sites-on-a-subdomain/).

### Routing within your Site

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/routing.yml">Routing</comp.GlossaryTerm> decides what visitors see at each path within your *Site*. By default, CloudCannon serves the file in your build output that matches the full path, including the *Site's* subpath. For example, it serves `example.com/docs/about/` from `docs/about/index.html`, so a *Site* at a subpath needs to build its files into a folder that matches the subpath. You can also add redirects, headers, a custom 404 page, and extensionless URLs. For more information, please read our documentation on [what routing is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-routing/) and [building your Site to be served on a subpath](/documentation/developer-articles/build-your-site-to-be-served-on-a-subpath/).
