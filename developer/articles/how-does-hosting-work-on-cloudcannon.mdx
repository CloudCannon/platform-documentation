When a visitor enters a web address into their internet browser, such as https://docs.example.com/setup/, their browser uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns.yml">DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> to find the web server for that website. If you host through CloudCannon, their browser will connect to CloudCannon's servers and check the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the web address the visitor requested.

Depending on how you configure hosting in CloudCannon, CloudCannon can return a built <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> to the visitor, redirect the visitor to a different domain, or send them to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">fallback redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

Your domain registrar, your DNS provider, and your *Custom Domain* and *Site* settings in CloudCannon each control one part of this process.

## Your domain registrar and DNS provider

Website domains are purchased from a third-party <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>, such as Cloudflare Registrar or Namecheap. Owning the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com) means you also own every <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., docs.example.com) and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subpath.yml">subpath</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com/staff/) on it.

Your registrar records who owns the domain, and which <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/n/nameserver.yml">nameservers</comp.GlossaryTerm> manage its DNS records. When you host through CloudCannon, you can choose whether you manage your DNS records through CloudCannon as well, or use an external provider.

* **CloudCannon DNS** — You set the nameservers in your domain registrar to CloudCannon, and CloudCannon manages the DNS records for each subdomain.
* **External DNS** — You set the nameservers in your domain registrar to another provider, and add the DNS records there for each subdomain you host through CloudCannon.

For more information, please read our documentation on [what DNS is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/), [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/) and [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/).

## Your Custom Domain in CloudCannon

To serve your *Sites* on a domain you own, you connect it to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> as a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Each *Custom Domain* you connect gets its own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-page.yml">Domain page</comp.GlossaryTerm>, where you can see whether each subdomain is set up.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Overview" alt="A screenshot of the Summary tab on the Domain page shows the Subdomains section with the Add a subdomain row, five subdomains, three that need attention, and the fallback, beside the DNS section, where the nameservers match and two of five subdomains need a change, and the Minimum TLS section set to 1.2." title="The Summary tab on the Domain page" type="screenshot"/>

In CloudCannon, every web address you host on is a subdomain (e.g., www.example.com, docs.example.com), including the *Base Domain* itself (e.g., example.com). Each subdomain either serves one or more *Sites*, each at its own subpath, or redirects visitors to another domain.

For more information, please read our documentation on [connecting a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/), [what the Domain page is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-the-domain-page/), and [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/).

### How visitors reach CloudCannon

Each subdomain has a hosting option, which decides the route visitors take from the DNS record to CloudCannon. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, the default, visitors reach CloudCannon through Cloudflare's global network. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, your own Cloudflare account proxies the subdomain to CloudCannon. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>, DNS points straight at CloudCannon's origin servers, or at your own proxy in front of them. The hosting option decides which DNS records the subdomain needs.

For more information on each hosting option, please read our documentation on [what a Custom Domain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/#hosting-https-and-dns). For the records each hosting option needs, please read our documentation on [choosing how visitors reach a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#choose-how-visitors-reach-a-subdomain).

### SSL certificates

When the browser connects to CloudCannon, it checks the SSL certificate for the subdomain before it loads your *Site* over HTTPS. Each subdomain has its own certificate, which covers every *Site* on that subdomain. CloudCannon can issue and renew the certificate for you, or you can upload your own.

For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/) and [resolving SSL certificate errors](/documentation/developer-articles/resolve-ssl-certificate-errors/).

### What each subdomain serves

Once a visitor reaches CloudCannon, what they see depends on the subdomain they asked for:

* **A subdomain that serves Sites** — CloudCannon serves the *Site* with the longest subpath that matches the request. For example, if example.com serves one *Site* at `/` and another at `/docs/`, a visit to example.com/docs/setup/ goes to the second *Site*. If no *Site* on the subdomain matches the request, the visitor sees a not found page. A *Site* at a subpath needs to build its files into a folder that matches the subpath. For more information, please read our documentation on [building your Site for a path](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/#build-your-site-for-a-path).
* **A redirect subdomain** — CloudCannon sends the visitor to another domain. For more information, please read our documentation on [managing redirect subdomains](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-redirect-subdomains/).
* **A subdomain that isn't set up, or has no Site yet** — CloudCannon sends the visitor to your fallback redirect. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, visitors only reach a subdomain you haven't added if you also add a wildcard DNS record. For more information, please read our documentation on [updating the fallback redirect for your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/update-the-fallback-redirect-for-your-custom-domain/).

## Your Site in CloudCannon

Once CloudCannon knows which *Site* to serve, the settings of that *Site* decide whether CloudCannon can build and host it, and what visitors see at each path within it.

### Site Building Mode

Each *Site* has a *Site Building Mode*, which you can change on the *Details* page under *Site Settings*. In *Hosted Mode*, the default, CloudCannon builds and hosts your *Site*, and gives it a free <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. In *Headless Mode*, CloudCannon doesn't build or host your *Site*, but you can still edit its source files.

Your *Site* must be in *Hosted Mode* to serve it on a *Custom Domain*. CloudCannon won't switch a *Site* that has a *Custom Domain* to *Headless Mode*. To switch it, first remove the *Custom Domain* from your *Site*. Switching to *Headless Mode* takes your *Site* offline and stops its builds.

For more information, please read our documentation on [what Headless Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-headless-mode/) and [removing a Custom Domain from your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/remove-a-custom-domain-from-your-site/).

### Routing within your Site

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/routing.yml">Routing</comp.GlossaryTerm> decides what visitors see at each path within your *Site*. By default, CloudCannon serves the file in your build output that matches the path, and a *Site* in *Hosted Mode* can add redirects, headers, a custom 404 page, and extensionless URLs. For more information, please read our documentation on [what routing is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-routing/).
