<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permission, can switch DNS provider, manage DNS records, fix the record for a subdomain, and update the nameserver status. Changing the hosting option of a subdomain, or marking its record as updated, requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission instead.
</comp.Notice>

External DNS allows you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon but manage your [DNS records](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/#what-are-dns-records) elsewhere. You can set up external DNS through a different provider (e.g., Cloudflare) or your own servers. You add the records CloudCannon needs at that provider, and CloudCannon checks that they point at CloudCannon.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you are using Cloudflare as your DNS provider with its Proxy feature, you need to configure your DNS records differently. For more information, please read the section on [configuring Cloudflare Proxy](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#configure-cloudflare-proxy).
</comp.Notice>

## Enable external DNS

You can choose *External DNS* when you connect a *Custom Domain*, or switch an existing domain to *External DNS* on the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Updating your DNS provider can cause downtime for anything connected to your domain, such as your website or custom email address. Please make sure you have a copy of your existing DNS records before you change providers.

  If you need help updating your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, your current and new DNS providers, and a copy of your existing DNS records.
</comp.Notice>

Records in the CloudCannon DNS zone for your domain stop working once you switch, so add them at your new provider before you change your nameservers.

To switch to *External DNS*:

1. Click the *Domains* button in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Domain Settings* tab. CloudCannon will open the *Details* section.
4. Under *DNS provider*, select the *External DNS* option.
5. Click the *Switch to External DNS* button.

CloudCannon will switch your domain to *External DNS* and check the records of every subdomain on the domain again. CloudCannon labels the provider you switched from *(current)* until you save.

## The records each subdomain needs

Each subdomain needs its own records at your DNS provider. The records depend on how visitors reach the subdomain:

* **CloudCannon Hosting**: A CNAME record pointing at `sites.cloudcannon.com`. Your *Base Domain* (e.g., example.com) needs two A records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.
* **Cloudflare Proxy**: A CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, proxied in your Cloudflare zone.
* **Single Origin**: A CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. Your *Base Domain* needs two A records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.

When you add your *Base Domain*, CloudCannon also adds www.example.com as a redirect to it, and www.example.com needs its own CNAME record. Adding example.com on *CloudCannon Hosting* therefore shows three records: two A records for example.com and a CNAME record for www.example.com.

On *CloudCannon Hosting* and *Cloudflare Proxy*, your *Base Domain* also has a wildcard certificate, which gives subdomains you haven't set up yet a certificate, so their visitors reach your fallback over HTTPS. On *External DNS*, this certificate is validated with TXT records you add at your DNS provider, and it needs new TXT records each time it renews.

## Follow the setup guide

When you connect a domain on *External DNS* or add a subdomain, a setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page shows how far along setup is. It covers the subdomains that don't point at CloudCannon yet, or are still waiting for their certificate, and that you added in the last seven days or marked as updated. The guide names those subdomains, shows three steps (*Add the records*, *Propagation*, and *[Subdomain] goes live*), and has a *Show the records* button that opens the *DNS* tab, where you add the records.

The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same guide for the subdomain that hosts your *Site*, and notes that the *Site* stays on its *Testing Domain* until the subdomain goes live. On that page, the *Show the record* button scrolls to the *DNS* section, which lists the record the subdomain needs.

## Add the records

To add the records:

1. Log in to your third-party DNS provider.
2. On the *DNS* tab of your *Domain* page, click the *Copy* button next to each value in the *Needs* column of the *Required records* table, and add the record at your DNS provider. For a TXT record, use the *Name* shown below its value. If the subdomain uses *Cloudflare Proxy*, add the record in your Cloudflare zone with the proxy turned on.
3. Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME records with the same name.
4. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *Status* column for each record you added.

CloudCannon will check every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button, and show *Waiting for DNS* with the time you marked the record as updated in place of the button. There's no need to keep the page open. Once CloudCannon finds the records and issues the certificate, it emails you that the subdomain is live. If the *Summary* tab is open at the time, the setup guide shows that the subdomain is live, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button and a *Dismiss* button.

Until your *Base Domain* has its first wildcard certificate, the *DNS* tab also lists the TXT records for that certificate under *Wildcard certificate records*. Add them the same way.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Overview-Setup-Guide-External-Waiting" alt="A screenshot of the setup guide on the Summary tab shows Nice work! We're looking for the records for example.org and www.example.org, with Propagating... in progress and a Show the records button." title="The setup guide while CloudCannon looks for the records" type="screenshot" />

If CloudCannon still can't find a record 48 hours after you clicked the *I've updated it* button, the setup guide shows that CloudCannon can't find the records, and the *DNS* tab shows a notice for the subdomain with what usually fixes it:

* Check that the record is saved at your DNS provider, not only entered.
* Some DNS providers add the domain for you, so the name might need to be the subdomain part on its own.
* Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME records with the same name.

## Review the records on the DNS tab

On *External DNS*, the *DNS* tab shows the nameservers CloudCannon found for your domain, each marked *Another provider*, a *Change DNS provider* link, and the *Required records* table, which lists every subdomain on your *Custom Domain* with *Subdomain*, *Needs*, *Found*, and *Status* columns. Below the heading, the table shows how many subdomains need a change, and when CloudCannon last checked them. Click the refresh button next to the heading to check every subdomain again. Each subdomain's status is one of:

* **Connected**: The subdomain reaches CloudCannon.
* **Not pointing at CloudCannon**: A record for the subdomain points somewhere else.
* **No DNS record**: CloudCannon found no record for the subdomain.
* **Not checked yet**: CloudCannon hasn't checked the subdomain yet.
* **Waiting for DNS**: You added the subdomain or marked its record as updated in the last 48 hours, and CloudCannon is still looking for the record.

For a subdomain that isn't connected, the *Status* column also shows the *I've updated it* button, or when you marked the record as updated.

Above the table, the *DNS* tab shows a notice for each subdomain that still isn't pointing at CloudCannon 48 hours after you marked it as updated, and for each subdomain whose record changed after it was live.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-External" alt="A screenshot of the DNS tab for a domain on external DNS shows the Nameservers table with both nameservers marked Another provider, and notices that blog.example.com still isn't pointing at CloudCannon and that the DNS record of www.example.com changed." title="The DNS tab for a domain on external DNS" type="screenshot" />

Below the table, the *Optional: catch unknown subdomains* section shows a wildcard CNAME record for your domain (e.g., \*.example.com pointing at `sites.cloudcannon.com`). Without a wildcard record, a subdomain you haven't added doesn't resolve. With one, its visitors reach your fallback. Records you add for specific subdomains always take priority over the wildcard.

The *DNS* section of each subdomain page shows the same record for that one subdomain, with the same *Copy* and *I've updated it* buttons. Any TXT records for its certificate are in its *HTTPS* section.

## How often CloudCannon checks your records

CloudCannon checks the records of each subdomain daily, and every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button.

To check a subdomain immediately, click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section of its subdomain page. You can check each subdomain at most once a minute. If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the section says when the check failed, and keeps the last known status.

## Subdomain emails

CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) when:

* **[Subdomain] is live**: CloudCannon found the record for a subdomain you marked as updated, and issued its certificate. The email shows the DNS record and the *Site* or redirect now live on the subdomain, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button.
* **[Subdomain] still is not pointing at CloudCannon**: CloudCannon still can't find the record 48 hours after you clicked the *I've updated it* button. The email shows the record the subdomain needs, what CloudCannon found, and what usually fixes it. CloudCannon sends this email once each time you click the *I've updated it* button.
* **[Subdomain]'s DNS record changed**: The record of a live subdomain stops pointing at CloudCannon. The email shows what the record points at now. Change the record back at your DNS provider, or, if you moved the subdomain on purpose, remove it from CloudCannon.

## Renew the wildcard certificate

The wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain* needs new TXT records at your DNS provider each time it renews. Before it expires, CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* the new TXT records, and the *HTTPS* section of the subdomain page for your *Base Domain* shows them under *Other subdomains* with the date to add them by. You can instead add one CNAME record that lets Cloudflare renew the certificate on its own. For more information, please read our documentation on [renewing the wildcard certificate on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-certificate-on-external-dns).

## Choose how visitors reach a subdomain

Each subdomain on your *Custom Domain* has its own hosting option, which applies to every *Site* on that subdomain. You can choose one of three options:

* **CloudCannon Hosting**: CloudCannon's global CDN serves the subdomain. This is the default and recommended option. Your *Base Domain* can't point at the CDN on *External DNS*, so its A records point straight at CloudCannon's origin instead.
* **Cloudflare Proxy**: Your own Cloudflare zone proxies the subdomain to CloudCannon's origin.
* **Single Origin**: DNS points straight at CloudCannon's origin, or through your own proxy.

For more information on subdomains, please read our documentation on [managing the subdomains on your Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-subdomains-on-your-custom-domain/).

## Configure Cloudflare Proxy

CloudCannon hosting uses Cloudflare. If you are using Cloudflare as your DNS provider and have Cloudflare's Proxy feature enabled for your DNS records, you can't proxy directly to the normal CloudCannon CNAME (i.e., `sites.cloudcannon.com`).

To resolve this issue, you can either turn off Cloudflare's Proxy feature for the record, or set the subdomain's hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy* and add a CNAME record to your DNS provider.

The *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal labels the saved option *Current*, shows the DNS record the selected option needs, and warns you if the record has to change.

To set a subdomain's hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy*:

1. Click the *Domains* button in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Open the *Subdomains* tab, then select the subdomain. CloudCannon will open the page for that subdomain.
4. Click the *Configure* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
5. Select the *Cloudflare Proxy* option under *Hosting*.
6. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your Cloudflare zone to proxy visitors to the CloudCannon origin.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Orange-Cloud" alt="A screenshot of the Hosting for example.com modal shows the CloudCannon Hosting, Cloudflare Proxy, and Single Origin options, with Cloudflare Proxy selected and the CNAME record it needs, and the Save hosting button." title="The Hosting modal" type="screenshot" />

To add a CNAME record:

1. Log in to Cloudflare and navigate to your DNS settings.
2. Add a CNAME record pointing your subdomain (e.g., docs.example.com) at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, with the proxy turned on.
3. Set Cloudflare's SSL/TLS encryption mode to *Full (strict)*.
4. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section of the subdomain page.

Cloudflare will confirm that you own the subdomain from the proxied record, and issue its certificate. To issue the certificate before you change your proxied record, click the *Issue the SSL certificate before you switch* button in the *HTTPS* section. For more information, please read our documentation on [issuing the certificate before you switch a proxied record](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#issue-the-certificate-before-you-switch-a-proxied-record).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you use *Cloudflare Proxy*, Cloudflare might cache your *Site*. In that case, your *Site* won't show any updates until you clear your Cloudflare cache.
</comp.Notice>

## Single Origin

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  CloudCannon does not recommend *Single Origin* unless you have specific setup requirements. For assistance setting up your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, the subdomain, your DNS provider and any proxy in front of CloudCannon, and the records shown under *Needs* and *Found* in the *DNS* section of the subdomain page.
</comp.Notice>

In some cases, you may need to configure your DNS records to point directly at CloudCannon's host origin rather than `sites.cloudcannon.com`, for example when another provider proxies your subdomain. *Single Origin* skips these features of *CloudCannon Hosting*:

* Faster DNS lookup speed due to a globally distributed edge caching network.
* Certificate validation before your DNS points at CloudCannon. With *Single Origin*, CloudCannon can only validate your certificate once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin.

To point your DNS straight at the CloudCannon origin, set the subdomain's hosting to *Single Origin* and add DNS records to your DNS provider.

To set a subdomain's hosting to *Single Origin*:

1. Click the *Domains* button in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Open the *Subdomains* tab, then select the subdomain. CloudCannon will open the page for that subdomain.
4. Click the *Configure* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
5. Select the *Single Origin* option under *Hosting*. CloudCannon will show the DNS record the subdomain needs.
6. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your DNS to point straight at the CloudCannon origin.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Single-Origin" alt="A screenshot of the Hosting for example.com modal shows the CloudCannon Hosting, Cloudflare Proxy, and Single Origin options, with Single Origin selected and the DNS records it needs, and the Save hosting button." title="The Hosting modal" type="screenshot" />

The *DNS* section of the subdomain page lists the records under *Needs*. For a subdomain, this is a CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. For your *Base Domain*, it is two A records instead.

To add the DNS records:

1. Log in to your third-party DNS provider.
2. Add the records listed under *Needs* in the *DNS* section of the subdomain page.
3. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section.

CloudCannon will show the subdomain as *Connected* in the *DNS* section once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin, and will answer the certificate challenge for the subdomain itself.

If your own proxy sits in front of CloudCannon, have it connect to CloudCannon over HTTPS, keep the visitor's hostname in the `Host` header, and pass requests to `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` through to CloudCannon, so the certificate authority can reach the challenge. If your proxy connects over HTTP and the subdomain has *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* ticked, CloudCannon answers every request with a redirect to HTTPS, which can send visitors in a redirect loop.
