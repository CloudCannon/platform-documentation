<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions, can view and change the DNS of a *Base Domain*.
</comp.Notice>

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">External DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> allows you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon but manage your [DNS Records](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/#what-are-dns-records) elsewhere. You can set up External DNS through a different provider (e.g., Cloudflare) or your own servers. You add the records CloudCannon needs at that provider, and CloudCannon checks that they point at CloudCannon.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you use Cloudflare as your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns-provider.yml">DNS Provider</comp.GlossaryTerm> and set a record's proxy status to *Proxied*, you need to configure your DNS Records differently. For more information, please read the section on [configuring Cloudflare Proxy](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#configure-cloudflare-proxy).
</comp.Notice>

## Enable External DNS

You can choose *External DNS* when you connect a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or switch an existing domain to External DNS on the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Updating your *DNS Provider* can cause downtime for anything connected to your domain, such as your website or custom email address. Please make sure you have a copy of your existing DNS Records before you change providers.

  If you need help updating your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, your current and new *DNS Providers*, and a copy of your existing DNS Records.
</comp.Notice>

Records in the CloudCannon DNS zone for your domain stop working once you switch, so add them at your new provider before you change your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/n/nameserver.yml">nameservers</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

If you are a member of multiple <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organizations</comp.GlossaryTerm>, make sure you select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to first. For more information, please read our documentation on [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

To switch to External DNS:

1. Click the *Custom Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Domain Settings* tab. CloudCannon will open the *Details* section.
4. Under *DNS Provider*, select the *External DNS* option. CloudCannon will label the provider you are switching from *(current)*, and warn that records in its DNS zone stop being used once you switch.
5. Click the *Switch to External DNS* button.

CloudCannon will switch your domain to External DNS. For the next 48 hours, each *Subdomain* that a check found not pointing at CloudCannon shows *Checking*, and a *Subdomain* CloudCannon hasn't checked yet shows *Not checked yet*. CloudCannon keeps checking the records daily.

## The records each Subdomain needs

Each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> needs its own records at your *DNS Provider*. The records depend on how visitors reach the *Subdomain*, through <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — A CNAME Record pointing at `sites.cloudcannon.com`. Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com) needs two A Records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — A CNAME Record in your Cloudflare zone pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, with its proxy status set to *Proxied*.
* **Single Origin** — A CNAME Record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. Your *Base Domain* needs two A Records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.

When you add your *Base Domain* as a new *Subdomain* that serves a *Site*, CloudCannon also adds www.example.com as a redirect to it, unless www.example.com already exists, and www.example.com needs its own CNAME Record. Adding example.com on *CloudCannon Hosting* therefore shows three records: two A Records for example.com and a CNAME Record for www.example.com. In the same way, adding www.example.com as a *Subdomain* that serves a *Site* also adds example.com as a redirect to it, unless example.com already exists.

Your *Base Domain* also has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/w/wildcard-certificate.yml">wildcard SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which gives subdomains you haven't set up yet a certificate, so their visitors reach your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/fallback-redirect.yml">Fallback Redirect</comp.GlossaryTerm> over HTTPS. On External DNS, this certificate is validated with TXT Records you add at your *DNS Provider*, and it needs new TXT Records each time it renews, unless you add a CNAME Record named `_acme-challenge.[Base Domain]` once. With that CNAME Record, the certificate renews by itself.

## Follow the setup guide

When you connect a domain on External DNS or add a *Subdomain*, a setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page shows how far along setup is. It covers the *Subdomains* that don't point at CloudCannon yet, or are still waiting for their certificate, and that you added in the last seven days or marked as updated. The guide is headed *A Subdomain requires DNS Record updates* or *Multiple Subdomains require DNS Record updates*, names those *Subdomains*, shows three steps (*Add the DNS Records*, *DNS Records match*, and *Your Subdomains go live*, or the singular forms for one record or one *Subdomain*), and has an *Open the DNS tab* button that opens the *DNS* tab, where you add the records. If you don't have the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` permission, the button is *Open the Subdomain page* instead, or *Open the Subdomains tab*, which opens the *Subdomains* tab filtered to the *Subdomains* that need attention, when more than one is waiting.

The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same guide for the *Subdomain* that hosts your *Site*, and notes that the *Site* stays on its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> until the *Subdomain* goes live. On that page, the *Scroll to DNS Records* button scrolls to the *DNS* section, which lists the record the *Subdomain* needs. The *DNS* section only appears if you have the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission.

## Add the records

The *Required records* table on the *DNS* tab lists the record each *Subdomain* needs in its *Record* column, followed by any records CloudCannon found at that name. For a TXT Record, the *Record* column shows the record's *Name* below its value, with its own *Copy* button. If the *Subdomain* uses *Cloudflare Proxy*, add the record in your Cloudflare zone with the proxy status set to *Proxied*.

Clicking the *I've updated it* button requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission.

If you are a member of multiple *Organizations*, make sure you select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to first. For more information, please read our documentation on [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

To add the records:

1. Click the *Custom Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to add records by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *DNS* tab. CloudCannon will open the *DNS* tab, with the *Required records* table.
4. Log in to your third-party *DNS Provider*.
5. Click the *Copy* icon next to a needed record in the *Record* column. CloudCannon will copy the value.
6. Add the record at your *DNS Provider* with the value you copied. Repeat steps 5 and 6 for each needed record in the *Record* column.
7. Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME Records with the same name at your *DNS Provider*.
8. Click the *I've updated it* button below the name of each *Subdomain* whose records you added.

CloudCannon will check every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button, and hide the button for those seven days. For the first 48 hours, the *Status* column shows *Checking*. After that, it shows the status CloudCannon found, while CloudCannon keeps checking. The setup guide and the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page show when you marked the records as updated. There's no need to keep the page open. Once CloudCannon finds the records and issues the *SSL Certificate*, it emails you that the *Subdomain* is live. If the *Summary* tab is open at the time, the setup guide shows that the *Subdomain* is live, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button and a *Dismiss* button.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Summary-Setup-Guide-External-Waiting" alt="A screenshot of the setup guide on the Summary tab shows the heading Checking the DNS Records for multiple Subdomains, that the DNS Records were marked as updated 12 minutes ago, You added the DNS Records done with Checking DNS Records in progress, and the Open the DNS tab button." title="The setup guide while CloudCannon looks for the records" type="screenshot" />

Whenever the wildcard *SSL Certificate* for your *Base Domain* needs validating, including when it is first issued and each time it renews, the *DNS* tab also lists the TXT Records for that certificate under *Wildcard certificate records*. For the first certificate, the heading reads *Wildcard certificate records (optional)*: the wildcard *SSL Certificate* only covers subdomains you haven't set up, so the setup guide doesn't ask for these records. Add them the same way if you want them.

Below the *Wildcard certificate records* table, the *Automatic renewal record (optional)* table offers a CNAME Record instead: *Add this CNAME Record once and the certificate renews by itself, without TXT Records. Remove any TXT Records with the same name first.* The record points `_acme-challenge.[Base Domain]` (e.g., `_acme-challenge.example.com`) at an address ending in `.dcv.cloudflare.com`, which starts with your *Base Domain*. The *DNS* tab shows the exact value, with a *Copy* icon for the name and the value. If you add it, you don't need to add TXT Records for later renewals.

If CloudCannon still can't find a record 48 hours after you clicked the *I've updated it* button, the setup guide shows *The DNS Record for a Subdomain does not match*, *The DNS Records for a Subdomain do not match*, or *The DNS Records for multiple Subdomains do not match*, with a *How can I fix this?* box, and the *DNS* tab shows a notice for the *Subdomain*. Both list what usually fixes it:

* Check that the record is saved at your *DNS Provider*, not only entered.
* Some *DNS Providers* add the domain for you, so the name might need to be the subdomain part on its own.
* Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME Records with the same name.

## Review the records on the DNS tab

On External DNS, the *DNS* tab starts with the *External DNS nameservers* section, if you have the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` permission. It has a *Configure External DNS* link, and, if you can view the settings of your *Base Domain*, a *change the DNS Provider* link. Its table lists your *Base Domain* with the nameservers CloudCannon found and the status *Not CloudCannon DNS*. Without the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` permission, the tab says *The Custom Domain [Base Domain] uses an external DNS Provider.* and links to this article instead.

The *Required records* table lists each *Subdomain* whose record doesn't point at CloudCannon yet, with *Subdomain*, *Record*, and *Status* columns. The *Record* column shows the record each *Subdomain* needs, then any records CloudCannon found at that name. Once every *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon, a notice takes the place of the table, headed *[Subdomain] points at CloudCannon* for one *Subdomain*, *Both subdomains point at CloudCannon* for two, or *All [Number] subdomains point at CloudCannon* for more. The *Status* column, and the *DNS* section of each *Subdomain* page, show what CloudCannon found, such as *Configured*, *Not pointing at CloudCannon*, or *Checking*. For what each status means, please read our documentation on [how CloudCannon checks your DNS Records](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-cloudcannon-check-your-dns-records/#what-each-dns-record-status-means).

For a *Subdomain* that isn't connected, the *Subdomain* column also shows the *I've updated it* button, with *Checks hourly for a week* below it. The button is hidden for seven days after you click it.

Above the table, one warning notice names each *Subdomain* that still isn't pointing at CloudCannon 48 hours after you marked it as updated, with what usually fixes it, and each *Subdomain* whose record changed after it was live.

The *External DNS nameservers* table and each record table on the *DNS* tab have a footer that says when CloudCannon last checked, with a *Check now* button that checks every record in the table again. The nameservers footer also says *CloudCannon checks daily.*, and the record table footers say *Check now checks every record once.* The *Automatic renewal record (optional)* table has no footer. The footer of the *External DNS nameservers* table also has a *View nameserver check history* icon, which opens the *Nameserver checks for [Base Domain]* modal.

When you click a *Copy* icon, the icon changes to a tick and *Copied!* appears next to it.

Without a wildcard record, a subdomain you haven't added doesn't resolve. With one, its visitors reach your *Fallback Redirect*, or get a not found page if you haven't set one. The status changes to *Checking*, and once CloudCannon finds the record, it changes to *Configured*. If your *Base Domain* is one of your *Subdomains* on CloudCannon, the notice has a *View HTTPS for [Base Domain]* link to its *Subdomain* page.

Until you add the CNAME Record that renews the wildcard *SSL Certificate* by itself, the *Automatic renewal record (optional)* table lists that record, below the *Wildcard certificate* notice or below the *Wildcard certificate records* table, with the same columns as the other record tables, a *Copy* icon for its name and value, and the status *Optional*. Once CloudCannon finds the record, the table goes away.

## How often CloudCannon checks your records

CloudCannon checks the records of each *Subdomain* daily, and every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button. Adding a *Subdomain*, or changing its hosting, doesn't start hourly checks, so click the *I've updated it* button once you have added the record.

To check a *Subdomain* immediately, click the *Check now* button in the footer of the *DNS* section of its *Subdomain* page, or in the footer of the *Required records* table to check every *Subdomain* listed there. For every time CloudCannon checks, the *Check now* limits and permissions, and the check history, please read our documentation on [how CloudCannon checks your DNS Records](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-cloudcannon-check-your-dns-records/).

## Renew the wildcard SSL Certificate

The wildcard *SSL Certificate* for your *Base Domain* needs new TXT Records at your *DNS Provider* each time it renews, unless you add a CNAME Record once. The first wildcard *SSL Certificate* is optional, because your *Base Domain* and the *Subdomains* you have set up have their own certificates. Once you have one, renew it on time, or visitors to subdomains you haven't set up see a security warning. Before it expires, CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* the new TXT Records, and the *DNS* tab lists them under *Wildcard certificate records*, with the CNAME Record in the *Automatic renewal record (optional)* table below. You can add the CNAME Record at any time, not only during a renewal: while the record is missing, the *DNS* tab offers it, and the *Other subdomains* block in the *HTTPS* section of the *Subdomain* page for your *Base Domain* links to it. Once you add it, the certificate renews by itself, and you don't need to add TXT Records again. For more information, please read our documentation on [renewing the wildcard SSL Certificate on External DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-ssl-certificate-on-external-dns).

## Choose how visitors reach a Subdomain

Each *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain* has its own hosting option, which applies to every *Site* on that *Subdomain* and decides the records it needs at your *DNS Provider*. Changing a *Subdomain's* hosting requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission. For the records each hosting option needs on external DNS, please read our documentation on [the records each Subdomain needs](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#the-records-each-subdomain-needs).

For how each hosting option routes visitors, and when to choose it, please read our documentation on [what the hosting options for a Subdomain are](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-the-hosting-options-for-a-subdomain/).

## Configure Cloudflare Proxy

CloudCannon hosting uses Cloudflare. If you use Cloudflare as your *DNS Provider* and a record's proxy status is *Proxied*, you can't proxy directly to the normal CloudCannon CNAME (i.e., `sites.cloudcannon.com`).

To resolve this issue, you can either set the record's proxy status to *DNS only*, or set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy* and add a CNAME Record to your *DNS Provider*.

The *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal labels the saved option *Current*, shows the DNS Record the selected option needs, and warns you if the record has to change.

If you are a member of multiple *Organizations*, make sure you select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to first. For more information, please read our documentation on [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

To set a *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy*:

1. Click the *Custom Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will list the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*.
4. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
5. Click the *Configure hosting* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
6. Select the *Cloudflare Proxy* option under *Hosting*. CloudCannon will show the DNS Record the *Subdomain* needs.
7. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your Cloudflare zone to proxy visitors to the CloudCannon origin.

To add a CNAME Record:

1. Log in to Cloudflare.
2. Navigate to the DNS Records for your domain.
3. Add a CNAME Record pointing your *Subdomain* (e.g., docs.example.com) at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, with the proxy status set to *Proxied*.
4. Set Cloudflare's SSL/TLS encryption mode to *Full (strict)*.
5. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.

Cloudflare will confirm that you own the *Subdomain* from the proxied record, and issue its *SSL Certificate*. To issue the certificate before you change your proxied record, click the *Switch to TXT validation* button in the *HTTPS* section. For more information, please read our documentation on [issuing the certificate before you switch a proxied record](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#issue-the-certificate-before-you-switch-a-proxied-record).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you use *Cloudflare Proxy*, Cloudflare can keep serving files it has cached until they expire or you purge them in Cloudflare. By default, Cloudflare caches static files, such as images, CSS, and JavaScript, but not HTML pages.
</comp.Notice>

## Configure Single Origin

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  CloudCannon does not recommend *Single Origin* unless you have specific setup requirements. For assistance setting up your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, the *Subdomain*, your *DNS Provider* and any proxy in front of CloudCannon, and the records shown in the *Record* column of the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
</comp.Notice>

In some cases, you may need to configure your DNS Records to point directly at CloudCannon's host origin rather than `sites.cloudcannon.com`, for example when another provider proxies your *Subdomain*. *Single Origin* skips these features of *CloudCannon Hosting*:

* CloudCannon's global network, which serves your pages from locations around the world, so they load quickly everywhere.
* Certificate validation before your DNS points at CloudCannon. With *Single Origin*, CloudCannon can only validate your *SSL Certificate* once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin.

To point your DNS straight at the CloudCannon origin, set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Single Origin* and add DNS Records to your *DNS Provider*.

If you are a member of multiple *Organizations*, make sure you select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to first. For more information, please read our documentation on [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

To set a *Subdomain's* hosting to *Single Origin*:

1. Click the *Custom Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
2. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will list the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*.
4. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
5. Click the *Configure hosting* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
6. Select the *Single Origin* option under *Hosting*. CloudCannon will show the DNS Records the *Subdomain* needs.
7. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your DNS to point straight at the CloudCannon origin.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Single-Origin" alt="A screenshot of the Hosting for example.com modal shows the CloudCannon Hosting, Cloudflare Proxy, and Single Origin options, with Single Origin selected and the DNS Records it needs, and the Save hosting button." title="The Hosting modal with Single Origin selected" type="screenshot" />

The *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page lists the records it needs in the *Record* column. For a *Subdomain*, this is a CNAME Record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. For your *Base Domain*, it is two A Records instead.

To add the DNS Records:

1. Log in to your third-party *DNS Provider*.
2. Add the records listed in the *Record* column of the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
3. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section.

CloudCannon will show the *Subdomain* as *Configured* in the *DNS* section once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin, and will answer the certificate challenge for the *Subdomain* itself.

If your own proxy sits in front of CloudCannon, have it connect to CloudCannon over HTTPS, keep the visitor's hostname in the `Host` header, and pass requests to `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` through to CloudCannon, so the certificate authority can reach the challenge. If your proxy connects over HTTP and the *Subdomain* has *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* ticked, CloudCannon answers every request with a redirect to HTTPS, which can send visitors in a redirect loop.
