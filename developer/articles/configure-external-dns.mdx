<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` and `base-domain:settings:dns:write` permissions, can view and change the DNS of a *Base Domain*. Changing how a *Subdomain* is hosted, and checking or updating the status of its DNS record, requires the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission.
</comp.Notice>

<comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">External DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> allows you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon but manage your [DNS records](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/#what-are-dns-records) elsewhere. You can set up external DNS through a different provider (e.g., Cloudflare) or your own servers. You add the records CloudCannon needs at that provider, and CloudCannon checks that they point at CloudCannon.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [connected a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you use Cloudflare as your DNS provider and set a record's proxy status to *Proxied*, you need to configure your DNS records differently. For more information, please read the section on [configuring Cloudflare Proxy](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#configure-cloudflare-proxy).
</comp.Notice>

## Enable external DNS

You can choose *External DNS* when you connect a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or switch an existing domain to external DNS on the *Details* section of the *Domain Settings* tab.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Updating your DNS provider can cause downtime for anything connected to your domain, such as your website or custom email address. Please make sure you have a copy of your existing DNS records before you change providers.

  If you need help updating your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, your current and new DNS providers, and a copy of your existing DNS records.
</comp.Notice>

Records in the CloudCannon DNS zone for your domain stop working once you switch, so add them at your new provider before you change your nameservers.

To switch to external DNS:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
4. Select the domain for which you want to configure DNS by clicking the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Domain Settings* tab. CloudCannon will open the *Details* section.
6. Under *DNS provider*, select the *External DNS* option. CloudCannon will label the provider you are switching from *(current)*, and warn that records in its DNS zone stop being used once you switch.
7. Click the *Switch to External DNS* button.

CloudCannon will switch your domain to external DNS. Each *Subdomain* that doesn't point at CloudCannon yet shows *Waiting for DNS* for the next 48 hours, while CloudCannon keeps checking its records daily.

## The records each Subdomain needs

Each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> needs its own records at your DNS provider. The records depend on how visitors reach the *Subdomain*, through <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm>, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — A CNAME record pointing at `sites.cloudcannon.com`. Your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com) needs two A records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — A CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, proxied in your Cloudflare zone.
* **Single Origin** — A CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. Your *Base Domain* needs two A records instead, pointing at `52.8.178.132` and `184.169.135.34`.

When you add your *Base Domain* as a new *Subdomain*, CloudCannon also adds www.example.com as a redirect to it, unless www.example.com already exists, and www.example.com needs its own CNAME record. Adding example.com on *CloudCannon Hosting* therefore shows three records: two A records for example.com and a CNAME record for www.example.com.

Your *Base Domain* also has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/w/wildcard-certificate.yml">wildcard certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which gives subdomains you haven't set up yet a certificate, so their visitors reach your fallback over HTTPS. On external DNS, this certificate is validated with TXT records you add at your DNS provider, and it needs new TXT records each time it renews, unless you add a CNAME record named `_acme-challenge.[Base Domain]` once. With that CNAME record, the certificate renews by itself.

## Follow the setup guide

When you connect a domain on external DNS or add a *Subdomain*, a setup guide at the top of the *Summary* tab of your *Domain* page shows how far along setup is. It covers the *Subdomains* that don't point at CloudCannon yet, or are still waiting for their certificate, and that you added in the last seven days or marked as updated. The guide names those *Subdomains*, shows three steps (*Add the records*, *Propagation*, and *[Subdomain] goes live*), and has a *Show the records* button that opens the *DNS* tab, where you add the records. If you don't have the `base-domain:settings:dns:read` permission, the button opens the *Subdomain* page instead, or the *Subdomains* tab filtered to the *Subdomains* that need attention when more than one is waiting.

The *Custom Domain* page under *Site Settings* shows the same guide for the *Subdomain* that hosts your *Site*, and notes that the *Site* stays on its *Testing Domain* until the *Subdomain* goes live. On that page, the *Show the record* button scrolls to the *DNS* section, which lists the record the *Subdomain* needs.

## Add the records

The *Required records* table on the *DNS* tab lists the records each *Subdomain* needs in its *Needs* column. For a TXT record, the *Needs* column shows the record's *Name* below its value, with its own *Copy* button. If the *Subdomain* uses *Cloudflare Proxy*, add the record in your Cloudflare zone with the proxy status set to *Proxied*.

To add the records:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
4. Select the domain for which you want to add records by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *DNS* tab. CloudCannon will open the *DNS* tab, with the *Required records* table.
6. Log in to your third-party DNS provider.
7. Click the *Copy* button next to a value in the *Needs* column. CloudCannon will copy the value.
8. Add the record at your DNS provider with the value you copied. Repeat steps 7 and 8 for each value in the *Needs* column.
9. Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME records with the same name at your DNS provider.
10. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *Status* column for each record you added.

CloudCannon will check every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button. For those seven days, the *Status* column shows when you marked the record as updated in place of the button. For the first 48 hours, the record shows *Waiting for DNS*. After that, it shows the status CloudCannon found, while CloudCannon keeps checking. There's no need to keep the page open. Once CloudCannon finds the records and issues the certificate, it emails you that the *Subdomain* is live. If the *Summary* tab is open at the time, the setup guide shows that the *Subdomain* is live, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button and a *Dismiss* button.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-Overview-Setup-Guide-External-Waiting" alt="A screenshot of the setup guide on the Summary tab shows Looking for the records for example.org and www.example.org, with Propagation in progress and a Show the records button." title="The setup guide while CloudCannon looks for the records" type="screenshot" />

Whenever the wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain* needs validating, including when it is first issued and each time it renews, the *DNS* tab also lists the TXT records for that certificate under *Wildcard certificate records*. Add them the same way.

Below the *Wildcard certificate records* table, the *DNS* tab offers a CNAME record instead: *Add this CNAME record once and the certificate renews by itself, without TXT records. Remove any TXT records with the same name first.* The record points `_acme-challenge.[Base Domain]` (e.g., `_acme-challenge.example.com`) at an address ending in `.dcv.cloudflare.com`, which starts with your *Base Domain*. The *DNS* tab shows the exact value, with a *Copy* button for the name and the value. If you add it, you don't need to add TXT records for later renewals.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-External-Wildcard-Renewal-Delegation" alt="A screenshot of the DNS tab for a domain on external DNS shows two TXT records for *.example.com under Wildcard certificate records, each with No DNS record and an I've updated it button, and below them the offer to add a CNAME record named _acme-challenge.example.com once so the certificate renews by itself." title="The wildcard certificate records on the DNS tab" type="screenshot" />

If CloudCannon still can't find a record 48 hours after you clicked the *I've updated it* button, the setup guide shows that CloudCannon can't find the records, and the *DNS* tab shows a notice for the *Subdomain* with what usually fixes it:

* Check that the record is saved at your DNS provider, not only entered.
* Some DNS providers add the domain for you, so the name might need to be the subdomain part on its own.
* Remove any other A, AAAA, or CNAME records with the same name.

## Review the records on the DNS tab

On external DNS, the *DNS* tab shows the nameservers CloudCannon found for your domain, each marked *Another provider*, a *Change DNS provider* link, and the *Required records* table, which lists every *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain* with *Subdomain*, *Needs*, *Found*, and *Status* columns. Below the heading, the table shows how many *Subdomains* need a change, and when CloudCannon last checked them. Click the refresh button next to the heading to check every *Subdomain* again. Each *Subdomain's* status is one of:

* **Connected** — The *Subdomain* reaches CloudCannon.
* **Not pointing at CloudCannon** — A record for the *Subdomain* points somewhere else.
* **No DNS record** — CloudCannon found no record for the *Subdomain*.
* **Not checked yet** — CloudCannon hasn't checked the *Subdomain* yet.
* **Waiting for DNS** — In the last 48 hours, you added the *Subdomain*, changed its hosting, changed your DNS provider, or marked its record as updated, and CloudCannon is still looking for the record.

For a *Subdomain* that isn't connected, the *Status* column also shows the *I've updated it* button, or, for seven days after you click it, when you marked the record as updated.

Above the table, the *DNS* tab shows a notice for each *Subdomain* that still isn't pointing at CloudCannon 48 hours after you marked it as updated, and for each *Subdomain* whose record changed after it was live.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Domain-DNS-External" alt="A screenshot of the DNS tab for a domain on external DNS shows the Nameservers table with both nameservers marked Another provider, and notices that blog.example.com still isn't pointing at CloudCannon and that the DNS record of www.example.com changed." title="The DNS tab for a domain on external DNS" type="screenshot" />

Below the table, the *Optional wildcard record* section shows a wildcard CNAME record for your domain (e.g., \*.example.com pointing at `sites.cloudcannon.com`). Without a wildcard record, a subdomain you haven't added doesn't resolve. With one, its visitors reach your fallback. Records you add for specific *Subdomains* always take priority over the wildcard.

Below the wildcard record, a notice explains how the wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain* renews. Until you add the CNAME record that renews it by itself, the notice offers that record, with a *Copy* button for its name and value. Once CloudCannon finds the record, the notice says that the certificate *renews by itself through the CNAME record you added*.

## How often CloudCannon checks your records

CloudCannon checks the records of each *Subdomain* daily, and every hour for up to seven days after you click the *I've updated it* button. Adding a *Subdomain*, or changing its hosting, doesn't start hourly checks, so click the *I've updated it* button once you have added the record.

To check a *Subdomain* immediately, click the *Check now* button in the *DNS* section of its *Subdomain* page. You can check each *Subdomain* at most once a minute. If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the section says when the check failed, and keeps the last known status.

## Subdomain emails

CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) about the DNS record of each *Subdomain*.

### [Subdomain] is live

CloudCannon sends this email when it finds the record for a *Subdomain* you marked as updated and issues its certificate. The email shows the DNS record and the *Site* or redirect now live on the *Subdomain*, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Mailer-Subdomain-Live" alt="A screenshot of the subdomain live email shows the headline careers.example.com is live!, a tracker with every step done, the DNS record CloudCannon found, and the Visit careers.example.com button." title="The subdomain live email" type="screenshot"/>

### [Subdomain] is still not pointing at CloudCannon

CloudCannon sends this email when it still can't find the record more than 48 hours after you clicked the *I've updated it* button. If you didn't click it, CloudCannon counts the 48 hours from when you added the *Subdomain*, or from the last time you changed its hosting or your DNS provider, whichever is later. Changing the hosting or the DNS provider starts the 48 hours again. CloudCannon only sends the email within seven days of that starting point, and only once for each starting point.

The email starts *It has been more than 48 hours since [...]*, then shows the record the *Subdomain* needs, what CloudCannon found, and what usually fixes it, with a *Check the record* button that opens the *Subdomain* page.

## Renew the wildcard certificate

The wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain* needs new TXT records at your DNS provider each time it renews, unless you add a CNAME record once. Before it expires, CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of your *Base Domain* the new TXT records, and the *HTTPS* section of the *Subdomain* page for your *Base Domain* shows them under *Other subdomains* with the date to add them by, along with the CNAME record under *Prefer to do this once?*. You can add the CNAME record at any time, not only during a renewal: while HTTPS is on and the record is missing, CloudCannon offers it under *Other subdomains* in the *HTTPS* section, and on the *DNS* tab. Once you add it, the certificate renews by itself, and you don't need to add TXT records again. For more information, please read our documentation on [renewing the wildcard certificate on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-certificate-on-external-dns).

## Choose how visitors reach a Subdomain

Each *Subdomain* on your *Custom Domain* has its own hosting option, which applies to every *Site* on that *Subdomain*. You can choose one of three options:

* **CloudCannon Hosting** — CloudCannon's global CDN serves the *Subdomain*. This is the default and recommended option. Your *Base Domain* can't point at the CDN on external DNS, so its A records point straight at CloudCannon's origin instead.
* **Cloudflare Proxy** — Your own Cloudflare zone proxies the *Subdomain* to CloudCannon's origin.
* **Single Origin** — DNS points straight at CloudCannon's origin, or through your own proxy.

For more information on *Subdomains*, please read our documentation on [what a subdomain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-subdomain/).

## Configure Cloudflare Proxy

CloudCannon hosting uses Cloudflare. If you use Cloudflare as your DNS provider and a record's proxy status is *Proxied*, you can't proxy directly to the normal CloudCannon CNAME (i.e., `sites.cloudcannon.com`).

To resolve this issue, you can either set the record's proxy status to *DNS only*, or set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy* and add a CNAME record to your DNS provider.

The *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal labels the saved option *Current*, shows the DNS record the selected option needs, and warns you if the record has to change.

To set a *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy*:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
4. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will list the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*.
6. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
7. Click the *Configure* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
8. Select the *Cloudflare Proxy* option under *Hosting*. CloudCannon will show the DNS record the *Subdomain* needs.
9. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your Cloudflare zone to proxy visitors to the CloudCannon origin.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Orange-Cloud" alt="A screenshot of the Hosting for example.com modal shows the CloudCannon Hosting, Cloudflare Proxy, and Single Origin options, with Cloudflare Proxy selected and the CNAME record it needs, and the Save hosting button." title="The Hosting modal" type="screenshot" />

To add a CNAME record:

1. Log in to Cloudflare.
2. Navigate to the DNS records for your domain.
3. Add a CNAME record pointing your *Subdomain* (e.g., docs.example.com) at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`, with the proxy status set to *Proxied*.
4. Set Cloudflare's SSL/TLS encryption mode to *Full (strict)*.
5. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.

Cloudflare will confirm that you own the *Subdomain* from the proxied record, and issue its certificate. To issue the certificate before you change your proxied record, click the *Issue the SSL certificate before you switch* button in the *HTTPS* section. For more information, please read our documentation on [issuing the certificate before you switch a proxied record](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#issue-the-certificate-before-you-switch-a-proxied-record).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  If you use *Cloudflare Proxy*, Cloudflare might cache your *Site*. In that case, your *Site* won't show any updates until you clear your Cloudflare cache.
</comp.Notice>

## Single Origin

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  CloudCannon does not recommend *Single Origin* unless you have specific setup requirements. For assistance setting up your DNS, please contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the *Custom Domain*, the *Subdomain*, your DNS provider and any proxy in front of CloudCannon, and the records shown under *Needs* and *Found* in the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
</comp.Notice>

In some cases, you may need to configure your DNS records to point directly at CloudCannon's host origin rather than `sites.cloudcannon.com`, for example when another provider proxies your *Subdomain*. *Single Origin* skips these features of *CloudCannon Hosting*:

* CloudCannon's global network, which serves your pages from locations around the world, so they load quickly everywhere.
* Certificate validation before your DNS points at CloudCannon. With *Single Origin*, CloudCannon can only validate your certificate once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin.

To point your DNS straight at the CloudCannon origin, set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Single Origin* and add DNS records to your DNS provider.

To set a *Subdomain's* hosting to *Single Origin*:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Domains Browser*.
4. Select the domain for which you want to configure hosting by clicking the *Card* for the domain. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will list the *Subdomains* on your *Custom Domain*.
6. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain*. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
7. Click the *Configure* button in the *Hosting* section. CloudCannon will open the *Hosting for [Subdomain]* modal.
8. Select the *Single Origin* option under *Hosting*. CloudCannon will show the DNS record the *Subdomain* needs.
9. Click the *Save hosting* button.

CloudCannon will expect your DNS to point straight at the CloudCannon origin.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Single-Origin" alt="A screenshot of the Hosting for example.com modal shows the CloudCannon Hosting, Cloudflare Proxy, and Single Origin options, with Single Origin selected and the DNS records it needs, and the Save hosting button." title="The Hosting modal" type="screenshot" />

The *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page lists the records under *Needs*. For a *Subdomain*, this is a CNAME record pointing at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. For your *Base Domain*, it is two A records instead.

To add the DNS records:

1. Log in to your third-party DNS provider.
2. Add the records listed under *Needs* in the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page.
3. Click the *I've updated it* button in the *DNS* section.

CloudCannon will show the *Subdomain* as *Connected* in the *DNS* section once your DNS points at the CloudCannon origin, and will answer the certificate challenge for the *Subdomain* itself.

If your own proxy sits in front of CloudCannon, have it connect to CloudCannon over HTTPS, keep the visitor's hostname in the `Host` header, and pass requests to `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` through to CloudCannon, so the certificate authority can reach the challenge. If your proxy connects over HTTP and the *Subdomain* has *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* ticked, CloudCannon answers every request with a redirect to HTTPS, which can send visitors in a redirect loop.
