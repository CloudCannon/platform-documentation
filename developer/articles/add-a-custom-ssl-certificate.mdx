<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` and `org:settings:certificate:write` permissions, can add a custom SSL certificate to a *Subdomain*.
</comp.Notice>

Managing your SSL certificate on CloudCannon requires you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon. If an external service hosts your *Site*, please review its documentation on SSL certificates.

You can purchase a custom SSL certificate from a trusted third-party provider and upload it to a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon. The certificate belongs to the *Subdomain*, so it covers every *Site* on that *Subdomain*. Each certificate you upload is saved to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so you can choose it for any *Subdomain* it covers, and the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings* lists them all.

To add a certificate, you need:

* A name your team will recognize
* Public Key
* Private Key
* Certificate Chain (optional)

Your certificate must cover the *Subdomain* you are adding it to. CloudCannon reads the expiry date from the certificate, so you do not need to enter it.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Give your private key only to the service that serves your website, such as CloudCannon. Do not write down your private key in a publicly accessible or insecure place. If you think the security of your private key has been compromised, please generate a new SSL certificate.
</comp.Notice>

CloudCannon can't renew a certificate you upload, so check its expiry date, and upload a new one before it expires.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [added a Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) to your *Site*, or [added a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) to your *Custom Domain*.

If your *Organization* already has the certificate, such as one you [uploaded from Org Settings](#upload-a-certificate-from-org-settings), select it from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown instead of the *New custom SSL certificate* option, and skip to step 15. The dropdown lists your *Organization's* uploaded certificates that haven't expired and aren't being removed.

To add a custom SSL certificate:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your *Custom Domain* is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
4. Click the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain to which you want to add a certificate. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will open the list of *Subdomains*.
6. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain* to which you want to add the certificate. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
7. Click the *Configure* button in the *HTTPS* section. CloudCannon will open the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal.
8. Select the *Use an SSL certificate you upload* option under *SSL certificate*. CloudCannon will show the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown below the options.
9. Select the *New custom SSL certificate* option from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown. CloudCannon will open the *Add SSL Certificate* modal.
10. Enter a name your team will recognize in the *Name* field.
11. Enter your certificate in the *Public Key* field.
12. Enter your private key in the *Private Key* field.
13. (Optional.) Enter your certificate chain in the *Certificate Chain (optional)* field.
14. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button.
15. (Optional.) Check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox under *HTTP*.
16. Click the *Save HTTPS* button.

CloudCannon will apply your custom SSL certificate to the selected *Subdomain*. The *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is on* and the date until which the certificate is valid. For what happens when it expires, please read the section on [expiring uploaded certificates](#when-an-uploaded-certificate-expires). If the *Subdomain* is set to use an uploaded certificate but none is selected, the *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is off*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Add-SSL-Certificate-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Add SSL Certificate modal shows the Name field, a note that the certificate must cover example.com, and the Public Key and Private Key fields." title="The Add SSL Certificate modal" type="screenshot" />

If the certificate does not cover the *Subdomain*, CloudCannon shows an error under the *Public Key* field and does not add the certificate.

To return to an auto-generated certificate, select the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. CloudCannon starts issuing its own certificate for the *Subdomain* straight away. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-hosting.yml">CloudCannon Hosting</comp.GlossaryTerm> and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, CloudCannon removes the uploaded certificate from the *Subdomain's* Cloudflare custom hostname. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  All certificates must use PEM encoding. The placeholder text indicates the correct start and end headers.
</comp.Notice>

## When an uploaded certificate expires

CloudCannon cannot renew an SSL certificate you upload. The *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings* marks the certificate *Expires soon* 30 days before it expires, and CloudCannon emails you 14 days and 3 days before it expires. If you have access to the *Health* tab on your *Organization Home* page, which is available through a private Beta granted to individual accounts rather than to an *Organization*, its *Uploaded SSL certificates are expiring soon* check lists each *Subdomain* that uses the certificate from nine days before it expires.

Before the certificate expires, upload a new one and select it for each *Subdomain* that uses it, or [remove the certificate](#remove-an-uploaded-certificate) so each *Subdomain* switches to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option while keeping HTTPS on. If a *Subdomain* still uses the certificate when it expires, CloudCannon switches that *Subdomain* to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option within the hour. Until CloudCannon issues the new certificate, visitors to the *Subdomain* may see a security warning in their browser.

### Action required: the [Certificate Name] SSL certificate expires on [Date]

CloudCannon sends this email 14 days and 3 days before an SSL certificate you uploaded expires, to everyone who can edit the settings of a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) with a *Subdomain* that still uses the certificate. The email lists those *Subdomains* and links to the page for the certificate.

## Redirect HTTP to HTTPS

Once HTTPS is on, visitors can open your *Site* on the more secure HTTPS URL. When you check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal, CloudCannon redirects anyone who opens the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/http.yml">HTTP</comp.GlossaryTerm> URL on the *Subdomain*, for every *Site* on it. For more information on HTTPS, please read our documentation on [what an SSL certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/#http-vs-https). For more information on redirects, please read our documentation on [configuring HTTP redirects](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-http-redirects/).

## Upload a certificate from Org Settings

You can upload a certificate before you choose it for a *Subdomain*, from the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*. Viewing the page requires the `org:settings:certificate:read` permission. The page lists your *Organization's* SSL certificates, with the domains each one covers and its status.

To upload a certificate from *Org Settings*:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* that has the certificate.
3. Click the *Org Settings* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Details* page.
4. Click the *SSL Certificates* link under the *Hosting* heading. CloudCannon will open the *SSL Certificates* page.
5. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add SSL Certificate* page.
6. Enter a name your team will recognize in the *Name* field.
7. Enter your certificate in the *Public Key* field.
8. Enter your private key in the *Private Key* field.
9. (Optional.) Enter your certificate chain in the *Certificate Chain (optional)* field.
10. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button.

CloudCannon will add the certificate and open its page. To use it, select it from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal of each *Subdomain* it covers.

## Review an uploaded certificate

To see which *Subdomains* use a certificate and when it expires, click the certificate on the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*. The page for the certificate shows:

* Its status, next to its name, and the date it expires.
* The domains the certificate covers. CloudCannon reads these from the certificate, so certificates uploaded before it recorded them don't show this line.
* The *Domains* list, with each *Subdomain* that serves HTTPS with the certificate, including each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Click a *Subdomain* to open its page.

The *Domains* list only shows *Subdomains* on *Base Domains* you can view.

Each certificate shows one of these statuses, on the *SSL Certificates* page and on its own page:

* *Valid* means the certificate expires in more than 30 days.
* *Expires soon* means the certificate expires within 30 days. CloudCannon cannot renew an SSL certificate you upload, so upload a new one and select it for each *Subdomain* in the *Domains* list before then.
* *Expired* means the certificate has expired. CloudCannon switches any *Subdomain* still using it to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option.
* *Renews automatically* means CloudCannon renews the certificate itself.
* *Removing* means you removed the certificate while *Subdomains* still use it. HTTPS stays on while each one switches, and CloudCannon deletes the certificate once each has its own.

## Remove an uploaded certificate

Removing a certificate deletes it from CloudCannon, and you cannot recover it. Every *Subdomain* that uses the certificate switches to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option, including *Subdomains* on *Base Domains* you can't view, which the *Domains* list doesn't show.

If no *Subdomain* uses the certificate, or it has expired, CloudCannon deletes it straight away. Otherwise, HTTPS stays on while each *Subdomain* switches to a certificate CloudCannon issues. Until then, the certificate shows the *Removing* status, and you can't select it in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. CloudCannon deletes it once every *Subdomain* has its own certificate, when it expires, or when CloudCannon stops retrying a certificate it can't issue, whichever comes first.

To remove an uploaded certificate:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* that has the certificate.
3. Click the *Org Settings* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the *Details* page.
4. Click the *SSL Certificates* link under the *Hosting* heading. CloudCannon will open the *SSL Certificates* page.
5. Click the *Card* for the certificate you want to remove. CloudCannon will open the page for that certificate.
6. Click the *Remove SSL Certificate* button in the *Remove* section. CloudCannon will open the *Remove this SSL certificate?* modal.
7. Click the *Remove SSL Certificate* button in the modal.

If no *Subdomain* uses the certificate, or it has expired, CloudCannon will delete it and return you to the *SSL Certificates* page. Otherwise, CloudCannon will mark the certificate *Removing*, and its page will list the *Subdomains* still using it until CloudCannon issues their own certificates.
