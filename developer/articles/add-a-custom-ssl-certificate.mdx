<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can change the HTTPS settings of a subdomain.
</comp.Notice>

Managing your SSL certificate on CloudCannon requires you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon. If an external service hosts your *Site*, please review its documentation on SSL certificates.

You can purchase a custom SSL certificate from a trusted third-party provider and upload it to a subdomain of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon. The certificate belongs to the subdomain, so it covers every *Site* on that subdomain.

* Name (i.e., Domain Name)
* Public Key
* Private Key
* Certificate Chain (optional)

Your certificate must cover the *Custom Domain* you are adding it to. CloudCannon reads the expiry date from the certificate, so you do not need to enter it.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Give your private key only to the service that serves your website, such as CloudCannon. Do not write down your private key in a publicly accessible or insecure place. If you think the security of your private key has been compromised, please generate a new SSL certificate.
</comp.Notice>

SSL certificates last for about a year, so renew yours every year to keep your website secure. You can renew your SSL certificate more often, but most websites do not require this level of security.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [added a Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) to your *Site*, or [added a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/manage-the-subdomains-on-your-custom-domain/#add-a-subdomain) to your *Custom Domain*.

If your *Organization* already has the certificate, select it from the certificate dropdown instead of the *New custom SSL certificate* option, and skip to step 9.

To add a custom SSL certificate:

1. Click the *Domains* button in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Select the domain to which you want to add a certificate. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Open the *Subdomains* tab, then select the subdomain to which you want to add the certificate. CloudCannon will open the page for that subdomain.
4. Click the *Configure* button in the *HTTPS* section. CloudCannon will open the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal.
5. Select the *Use an SSL certificate you upload* option under *SSL certificate*.
6. Select the *New custom SSL certificate* option from the certificate dropdown. CloudCannon will open the *Add SSL Certificate* modal.
7. Enter a name your team will recognize in the *Name* field, then enter your certificate in the *Public Key* field, your private key in the *Private Key* field, and, if you have one, your certificate chain in the *Certificate Chain (optional)* field.
8. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button.
9. (Optional.) Check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox under *HTTP*.
10. Click the *Save HTTPS* button.

CloudCannon will apply your custom SSL certificate to the selected subdomain. The *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is on* and the date until which the certificate is valid. Once it expires, the *HTTPS* section shows *The SSL certificate expired*, and you need to upload a new one. CloudCannon doesn't email you before an uploaded certificate expires, so note the date. If the subdomain is set to use an uploaded certificate but none is selected, the *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is off*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Add-SSL-Certificate-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Add SSL Certificate modal shows the Name field, a note that the certificate must cover example.com, and the Public Key and Private Key fields." title="The Add SSL Certificate modal" type="screenshot" />

If the certificate does not cover the subdomain, CloudCannon shows an error under the *Public Key* field and does not add the certificate.

To return to an auto-generated certificate, select the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. On *CloudCannon Hosting* and *Cloudflare Proxy*, CloudCannon removes the uploaded certificate from the subdomain's Cloudflare custom hostname. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  All certificates must use PEM encoding. The placeholder text indicates the correct start and end headers.
</comp.Notice>

## Redirect HTTP to HTTPS

Once HTTPS is on, visitors can open your *Site* on the more secure [HTTPS](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/#http-vs-https) URL. When you check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal, CloudCannon redirects anyone who opens the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/http.yml">HTTP</comp.GlossaryTerm> URL on the subdomain, for every *Site* on it. For more information on redirects, please read our documentation on [configuring HTTP redirects](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-http-redirects/).
