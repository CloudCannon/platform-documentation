<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can change the HTTPS settings of a subdomain. A Custom Permission Group also needs the `org:settings:certificate:write` permission to upload or remove an SSL certificate, and the `org:settings:certificate:read` permission to view the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*.
</comp.Notice>

Managing your SSL certificate on CloudCannon requires you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon. If an external service hosts your *Site*, please review its documentation on SSL certificates.

You can purchase a custom SSL certificate from a trusted third-party provider and upload it to a subdomain of your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> on CloudCannon. The certificate belongs to the subdomain, so it covers every *Site* on that subdomain. Each certificate you upload is saved to your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so you can choose it for any subdomain it covers, and the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings* lists them all.

* Name (i.e., Domain Name)
* Public Key
* Private Key
* Certificate Chain (optional)

Your certificate must cover the *Custom Domain* you are adding it to. CloudCannon reads the expiry date from the certificate, so you do not need to enter it.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Give your private key only to the service that serves your website, such as CloudCannon. Do not write down your private key in a publicly accessible or insecure place. If you think the security of your private key has been compromised, please generate a new SSL certificate.
</comp.Notice>

SSL certificates last for about a year, so renew yours every year to keep your website secure. You can renew your SSL certificate more often, but most websites do not require this level of security.

Before we begin, this article assumes you have [added a Custom Domain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/) to your *Site*, or [added a subdomain](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-subdomain-to-your-custom-domain/) to your *Custom Domain*.

If your *Organization* already has the certificate, such as one you [uploaded from Org Settings](#upload-a-certificate-from-org-settings), select it from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown instead of the *New custom SSL certificate* option, and skip to step 10. The dropdown lists your *Organization's* uploaded certificates that haven't expired and aren't being removed.

To add a custom SSL certificate:

1. Click the *Domains* link in the *App Sidebar*. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
2. Click the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain to which you want to add a certificate. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
3. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will open the list of subdomains.
4. Click the subdomain to which you want to add the certificate. CloudCannon will open the page for that subdomain.
5. Click the *Configure* button in the *HTTPS* section. CloudCannon will open the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal.
6. Select the *Use an SSL certificate you upload* option under *SSL certificate*. CloudCannon will show the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown below the options.
7. Select the *New custom SSL certificate* option from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown. CloudCannon will open the *Add SSL Certificate* modal.
8. Enter a name your team will recognize in the *Name* field, then enter your certificate in the *Public Key* field, your private key in the *Private Key* field, and, if you have one, your certificate chain in the *Certificate Chain (optional)* field.
9. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button.
10. (Optional.) Check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox under *HTTP*.
11. Click the *Save HTTPS* button.

CloudCannon will apply your custom SSL certificate to the selected subdomain. The *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is on* and the date until which the certificate is valid. For what happens when it expires, please read the section on [expiring uploaded certificates](#when-an-uploaded-certificate-expires). If the subdomain is set to use an uploaded certificate but none is selected, the *HTTPS* section shows *HTTPS is off*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Custom-Domain-Add-SSL-Certificate-Modal" alt="A screenshot of the Add SSL Certificate modal shows the Name field, a note that the certificate must cover example.com, and the Public Key and Private Key fields." title="The Add SSL Certificate modal" type="screenshot" />

If the certificate does not cover the subdomain, CloudCannon shows an error under the *Public Key* field and does not add the certificate.

To return to an auto-generated certificate, select the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. CloudCannon starts issuing its own certificate for the subdomain straight away. On *CloudCannon Hosting* and *Cloudflare Proxy*, CloudCannon removes the uploaded certificate from the subdomain's Cloudflare custom hostname. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding an auto-generated SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/).

  All certificates must use PEM encoding. The placeholder text indicates the correct start and end headers.
</comp.Notice>

## When an uploaded certificate expires

CloudCannon cannot renew an SSL certificate you upload. The *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings* marks the certificate *Expires soon* 30 days before it expires, and CloudCannon emails you 14 days and three days before it expires. If you have access to the *Health* tab on your *Organization Home* page, which is available through a private Beta, its *Uploaded SSL certificates are expiring soon* check lists each subdomain that uses the certificate from nine days before it expires.

Before the certificate expires, upload a new one and select it for each subdomain that uses it, or [remove the certificate](#remove-an-uploaded-certificate) so each subdomain switches to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option while keeping HTTPS on. If a subdomain still uses the certificate when it expires, CloudCannon switches that subdomain to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option within the hour. Until CloudCannon issues the new certificate, visitors to the subdomain may see a security warning in their browser.

### Action required: the [Certificate Name] SSL certificate expires on [Date]

CloudCannon sends this email 14 days and three days before an SSL certificate you uploaded expires, to everyone who can edit the settings of a *Base Domain* (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) with a subdomain that still uses the certificate. The email lists those subdomains and links to the page for the certificate.

## Redirect HTTP to HTTPS

Once HTTPS is on, visitors can open your *Site* on the more secure [HTTPS](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/#http-vs-https) URL. When you check the *Redirect http:// visitors to https://* checkbox in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal, CloudCannon redirects anyone who opens the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/http.yml">HTTP</comp.GlossaryTerm> URL on the subdomain, for every *Site* on it. For more information on redirects, please read our documentation on [configuring HTTP redirects](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-http-redirects/).

## Upload a certificate from Org Settings

You can upload a certificate before you choose it for a subdomain, from the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*. The page lists your *Organization's* SSL certificates, with the domains each one covers and its status.

To upload a certificate from *Org Settings*:

1. Navigate to the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*.
2. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button. CloudCannon will open the *Add SSL Certificate* page.
3. Enter a name your team will recognize in the *Name* field, then enter your certificate in the *Public Key* field, your private key in the *Private Key* field, and, if you have one, your certificate chain in the *Certificate Chain (optional)* field.
4. Click the *Add SSL Certificate* button.

CloudCannon will add the certificate and open its page. To use it, select it from the *Uploaded SSL certificate* dropdown in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal of each subdomain it covers.

## Review an uploaded certificate

To see which subdomains use a certificate and when it expires, click the certificate on the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*. The page for the certificate shows:

* Its status, next to its name, and the date it expires.
* The domains the certificate covers. CloudCannon reads these from the certificate, so certificates uploaded before it recorded them don't show this line.
* The *Domains* list, with each subdomain that serves HTTPS with the certificate, including redirect subdomains. Click a subdomain to open its page.

The *Domains* list only shows subdomains on *Base Domains* you can view.

Each certificate shows one of these statuses, on the *SSL Certificates* page and on its own page:

* *Valid* means the certificate expires in more than 30 days.
* *Expires soon* means the certificate expires within 30 days. CloudCannon cannot renew an SSL certificate you upload, so upload a new one and select it for each subdomain in the *Domains* list before then.
* *Expired* means the certificate has expired. CloudCannon switches any subdomain still using it to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option.
* *Renews automatically* means CloudCannon renews the certificate itself.
* *Removing* means you removed the certificate while subdomains still use it. Each one keeps serving it until CloudCannon issues that subdomain's own certificate, and CloudCannon then deletes it.

## Remove an uploaded certificate

Removing a certificate deletes it from CloudCannon, and you cannot recover it. Every subdomain that uses the certificate switches to the *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* option, including subdomains on *Base Domains* you can't view, which the *Domains* list doesn't show.

If no subdomain uses the certificate, or it has expired, CloudCannon deletes it straight away. Otherwise, each subdomain keeps serving the certificate until CloudCannon issues that subdomain's own certificate, so HTTPS stays on in the meantime. Until then, the certificate shows the *Removing* status, and you can't select it in the *HTTPS for [Subdomain]* modal. CloudCannon deletes it once every subdomain has its own certificate, when it expires, or when CloudCannon stops retrying a certificate it can't issue, whichever comes first.

To remove an uploaded certificate:

1. Navigate to the *SSL Certificates* page under *Org Settings*.
2. Click the *Card* for the certificate you want to remove. CloudCannon will open the page for that certificate.
3. Click the *Remove SSL Certificate* button in the *Remove* section. CloudCannon will open the *Remove this SSL certificate?* modal.
4. Click the *Remove SSL Certificate* button in the modal.

If no subdomain uses the certificate, or it has expired, CloudCannon will delete it and return you to the *SSL Certificates* page. Otherwise, CloudCannon will mark the certificate *Removing*, and its page will list the subdomains still serving it until CloudCannon issues their own certificates.
