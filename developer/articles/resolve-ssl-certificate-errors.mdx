<comp.Notice info_type="permissions">
  **Permissions required**

  Members of the *Owners*, *Developers*, and *Partner* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/), or [Custom Permission Groups](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-custom-permission-groups/) with the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission, can change the HTTPS settings of a *Subdomain* and retry its SSL certificate.
</comp.Notice>

Managing your SSL certificate on CloudCannon requires you to host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon. If an external service hosts your *Site*, please review its documentation on SSL certificates.

CloudCannon issues a free auto-generated SSL certificate for each <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> you host on CloudCannon. Most certificates issue within a few minutes of the *Subdomain's* DNS pointing at CloudCannon, but a certificate can stay waiting, or fail to issue or renew, when the *Subdomain's* DNS isn't set up correctly.

Most SSL certificate errors come down to <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns.yml">DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>: the certificate authority must confirm that your *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon before it can issue a certificate.

## SSL certificate emails

CloudCannon emails everyone who can edit the settings of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/base-domain.yml">Base Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> (the `base-domain:settings:details:write` permission) when a certificate keeps failing to issue, and again once it issues.

### [Action Required] [Subdomain] is failing to generate an SSL certificate

CloudCannon sends this email once a certificate has failed to issue 23 or more times, and only while the *Subdomain's* DNS is *Connected*. The email says how many attempts failed and what usually fixes it, with a *See the last error* button. The *HTTPS* section of the *Subdomain* page shows the last error and a *Retry now* button.

### [Subdomain] has a new SSL certificate

CloudCannon sends this email when a certificate issues after CloudCannon sent the failing email for it. The email shows the active certificate and what the *Subdomain* serves, with a *Visit [Subdomain]* button.

## Check your SSL certificate status

The *HTTPS* section of the *Subdomain* page explains the state of the certificate, and the *HTTPS* column of the *Subdomains* tab shows a short status for every *Subdomain*. The *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page also names the *Subdomains* with certificate problems on the *[Number] of [Number] subdomains need attention* *Card*. Click the *Card* to see those *Subdomains* on the *Subdomains* tab.

If your account has the *Health* tab on the *Organization Home* page, it lists the *Subdomains* across your *Organization* whose SSL certificate has expired, and those whose certificate failed to renew and expires within seven days, with a link to each *Subdomain* page. The *Health* tab is available through a private Beta, which CloudCannon enables for individual accounts. To request access, please contact our friendly <a href="mailto:support@cloudcannon.com?subject=A%20question%20about%20health%20checks">support team</a>.

The *HTTPS* section shows one of these headings:

* **Waiting for DNS** — The *Subdomain's* DNS record doesn't point at CloudCannon yet. HTTPS turns on by itself once it does. On <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudflare-proxy.yml">Cloudflare Proxy</comp.GlossaryTerm>, the heading is *Waiting for your proxied record* until an attempt to issue the certificate fails. If an attempt to issue the certificate has already failed, the section shows which attempt failed, why, and when CloudCannon tries again.
* **Waiting for nameservers** — Your domain uses CloudCannon DNS, and its nameservers don't point at CloudCannon DNS yet. HTTPS turns on by itself once they do. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/).
* **Turning HTTPS on** — CloudCannon is requesting the certificate. This usually takes a few minutes. If an attempt has already failed for a reason other than DNS, the section shows which attempt failed, why, and when CloudCannon tries again.
* **HTTPS is on** — The certificate is active, and the section shows the date by which CloudCannon renews it. For an uploaded certificate, it shows the date until which the certificate is valid instead. If an attempt to renew the certificate CloudCannon keeps for its origin servers has failed, a note says so. Visitors are not affected.
* **Renewing the SSL certificate** — CloudCannon is renewing the certificate, or a renewal attempt failed and CloudCannon is trying again. The current certificate is valid until the date shown, so visitors are not affected. If an attempt failed, the section says why, and when CloudCannon tries again or that it has stopped.
* **The SSL certificate expires in [Number] days** — Renewal is failing, and the current certificate expires within seven days. On the last day, the heading reads *The SSL certificate expires today*. Fix the problem before the date shown, or visitors see a security warning.
* **The SSL certificate expired** — Visitors who open https:// see a security warning. The section says what brings HTTPS back: a successful renewal attempt, the *Subdomain* pointing at CloudCannon again, clicking the *Retry now* button, or uploading a new certificate.
* **HTTPS could not be turned on** — CloudCannon stopped after 40 failed attempts. Fix the problem, then click the *Retry now* button. If the problem was the *Subdomain's* DNS record, CloudCannon also starts trying again on its own once the record points at CloudCannon.
* **HTTPS setup timed out** — Cloudflare's request for the certificate timed out. Check that the *DNS* section shows *Connected*, then click the *Retry now* button.
* **[Subdomain] stopped pointing at CloudCannon** — Cloudflare found that the *Subdomain's* DNS record no longer points at CloudCannon, so HTTPS is off until it does. Change the record at your DNS provider back to the record shown in the *DNS* section, then click the *Retry now* button.
* **Cloudflare is blocking [Subdomain]** — A zone hold on your *Base Domain* in another Cloudflare account blocks HTTPS for the *Subdomain*. A zone hold is a Cloudflare setting that stops any other Cloudflare account, including CloudCannon's, from serving the domain. Release the zone hold in that account, then click the *Retry now* button. If you don't have access to that account, contact our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>.
* **CloudCannon could not apply the HTTPS settings** — Cloudflare rejected the HTTPS settings, for example because an uploaded certificate has expired. Click the *Retry now* button, or click the *Configure* button and choose another certificate.
* **HTTPS is off** — The *Subdomain* is set to use an uploaded certificate, but none is selected. Click the *Configure* button, then choose a certificate or let CloudCannon issue one. If an uploaded certificate expires while a *Subdomain* still uses it, CloudCannon switches that *Subdomain* to *Let CloudCannon issue and renew it* within the hour. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding a custom SSL certificate](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-ssl-certificate/).
* **Setting HTTPS up again** — CloudCannon is changing the certificate. This usually takes a few minutes, and there is nothing to do.
* **Updating the SSL certificate** — CloudCannon is changing the certificate. This usually takes a few minutes, and there is nothing to do.

Until HTTPS is on, visitors who open the https:// address of the *Subdomain* see a security warning in their browser.

To check your SSL certificate status:

1. Log in to CloudCannon.
2. Select the *Organization* your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> is connected to.
3. Click the *Domains* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon will open the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domains-browser.yml">Domains Browser</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
4. Click the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm> for the domain you want to check. CloudCannon will open the *Summary* tab of the *Domain* page.
5. Click the *Subdomains* tab. CloudCannon will open the list of *Subdomains*.
6. Click the *Card* for the *Subdomain* you want to check. CloudCannon will open the page for that *Subdomain*.
7. Review the status in the *HTTPS* section.
8. (Optional.) Click the *See the last error* button. CloudCannon will open the *HTTPS problem on [Subdomain]* modal.

CloudCannon will show the current state of the certificate, and the modal explains the latest failure.

The *HTTPS problem on [Subdomain]* modal shows which attempt failed (e.g., *Attempt 2 of 40*), when it failed, when the next automatic attempt is, and the error code to include if you contact support, with a *Copy* icon. Below those details, it explains what went wrong and what to change. When the problem is DNS, the *View DNS checks* link opens the history of CloudCannon's DNS checks for the *Subdomain*. Click the *Retry now* button in the modal to try again immediately.

## Check your DNS

The *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page says whether the *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon, and shows the record it *Needs*, the records CloudCannon *Found*, and its *Status*: *Connected*, *Not pointing at CloudCannon*, *No DNS record*, *Not checked yet*, or, in the 48 hours after you add the *Subdomain*, change its hosting, change your DNS provider, or mark its record as updated, *Waiting for DNS*. The footer of the table says how often CloudCannon checks and when it last checked. Click the *Check now* button in the footer to run a new check, or the *View DNS check history* icon to open the *DNS checks for [Subdomain]* modal, which lists earlier checks, including the address the *Subdomain* resolved to and whether a test request reached CloudCannon. You can check the DNS for each *Subdomain* at most once a minute: for a minute after a check, the *Check now* button is disabled, with the tooltip *Checked less than a minute ago. You can check again in a minute.* If CloudCannon couldn't complete the latest check, the footer says so and the table keeps the last known status.

To check your domain's DNS from outside CloudCannon, you can use a public tool such as <a href="https://dnsviz.net/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">DNSViz</a>. Enter the subdomain to see which nameservers answer for it, the records they return, and any DNSSEC errors. DNSViz shows errors in red.

The *Hosting* section shows how visitors reach the *Subdomain* as a diagram. Click a connection in the diagram to see whether it works, what CloudCannon found, and what to change. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring a Subdomain's hosting, HTTPS, and DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-a-subdomains-hosting-https-and-dns/).

## Resolve a certificate that is waiting for DNS

A certificate stays waiting until the certificate authority can confirm that your *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon. Once the DNS record is correct, the certificate usually issues within a few minutes. If you added a *Custom Domain* or changed a DNS record a short time ago, the certificate stays waiting until the record spreads. Most DNS providers publish new records within an hour, and some take up to 48 hours.

If the *DNS* section doesn't show *Connected*, add the record listed under *Needs* at your DNS provider when your domain uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>, or click the *Fix* button when it uses <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/) and [configuring CloudCannon DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-cloudcannon-dns/).

To resolve a certificate that is waiting for DNS:

1. Check that the *DNS* section of the *Subdomain* page shows *Connected*.
2. Add any TXT records listed in the *HTTPS* section at your DNS provider.
3. Wait for your DNS records to spread.

CloudCannon will validate the *Subdomain* and issue the certificate once it finds the record.

For more information, please read our documentation on [getting HTTPS ready before you switch DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#get-https-ready-before-you-switch-dns) and [renewing the wildcard certificate on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-certificate-on-external-dns).

## Resolve a certificate that failed to issue

When an attempt to issue or renew a certificate fails, CloudCannon shows the reason in the *HTTPS* section and tries again later, up to 40 times.

To resolve a certificate that failed to issue:

1. Click the *See the last error* button in the *HTTPS* section to identify the cause.
2. Correct your DNS configuration based on the cause.
3. Click the *Retry now* button to try again without waiting for the next attempt.

CloudCannon will attempt to issue the certificate again. If the certificate still fails after you have corrected your DNS configuration, contact our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>.

For more information, please read the section on [common causes of SSL certificate errors](#common-causes-of-ssl-certificate-errors).

### What the last error means

The *HTTPS problem on [Subdomain]* modal shows one of these explanations:

* **CloudCannon could not reach [Subdomain], so it did not request an SSL certificate** — The *Subdomain's* DNS doesn't point at CloudCannon yet. Check the record in the *DNS* section, and any proxy or CDN in front of the *Subdomain*.
* **The certificate authority could not confirm that CloudCannon serves [Subdomain]** — The certificate authority's check did not reach CloudCannon. Check that the DNS record for the *Subdomain* points at CloudCannon, and that no proxy or firewall in front of it blocks the check. A DNSSEC error can also cause this.
* **A CAA record on [Subdomain] or a parent domain does not allow CloudCannon's certificate authority to issue SSL certificates** — Add a CAA record that allows `sectigo.com` and the certificate authorities Cloudflare uses, or remove the CAA records. For more information, please read the section on [common causes of SSL certificate errors](#common-causes-of-ssl-certificate-errors).
* **[Subdomain] is not a valid domain name for an SSL certificate** — Check that the *Subdomain* is spelled correctly.
* **The certificate authority did not accept the request for an SSL certificate** — The rejection is usually temporary, and the next attempt often succeeds.
* **The previous request for an SSL certificate expired before it finished** — CloudCannon starts a new request automatically.
* **The SSL certificate was issued, but it did not finish installing** — CloudCannon tries again automatically, and the next attempt usually succeeds.
* **A temporary problem at CloudCannon interrupted the request for an SSL certificate** — CloudCannon tries again automatically, and the next attempt usually succeeds. If the error keeps appearing, contact support.
* **CloudCannon does not issue SSL certificates while the Organization's subscription is suspended** — Update the payment details on the *Subscription* page under *Org Settings*, and CloudCannon tries again automatically.
* **The request for an SSL certificate failed with an error CloudCannon does not recognize** — Contact support with the error code shown in the modal.

## Common causes of SSL certificate errors

* **Missing or incorrect DNS records** — Your *Subdomain* must point at CloudCannon. On external DNS, add the record CloudCannon shows for the *Subdomain's* hosting option. On CloudCannon DNS, CloudCannon manages these records for you, and the *Fix* button repairs a record that points somewhere else.
* **Missing TXT records** — If you use an external DNS provider and the *HTTPS* section lists TXT records, you must add them at your DNS provider. The wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain* needs new TXT records at each renewal, unless you add the CNAME record that lets Cloudflare renew it on its own. For more information, please read our documentation on [renewing the wildcard certificate on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#renew-the-wildcard-certificate-on-external-dns).
* **DNS records have not spread yet** — Newly added or changed DNS records usually take effect within an hour, and can take up to 48 hours with some DNS providers.
* **A CAA record blocks the certificate authority** — A CAA record is a DNS record that restricts which certificate authorities are allowed to issue certificates for your domain. A CAA record on your *Base Domain* also applies to its subdomains. If your domain has CAA records that don't include the authorities CloudCannon uses, CloudCannon doesn't request a certificate, and the *HTTPS problem on [Subdomain]* modal says that a CAA record does not allow CloudCannon's certificate authority. CloudCannon checks the CAA records on the subdomain first, then on each parent domain, and uses the closest ones it finds. To fix this, remove your CAA records, or add CAA records that allow `sectigo.com`, and `letsencrypt.org`, `pki.goog`, and `ssl.com` (for certificates from Cloudflare). A CAA record that allows one authority looks like `0 issue "sectigo.com"`, and you add one record for each authority. For more information, please read Cloudflare's documentation on <a href="https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/reference/certificate-authorities/#caa-records" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CAA records for its certificate authorities</a>.
* **DNSSEC is misconfigured** — If DNSSEC is turned on for your domain at your registrar, but your DNS provider doesn't sign your records with matching keys, resolvers and certificate authorities reject your DNS answers. A DNSSEC mismatch often happens after you change DNS provider without removing or updating the DS record at your registrar, which is the record that links your domain to your DNS provider's DNSSEC keys. Check your domain with <a href="https://dnsviz.net/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">DNSViz</a>. If it shows DNSSEC errors, turn DNSSEC off at your registrar, or update the DS record to match your DNS provider. CloudCannon DNS doesn't support DNSSEC, so turn DNSSEC off at your registrar before you switch to CloudCannon DNS.
* **Another platform still has the subdomain** — If you are moving the subdomain from another platform that also serves websites through Cloudflare (e.g., Webflow), Cloudflare can keep sending visitors to that platform until the certificate on CloudCannon is active. For more information, please read our documentation on [moving a subdomain from another platform that uses Cloudflare](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#move-a-subdomain-from-another-platform-that-uses-cloudflare).
* **The hosting option does not match your proxy** — If your own Cloudflare zone proxies the *Subdomain*, or another provider proxies it to CloudCannon, set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy* or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/single-origin.yml">Single Origin</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring Cloudflare Proxy](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#configure-cloudflare-proxy).
* **A zone hold in another Cloudflare account** — If your *Base Domain* is in another Cloudflare account with a zone hold, such as an account that belonged to a previous agency or platform, Cloudflare blocks CloudCannon from serving its subdomains, and the *HTTPS* section shows *Cloudflare is blocking [Subdomain]*. Release the zone hold in that account, then click the *Retry now* button. If you don't have access to that account, contact our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>.

## Resolve an error page from Cloudflare

CloudCannon serves your *Sites* through Cloudflare, so a problem with a *Subdomain's* DNS or certificate can show visitors an error page from Cloudflare instead of your *Site* or your fallback redirect. The most common causes are:

* **The subdomain isn't set up on CloudCannon, and your domain uses external DNS** — Visitors to a subdomain you haven't added, such as www.example.com, rely on the wildcard certificate for your *Base Domain*, which needs TXT records at your DNS provider at every renewal. Add the *Subdomain* on CloudCannon instead, as a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/r/redirect-subdomain.yml">Redirect Subdomain</comp.GlossaryTerm> if it should send visitors elsewhere. For more information, please read our documentation on [SSL certificates on external DNS](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/#ssl-certificates-on-external-dns).
* **Your own Cloudflare zone proxies the subdomain** — Set the *Subdomain's* hosting to *Cloudflare Proxy*, and point the record at `orange-cloud.cloudcannon.com`. For more information, please read our documentation on [configuring Cloudflare Proxy](/documentation/developer-articles/configure-external-dns/#configure-cloudflare-proxy).
* **Another platform that uses Cloudflare still has the subdomain** — For more information, please read our documentation on [moving a subdomain from another platform that uses Cloudflare](/documentation/developer-articles/add-an-auto-generated-ssl-certificate/#move-a-subdomain-from-another-platform-that-uses-cloudflare).

## Contact support

If your certificate still fails after you have corrected your DNS configuration and waited for your records to spread, contact our friendly <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/support/">support team</a>. Include the following details so the support team can resolve your issue quickly:

* The name of the affected *Site*
* The *Subdomain* you are trying to secure
* The error code from the *HTTPS problem on [Subdomain]* modal, or a screenshot of the error page from Cloudflare, including its error code
* Your DNS provider, and whether you use CloudCannon DNS or an external provider
* The hosting option for the *Subdomain*
* When you added the *Custom Domain* or last changed your DNS records
