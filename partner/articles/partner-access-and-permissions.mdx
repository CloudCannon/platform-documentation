When you run a client's work in a [Client Organization](/documentation/partner-articles/what-is-a-client-organization/), your team reaches it through a dedicated *Permission Group* and normal *Organization* membership. This article explains who has access, who owns the *Client Organization*, and what the client can see and audit.

## The Partner Permission Group

When a *Client Organization* is linked to your Partner *Organization*, CloudCannon adds a *Partner* <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permission-group.yml">Permission Group</comp.GlossaryTerm> to it, containing the *Owners* of your Partner *Organization*. This group gives those *Owners* broad management access across the *Client Organization*.

The *Partner* group always mirrors your Partner *Organization*'s *Owners*. Add someone to your Partner *Organization*'s *Owners* group and they're added to the *Partner* group of every *Client Organization* you manage; remove them and they're removed everywhere. The group is managed entirely through this mirror, so unlike a custom *Permission Group* it isn't edited or deleted from the *Client Organization*'s *Team* page.

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  Because the *Partner* group mirrors your Partner *Organization*'s *Owners*, making someone an *Owner* of your Partner *Organization* gives them management access to *every* *Client Organization* you manage, and removing them takes it away everywhere. Keep your Partner *Organization*'s *Owners* group tight for this reason.
</comp.Notice>

The *Partner* group's access also lets you view a *Client Organization* as one of its *Team Members*, to preview what they can see and do before you hand the *Organization* over.

## Give the rest of your team access

The *Partner* group only ever holds your Partner *Organization*'s *Owners*. To let other team members work in a *Client Organization* without making them *Owners*, add them as members the same way you add anyone to an *Organization*, into whichever *Permission Group* suits their role.

Because every member of your Partner *Organization* is part of your team, they don't count toward the *Client Organization*'s seat limit, whichever group you add them to. This is what lets your team come for free, as described in our documentation on [managing your Client Organizations](/documentation/partner-articles/manage-your-client-organizations/).

## Who owns a new Client Organization

The person who creates a *Client Organization* is added to its *Owners* *Permission Group*, so they hold full owner access, and they're in the *Partner* group as well. Because only a Partner *Organization* *Owner* can create a *Client Organization*, other *Owners* on your team get management access through the *Partner* group too.

The practical consequence is that at the start, your Partner *Organization*, not the client, owns the *Client Organization*. When it's time to give the client control, you hand it over by adding them to the *Owners* group, as covered in our documentation on [handing over or detaching a Client Organization](/documentation/partner-articles/hand-over-or-detach-a-client-organization/).

## What the client can see

Partner access is visible to the client, not hidden. The *Partner* group and its members appear on the *Client Organization*'s *Team* page like any other group, and partner members are marked as not counting toward the *Client Organization*'s billing. A client can see who from your agency can reach their *Organization*.

The client can't remove the *Partner* group or its members from the *Team* page, because partner access is tied to the partner relationship rather than managed member by member. Access is removed by detaching the *Client Organization* (see below), which removes the *Partner* group.

## Auditing what a partner did

Actions taken in a *Client Organization*, such as commits, publishes, and settings changes, appear in its activity feed, attributed to the person who performed them, including your team members. The feed is available per *Site* on the *Site* dashboard and aggregated across the *Organization* on *Organization Home*. This is the general activity feed rather than a dedicated partner audit trail, but it gives a client an actor-attributed record of what happened in their *Organization*.

## Removing partner access

There are two related steps, depending on how far you want to go:

* **Handover** — keeps the *Client Organization* linked to your Partner *Organization* but gives the client control, by adding them to the *Owners* group. You can then step back from day-to-day management.
* **Detaching** — fully separates the *Client Organization* from your Partner *Organization*. It removes the *Partner* group and ends the free-team and partner-billing arrangement. Detaching isn't a self-serve action in the app today, so coordinate it with CloudCannon support.

Both are covered in our documentation on [handing over or detaching a Client Organization](/documentation/partner-articles/hand-over-or-detach-a-client-organization/).
