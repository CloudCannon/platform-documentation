A client's website is represented in CloudCannon as a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Rather than a one-to-one relationship, a CloudCannon *Site* is one version of a website, allowing you to keep live, staging, and draft versions of the same website. When multiple *Sites* are connected to a branch of the same <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm>, you can organize them into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/project.yml">Project</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

Each client might account for a single *Site* or several, requiring different features and resources. These factors determine where you should store you client content.

CloudCannon gives you two places to keep your clients' *Sites*:

* A separate <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> linked to yours, which your agency manages on their behalf.
* Your own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, alongside the rest of your work, with your clients given access to their own content.

CloudCannon has no preference between the two, and you don't have to use the same one for every client. Most agencies run both at once, and you can move a client from one to the other later, which we'll cover further on in this guide.

To use a *Client Organization*, you need to be a member of CloudCannon's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-program.yml">Partner Program</comp.GlossaryTerm>; keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* does not. Membership is free, and joining turns your *Organization* into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-organization.yml">Partner Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which adds specialized features, increasing discounts on *Subscription Plans*, and an extended *Free Trial* for *Client Organizations* you manage. We'll cover the *Partner Program* in the next step of this guide.

## A Client Organization for each client

A *Client Organization* is a separate *Organization* that you manage on behalf of a client, with its own *Subscription Plan* and invoices, *Organization Members*, *Projects*, *Sites*, *Custom Domains*, *Build Time*, and *Hosting Bandwidth*. Additionally, you can customize the branding for each CloudCannon *Organization*, allowing you to make the *Client Organization* feel like theirs.

In most cases, *Organizations* are entirely independent from one another. However, a *Client Organization* is always linked to a *Partner Organization*. You can have as many *Client Organizations* as you require.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Client-Organization-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Client Organizations Browser in a Partner Organization shows four Client Organizations as cards, each listing its Sites and domains counts, Subscription Plan, and issue status." title="The Client Organizations Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because each *Client Organization* has its own *Subscription Plan* and payment details, you can choose who receives the invoice. Your client can add their own credit card and receive their own invoices, or you can add yours and fold the cost into your existing billing structure.

*Client Organizations* suit clients with their own budgets and content creators. A separate *Subscription Plan* means they have their own limits for *Build Time*, *Hosting Bandwidth*, *Custom Domains*, and *Seats*, so a client with heavy traffic, or one that needs constant work from your agency, never draws down the limits you rely on for everyone else, and the people they invite land on their invoice rather than yours. Your own team is the exception: everyone in your *Partner Organization* can work in every *Client Organization* you manage without using one of your client's *Seats*.

Access to a *Client Organization* works the same way as access to any other *Organization* in CloudCannon. A *Permission Group* defines a set of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permissions.yml">Permissions</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each of its members. Every *Organization Member* in an *Organization* must be a member of at least one *Permission Group*.

When you create a *Client Organization*, CloudCannon adds a dedicated *Partner Permission Group* to it that mirrors the membership of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/owners.yml">Owners Permission Group</comp.GlossaryTerm> in your *Partner Organization*. This group has full *Permissions*, so *Owners* can reach the settings, content, and *Organization Members* for every *Client Organization*, and give other members of your agency access by adding them to other *Permission Groups* in each *Client Organization*.

We'll cover how your clients access their content in the next step of this guide.

## Keep client Sites in your own Organization

You can have as many *Sites* and *Projects* in your own *Organization* as you require, which can be your agency's marketing website, or the content of your clients. Storing client work on your own *Organization* means they come under a single *Subscription Plan* and draw from the same pool of *Seats*, *Custom Domains*, *Build Time*, and *Hosting Bandwidth*.

You can organize your clients' *Sites* into one *Project* for each *Git Repository*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Projects-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Projects Browser shows a Project card for each client, with the Project's name, description, and status." title="The Projects Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because every client you manage this way comes under your *Organization's* *Subscription Plan*, you always receive the CloudCannon invoice. You can fold the cost into your existing billing structure alongside maintenance or other services (e.g., monthly development or content creation hours), so your client receives a single invoice from you rather than one from you and one from CloudCannon.

Keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* suits smaller clients, or clients with light traffic or development needs. Your agency team members will automatically have access to all your client work, with what they can access determined by the *Permission Groups* they belong to.

This method is also the cheapest way to set up your agency on CloudCannon, using the *Standard Plan*, or the *Lite Plan* available through the *Partner Program*. We'll look at what a minimal agency setup looks like further on in this guide.
