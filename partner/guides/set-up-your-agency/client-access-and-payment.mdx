Your client's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm> are built, so the last thing they need is a way in. In a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, that is an invitation to become a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/team-member.yml">Team Member</comp.GlossaryTerm> of their own <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>. If you kept their *Sites* in your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-organization.yml">Partner Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> instead, it is <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-sharing.yml">Site Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm> or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-sharing.yml">Client Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm> on a single *Site*.

## Decide how much control your client gets

Inviting your client means choosing which <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permission-group.yml">Permission Groups</comp.GlossaryTerm> they belong to. There are three useful shapes, from the most access to the least:

* **Owners** — Your client can do everything you can, including managing *Team Members*, changing *Organization* settings, and deleting the *Organization*. This is a handover.
* **Editors and Billing** — Your client can edit and publish content and manage their own billing, but cannot change *Site* or *Organization* settings, or manage the team. This is what most partners want, because it lets the client pay and work without ending your management of the *Organization*.
* **Billing on its own** — Your client can pay and nothing else. Use this when someone at the client handles invoices but never touches content.

A person can belong to more than one *Permission Group*, so *Editors* and *Billing* together is a single invitation rather than two accounts. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Default Permission Groups are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/).

Handing over control does not remove your team. Your *Partner Organization* still reaches the *Client Organization* through the *Partner Permission Group*, and your members still do not count toward the *Seats* in your client's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [how Partners access their Client Organizations](/documentation/partner-articles/how-do-partners-access-their-client-organizations/).

## Invite your client

Inside the *Client Organization*, navigate to the *Team* page under *Org Settings*. On the *Members* tab, click the *Add members* button, enter your client's email address, select the *Permission Groups* you decided on, and send the invitation. CloudCannon emails your client, and asks them to create an account if they do not have one. For the full walkthrough, please read our documentation on [letting your client pay for their Client Organization](/documentation/partner-articles/let-your-client-pay-for-their-client-organization/).

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Inviting your client is one of the four actions that shorten a *Client Organization's* six-month <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/f/free-trial.yml">Free Trial</comp.GlossaryTerm> to three days. The clock changes when they accept, not when you send. Invite them once their *Sites* are ready and they are ready to start paying.
</comp.Notice>

## What changes when your client pays

A *Client Organization* has its own payment method, so your client adding a card means CloudCannon bills them directly for that *Organization* and nothing else. Your *Partner Organization* keeps its own *Subscription Plan* and its own invoice.

Members of the *Billing Permission Group* can change the *Subscription Plan*, not only pay for it, so your client can upgrade or downgrade once they have that access.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Only members of the *Partner Program* see the *Lite Plan* on the *Update your Subscription Plan* page. A client who holds their own billing sees it while it is their current *Subscription Plan*, but once they move to another one, the *Lite Plan* is no longer listed for them and they cannot move back to it themselves. You still can, from inside their *Client Organization*.
</comp.Notice>

Who pays and who receives the invoices are separate choices. CloudCannon emails invoices to the address in the *Billing Email* field on the *Subscription* page under *Org Settings*, so you can send a client's invoices to them while your card covers the cost, or the other way around. For more information, please read our documentation on [adding billing contact information](/documentation/user-articles/add-billing-contact-information/) and [managing your payment methods](/documentation/user-articles/manage-your-payment-methods/).

## If your client's Sites are in your Partner Organization

Without a *Client Organization*, your client reaches a single *Site* one of two ways. *Site Sharing* invites them with their own CloudCannon account, scoped to that *Site*. *Client Sharing* gives them a password and a simplified version of CloudCannon, with no account at all. Either way the seat sits on your *Subscription Plan*, and your client cannot hold their own billing, because the *Site* belongs to your *Organization*.

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  Never turn on *Client Sharing* for someone who is already a *Team Member*. Each *Site* with *Client Sharing* enabled counts as one *Team Member* on top of the people with accounts, so the same person counts twice and the *Organization* pays for a seat nobody is using. Pick one method for each person.
</comp.Notice>

For more information, please read our documentation on [sharing a Site with Site Sharing](/documentation/user-articles/share-a-site-with-site-sharing/) and [what Client Sharing is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-client-sharing/).

## Enable Overages to avoid client downtime

Whether your client *Sites* are on a *Client Organization* or your own agency *Organization*, you can extend any *Subscription Plan* with <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/add-on.yml">Add-Ons</comp.GlossaryTerm> and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/overage.yml">Overages</comp.GlossaryTerm>. We recommend enabling *Overages* even if you don't need more resources than a *Subscription Plan* provides.

*Add-Ons* allow you to purchase additional *Seats* or *Custom Domains*, while enabling *Overages* removes any *Subscription Plan* limits on *Build Time* or *Hosting Bandwidth*, instead invoicing you for the amount an *Organization* uses each month rather than capping usage.

When you reach the limit on your *Build Time* and *Hosting Bandwidth* usage, CloudCannon will prevent more *Site* builds or take websites offline for the rest of the month. Because *Subscription Plan* resources are used by every *Site* in an *Organization*, reaching your resource limit due to activity on one *Site* will affect all *Sites* on that *Organization*. If you store multiple client *Sites* on your agency *Organization*, that means one client's activity can risk downtime for other client *Sites*.

To turn *Overages* on for an *Organization*, click the *Org Settings* link in the *App Sidebar* and open the *Subscription* page. If you are enabling *Overages* for a *Client Organization*, open that *Organization* first by clicking the *Clients* link in the *App Sidebar*, then selecting your client in the *Client Organizations Browser*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Org-Settings-Subscription-Add-Ons-Off-Warning" alt="A screenshot of the Subscription page in Org Settings shows an Overages are disabled warning, explaining that reaching a Build Time or Hosting Bandwidth limit stops changes appearing on your live Site or stops CloudCannon serving webpages, above the Enable Overages and View Subscription Plans buttons." title="The Subscription page with Overages disabled" type="screenshot"/>

While *Overages* are off, CloudCannon shows an *Overages are disabled* warning, telling you that your changes won't appear on your live *Site*, or that CloudCannon will stop serving webpages to visitors, if you reach a limit.

Click the *Enable Overages* button. CloudCannon will open the *Your Subscription Plan* page with the *Overages* toggle already switched on, so you can confirm the change before it takes effect. 

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Org-Settings-Your-Subscription-Plan-Add-Ons-Element" alt="A screenshot of the Overages section on the Your Subscription Plan page shows a toggle beside a description explaining that CloudCannon will automatically extend your Hosting Bandwidth with Overage Bundles when you reach the limit in your Subscription Plan." title="The Overages toggle" type="ui-snippet"/>

Turning *Overages* on does not charge you immediately. Instead, you are enabling CloudCannon to invoice you for usage above your *Subscription Plan* limits each month, so a charge will only appear on your invoice in a month where you go over one. Click the *Enable Overages* button to confirm.

CloudCannon will return you to the *Subscription* page, where the warning is replaced by an *Overages are enabled* confirmation.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Org-Settings-Subscription-Add-Ons-On-Element" alt="A screenshot of the Subscription page in Org Settings shows a confirmation reading Overages are enabled beside a verified icon." title="The Overages are enabled confirmation" type="ui-snippet"/>

Turning *Overages* off again takes a different route, because the confirmation carries no button. On the *Subscription* page, click the *Review your Subscription Plan* button, click the *Overages* toggle to switch it off, then click the *Disable Overages* button that appears.
