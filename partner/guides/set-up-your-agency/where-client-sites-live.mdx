Every client you onboard to CloudCannon will have website files, stored in a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm>. You can access, edit, and review these files using a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> in CloudCannon.

A *Site* in CloudCannon is one version of a website, containing all the files, content, configuration, and settings needed to edit, build, and host it. Because *Sites* sync with a branch of a *Git Repository*, your content is the same whether you change it in CloudCannon or in a local development environment.

One website can have multiple *Sites* on CloudCannon, each representing one version of it. A client's website might exist as a live *Site* their visitors reach, a staging *Site* where you review changes before they go public, and draft *Sites* for whoever is working on them. This arrangement benefits larger clients, but smaller clients may only need one CloudCannon *Site*.

Every *Site* belongs to an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> and draws from the resources that *Organization's* <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm> provides. Choosing which *Organization* a client's *Site* should belong to depends on whether they need a single *Site* or several, require different features, or need larger limits on *Subscription Plan* resources.

CloudCannon gives you two places to keep your clients' *Sites*:

* A separate <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> linked to yours, which your agency manages on their behalf.
* Your agency's *Organization*, alongside the rest of your work, with your clients given access to their own content.

CloudCannon has no preference between the two, and you don't have to use the same one for every client. You can run both at once, and move a client from one to the other later, which we'll cover further on in this guide.

Using a *Client Organization* requires you to be a member of CloudCannon's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-program.yml">Partner Program</comp.GlossaryTerm>, while keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* does not. There is no fee to apply, and joining turns your *Organization* into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-organization.yml">Partner Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>. We covered what the *Partner Program* adds in the previous step of this guide.

## Client Sites in a separate Client Organization

A *Client Organization* is a separate *Organization* that you manage on behalf of a client, with its own *Subscription Plan* and invoices, *Organization Members*, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/project.yml">Projects</comp.GlossaryTerm>, *Sites*, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domains</comp.GlossaryTerm>, <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/build-time.yml">Build Time</comp.GlossaryTerm>, and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosting-bandwidth.yml">Hosting Bandwidth</comp.GlossaryTerm>. Additionally, you can customize the branding for each CloudCannon *Organization*, allowing you to make the *Client Organization* feel like theirs.

In most cases, *Organizations* are entirely independent from one another. However, a *Client Organization* is always linked to a *Partner Organization*. You can have as many *Client Organizations* as you require, though only members of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/owners.yml">Owners Permission Group</comp.GlossaryTerm> in your *Partner Organization* can create them.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Client-Organization-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Client Organizations Browser in a Partner Organization shows four Client Organizations as cards, each listing its Sites and domains counts, Subscription Plan, and issue status." title="The Client Organizations Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because each *Client Organization* has its own *Subscription Plan* and payment details, you can choose who receives the invoice. Your client can add their own credit card and receive their own invoices, or you can add yours and fold the cost into your existing billing structure. For more information, please read our documentation on [how billing works in the Partner Program](/documentation/partner-articles/how-does-billing-work-in-the-partner-program/).

*Client Organizations* also have their own limits for *Build Time*, *Hosting Bandwidth*, *Custom Domains*, and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/seat.yml">Seats</comp.GlossaryTerm>, so a client with heavy traffic, or one that needs constant work from your agency, never draws on the resources that serve your other clients. Clients can invite their own *Organization Members*, who use *Seats* on the client's *Subscription Plan* rather than on yours, while everyone in your *Partner Organization* can work in every *Client Organization* you manage without using a *Seat*. This means your agency's own bill depends on the size of your team rather than the number of clients you take on, with each client's *Subscription Plan* sized to what they need and billed to whoever you decide. We'll look at what a minimal agency setup looks like further on in this guide.

When you create a *Client Organization*, CloudCannon adds a dedicated *Partner Permission Group* to it that mirrors the membership of the *Owners Permission Group* in your *Partner Organization*. A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permission-group.yml">Permission Group</comp.GlossaryTerm> defines a set of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permissions.yml">Permissions</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each of its members, and the *Partner Permission Group* has full *Permissions*. Your *Owners* can reach the settings, content, and *Organization Members* for every *Client Organization*, and give other members of your agency access by adding them to other *Permission Groups* in each *Client Organization*. For finding and moving between the clients you manage, please read our documentation on [what a Client Organization is](/documentation/partner-articles/what-is-a-client-organization/).

## Client Sites in your own Organization

You can have as many *Sites* and *Projects* in your own *Organization* as you require, covering your agency's own marketing website as well as your clients' content. Storing client work on your own *Organization* means those *Sites* come under a single *Subscription Plan* and draw from the same pool of *Seats*, *Custom Domains*, *Build Time*, and *Hosting Bandwidth*.

A *Project* groups the *Sites* built from one *Git Repository*, which keeps each client's live and staging *Sites* together rather than mixed in with your other clients' work. CloudCannon allows one *Project* for each *Git Repository* in an *Organization*, so a client with several websites has a *Project* for each of them.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Projects-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Projects Browser shows a Project card with the Project's name and description, its sync and build status, and its live URL." title="The Projects Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because every client you manage this way comes under your *Organization's* *Subscription Plan*, you always receive the CloudCannon invoice. You can fold the cost into your existing billing structure alongside maintenance or other services (e.g., monthly development or content creation hours), so your client receives a single invoice from you rather than one from you and one from CloudCannon.

Your agency team members will automatically have access to all your client work, with what they can access determined by the *Permission Groups* they belong to.

Because one *Subscription Plan* covers every client you store this way, taking on a new client costs you <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/add-on.yml">Add-Ons</comp.GlossaryTerm> rather than a whole new *Subscription Plan*.

## Giving your clients access to their content

Whether you store client *Sites* in a *Client Organization* or your agency's *Organization*, your clients reach their content in one of three ways:

* Join an *Organization* as a full *Organization Member*, usually of their own *Client Organization*. 
* Join an *Organization* as an *Organization Member* with access to only a single *Site* through <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-sharing.yml">Site Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
* Log in to a *Site* with <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/guest-access.yml">Guest Access</comp.GlossaryTerm> enabled using a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/guest-password.yml">Guest Password</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which needs no CloudCannon account.

These methods differ in how much you can control your clients' permissions and which *Subscription Plan* pays for the *Seat*.

You only need to use one of these methods for each client. For example, if you invite someone to be an *Organization Member* of their *Client Organization*, you do not need to also give them access via *Site Sharing*.

<comp.OptionsTable>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Organization Member" type_markdown="An entire Organization">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to an *Organization*. As an *Organization Member*, clients are members of at least one *Permission Group* which determines what actions they are allowed to take within the *Organization*.

    You can use <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/default-permission-groups.yml">Default Permission Groups</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which provide broad permission categories designed for types of users, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-permission-group.yml">Custom Permission Groups</comp.GlossaryTerm> for fine-grained permission control if the *Organization* uses the *Team Plan* or *Enterprise Plan*.

    <comp.Notice info_type="important">
      We do not recommend inviting clients as *Organization Members* of your *Partner Organization* unless you use *Custom Permission Groups*, as even the lowest level *Default Permission Group* would allow your client to see and interact with every *Site* you store there, including content that belongs to other clients.
    </comp.Notice>

    * They count as an *Organization Member* in that *Organization*, and contribute to the number of *Seats* on that *Subscription Plan*.
    * If the *Organization* is a *Client Organization*, you can choose whether your client receives and pays their own invoices directly from CloudCannon.
    * Depending on the *Permission Group* of your client, they can invite their own team to the *Organization* without your intervention. Anyone they invite contributes to the number of *Seats* on their *Subscription Plan*. We'll cover which *Permission Groups* to put your client in further on in this guide.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Organization Member via Site Sharing" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to an *Organization*, but is limited to interacting with a single *Site*. As an *Organization Member* through *Site Sharing*, clients are members of one of the following *Default Permission Groups*: *[Site Name] Editors* or *[Site Name] Technical Editors*. Only two *Default Permission Groups* are available using this method; you cannot add a client to the *Owners*, *Developers*, or *Billing Permission Groups* this way.

    This method is designed for agencies that keep their clients' content in their own *Organization*, rather than a dedicated *Client Organization*. Clients cannot see or interact with *Sites* that belong to other clients.

    If your *Organization* uses the *Team Plan* or *Enterprise Plan*, we recommend using *Custom Permission Groups* instead of *Site Sharing* for finer control over what each client can reach.

    * They count as an *Organization Member* in the *Organization* that owns the *Site*, and contribute to the number of *Seats* on that *Subscription Plan*.
    * You cannot use this method to let clients receive and pay their own invoices directly from CloudCannon.
    * You can share multiple *Sites* using this method without increasing the number of *Seats* used. One *Organization Member* uses one *Seat* on your *Subscription Plan*.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Guest Access" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client does not need a CloudCannon account. Instead, you enable the *Guest Access* feature on one *Site* and set a *Guest Password*. Anyone can log in to CloudCannon with that password and see a simplified *Site Dashboard*. You can only have one *Guest Password* per *Site*.

    * Each *Site* with *Guest Access* enabled contributes to the number of *Seats* on that *Organization's* *Subscription Plan*, regardless of how many people use the password.
    * You cannot use this method to let clients receive and pay their own invoices directly from CloudCannon.
    * You cannot separate activity on your *Site* between users if you give the *Guest Password* to multiple people. Use *Site Sharing* instead where you need to know who made a change.
    * There is no way to revoke one person's access. Changing the *Guest Password* only stops the old one working for new sign-ins, so ending every guest session means turning *Guest Access* off for that *Site*.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
</comp.OptionsTable>

## Which setup should you use?

Which way you store your clients' content and give them access depends on your agency's priorities.

Join the *Partner Program*, set up a *Client Organization*, and invite your clients as *Organization Members* if:

* A client needs features your own *Subscription Plan* does not include
* A client will use a large amount of *Build Time* or *Hosting Bandwidth*
* A client wants several of their own team editing their content
* A client wants to pay CloudCannon directly
* You want your agency's own costs to stay flat as you take on more clients

Create client *Sites* inside your own *Organization*, and invite your clients using *Site Sharing* or *Guest Access* if:

* You want to cover the cost of CloudCannon yourself
* A client only edits their content occasionally
* An engagement is small or a proof of concept

In the next step of this guide, we'll create your CloudCannon account, set up your agency's *Organization*, and join the *Partner Program*.
