As a web development agency, setting up your clients on a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cms.yml">Content Management System</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a decision about how much control each client gets over their own content, how much of your code and infrastructure they can reach, who pays for the tools that run their website, how your own team prefers to build, and whether you retain ownership over your files. As a Git-backed CMS, CloudCannon allows you to:

* Give each client as much control over their own content as you want.
* Keep each client away from your code and from your other clients' content.
* Decide how each client pays for editing and hosting, and fold that into your existing invoice structure.
* Let your developers keep building in the tools they already use, while everyone else edits the same files through CloudCannon.
* Retain ownership of your files, which stay in a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm> you control rather than a database CloudCannon owns.

When you sign up, CloudCannon will automatically create an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> for you, which acts as your home base for content, settings, and people. As an agency, that can hold your own website, your clients' websites, or both.

Each *Organization* has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which determines how much you pay for CloudCannon, what features you have access to, and what resource limits you have. CloudCannon provides a range of *Subscription Plans* designed for teams of different sizes and requirements. All the work you do on an *Organization* is billed under a single invoice.

An *Organization* also contains:

* Any number of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon *Sites* differ from websites, as you can have many *Sites* which are all copies of the same website maintained for different purposes. Copies of the same website, such as its live and draft versions, are grouped into a [Project](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-project/).
* A number of [Custom Domains](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/) (e.g., example.com or company.net). You can connect any CloudCannon *Site* to a *Custom Domain* in your *Organization* and host it live on the internet, with options for subdomains, subpaths, authentication, and more.
* A number of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/seat.yml">Seats</comp.GlossaryTerm>. You can use these *Seats* to invite [Team Members](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-team-member/) to view, edit, or publish content in your *Organization*, with [Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-permission-groups/) to determine their level of access, or enable <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-sharing.yml">Client Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm> for a specific *Site* to allow people who don't have CloudCannon accounts to log in with a password.
* An amount of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/build-time.yml">Build Time</comp.GlossaryTerm> and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosting-bandwidth.yml">Hosting Bandwidth</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which set how much you can update your *Sites* each month and how many visitors can view your live websites.

Where you keep your client content will depend on several factors, including which features your clients need, how much of a *Subscription Plan's* resource limits you expect them to use, and how you want to pay for each client. Let's take a look at the options.

## Where does your client content live in CloudCannon?





CloudCannon gives you two places to keep a client's *Sites*: in a separate <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> that you manage for them, or in your own *Organization* alongside the rest of your work. Most agencies run both at once, and you can move a client from one to the other later.

Only one of those two needs the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-program.yml">Partner Program</comp.GlossaryTerm>. *Client Organizations* are what membership unlocks, so if you want each client paying for their own *Subscription Plan*, joining is how you get there. Keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* uses features any *Organization* has, whether you join or not. Membership is free, and joining turns your *Organization* into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-organization.yml">Partner Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>. We'll do that in the next step of this guide.

### A Client Organization for each client

A *Client Organization* is a separate *Organization* with its own *Subscription Plan*, invoices, *Team Members*, branding, and *Sites*. You create it, you manage it, and your client works inside it without seeing your other clients.

The part that matters most for an agency is who pays. A *Client Organization* has its own payment method, so your client can add their own card and receive their own invoices, or you can add yours and keep covering the cost.

Most partners give each client their own *Client Organization*, because it lets the client hold their own billing while the agency keeps doing the work.

### Client Sites in your own Organization

You can also keep every client's *Sites* in your own *Organization*, grouped by *Project*. There is one *Subscription Plan*, one payment method, and one invoice, and everyone on your team can reach every client's *Sites* without being added anywhere.

Because the *Sites* sit in your *Organization*, the cost sits there too. All your clients draw on the same pool of *Build Time*, *Hosting Bandwidth*, *Custom Domains*, and *Team Members*, and any client you give access to uses one of your *Seats*. In exchange, you control the whole bill, which you can fold into how you already invoice the client alongside maintenance or other services.

CloudCannon has no preference between the two, and you don't have to use the same one for every client. For a fuller comparison, please read our documentation on [best practices for structuring client Sites](/documentation/partner-articles/best-practices-for-structuring-client-sites/).

## Where your team sits

Your own team occupies *Seats* once, in your *Organization*, and those *Seats* cover your client work.

The people you bring in from your *Partner Organization* work in a *Client Organization* for free, and never count toward the *Team Members* included in your client's *Subscription Plan*, however many clients you manage. A five-person agency pays for five *Seats* whether it runs two clients or fifty.

That exemption applies inside *Client Organizations*. Client *Sites* kept in your own *Organization* are on your *Subscription Plan* like any other *Site*, so anyone you give access to there uses one of your *Seats*.

## How your clients sign in

Whichever shape a client takes, someone on their side usually needs a way in. There are three, and they differ in how far the client's access reaches and whose *Subscription Plan* pays for it.

<comp.OptionsTable>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Client Organization" type_markdown="Their whole Organization">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to an *Organization* of their own, where they can reach every *Site* you have built for them.

    * They count as a *Team Member* in their own *Client Organization*, not in yours.
    * They can hold their own payment method and pay CloudCannon directly.
    * Your team works in the same *Client Organization* for free.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Site Sharing" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to a single *Site* in your *Organization*, with the same access as someone editing content on that *Site*.

    * They count as one *Team Member* in the *Organization* that owns the *Site*.
    * They cannot hold their own payment method, because the *Site* is on your *Subscription Plan*.
    * Each person you share with is a separate *Team Member*.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Client Sharing" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client signs in to a single *Site* with a password you set, using a simplified version of CloudCannon. No CloudCannon account is involved.

    * Each *Site* with *Client Sharing* turned on counts as one *Team Member*, however many people use the password.
    * They cannot hold their own payment method, and everyone using the password shares one identity in your *Site's* activity.
    * Changing who has access means changing the password for everyone.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
</comp.OptionsTable>

<comp.Notice info_type="important">
  <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-sharing.yml">Site Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm> and *Client Sharing* draw on the same *Team Members* as people with a CloudCannon account. If you invite someone as a *Team Member* and also turn on *Client Sharing* for them, they count twice and the *Organization* pays for a *Seat* nobody is using. Pick one method for each person.
</comp.Notice>

For more information, please read our documentation on [sharing a Site with Site Sharing](/documentation/user-articles/share-a-site-with-site-sharing/) and [what Client Sharing is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-client-sharing/).

## Which shape fits which client

There is no single right answer, and the cheapest option depends on how many people at the client need access and who you want the invoice to reach.

Give a client their own *Client Organization* when they want to pay CloudCannon directly, when more than one person on their side edits content, when they need their own branding or team, or when you expect to hand the *Sites* over to them later.

Keep a client's *Sites* in your own *Organization* when you cover the cost of CloudCannon yourself, when one or two people on the client's side edit occasionally, or when the engagement is small or a proof of concept.
