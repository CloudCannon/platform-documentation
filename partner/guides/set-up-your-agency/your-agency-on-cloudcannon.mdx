As a web development agency, setting up your clients on a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cms.yml">Content Management System</comp.GlossaryTerm> is a decision about how much control each client gets over their own content, how much of your code and infrastructure they can reach, who pays for the tools that run their website, how your own team prefers to build, and whether you retain ownership over your files. As a Git-backed CMS, CloudCannon allows you to:

* Give each client as much control over their own content as you want.
* Keep each client away from your code and from your other clients' content.
* Decide how each client pays for editing and hosting, and fold that into your existing invoice structure.
* Let your developers keep building in the tools they already use, while everyone else edits the same files through CloudCannon.
* Retain ownership of your files, which stay in a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm> you control rather than a database CloudCannon owns.

When you sign up, CloudCannon will automatically create an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> for you, which acts as your home base for content, settings, and people. As an agency, that can hold your own website, your clients' websites, or both.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Organization-Home-Single-Org" alt="A screenshot of the Organization Home page shows quick links to Sites, Projects, and Domains, alongside a summary of the Organization's usage against its Subscription Plan limits." title="The Organization Home page" type="screenshot"/>

Each *Organization* has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which determines how much you pay for CloudCannon, what features you have access to, and what resource limits you have. CloudCannon provides a range of *Subscription Plans* designed for teams of different sizes and requirements. All the work you do on an *Organization* is billed under a single invoice.

An *Organization* also contains:

* Any number of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Sites</comp.GlossaryTerm>. CloudCannon *Sites* differ from websites, as you can have many *Sites* which are all copies of the same website maintained for different purposes. Copies of the same website, such as its live and draft versions, are grouped into a [Project](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-project/).
* A number of [Custom Domains](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/) (e.g., example.com or company.net). You can connect any CloudCannon *Site* to a *Custom Domain* in your *Organization* and host it live on the internet, with options for subdomains, subpaths, authentication, and more.
* A number of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/seat.yml">Seats</comp.GlossaryTerm>. You can use these *Seats* to invite [Organization Members](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-team-member/) to view, edit, or publish content in your *Organization*, with [Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-permission-groups/) to determine their level of access, or enable <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-sharing.yml">Client Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm> for a specific *Site* to allow people who don't have CloudCannon accounts to log in with a password.
* An amount of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/b/build-time.yml">Build Time</comp.GlossaryTerm> and <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/h/hosting-bandwidth.yml">Hosting Bandwidth</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which set how much you can update your *Sites* each month and how many visitors can view your live websites.

Where you keep your client content will depend on several factors, including which features your clients need, how much of a *Subscription Plan's* resource limits you expect them to use, and how you want to pay for each client. Let's take a look at the options.

## Where does your client content live in CloudCannon?

CloudCannon gives you two places to keep your clients' *Sites*:

* A separate <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/client-organization.yml">Client Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm> linked to yours, which your agency manages on their behalf.
* Your own *Organization*, alongside the rest of your work, with your clients given access to their own content.

CloudCannon has no preference between the two, and you don't have to use the same one for every client. Most agencies run both at once, and you can move a client from one to the other later, which we'll cover further on in this guide.

To use a *Client Organization*, you need to be a member of CloudCannon's <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-program.yml">Partner Program</comp.GlossaryTerm>; keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* does not. Membership is free, and joining turns your *Organization* into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/partner-organization.yml">Partner Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which adds specialized features, increasing discounts on *Subscription Plans*, and an extended *Free Trial* for *Client Organizations* you manage. We'll cover the *Partner Program* in the next step of this guide.

### A Client Organization for each client

A *Client Organization* is a separate *Organization* that you manage on behalf of a client, with its own *Subscription Plan* and invoices, *Organization Members*, *Projects*, *Sites*, *Custom Domains*, *Build Time*, and *Hosting Bandwidth*. Additionally, you can customize the branding for each CloudCannon *Organization*, allowing you to make the *Client Organization* feel like theirs.

In most cases, *Organizations* are entirely independent from one another. However, a *Client Organization* is always linked to a *Partner Organization*. You can have as many *Client Organizations* as you require.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Client-Organization-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Client Organizations Browser in a Partner Organization shows four Client Organizations as cards, each listing its Sites and domains counts, Subscription Plan, and issue status." title="The Client Organizations Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because each *Client Organization* has its own *Subscription Plan* and payment details, you can choose who receives the invoice. Your client can add their own credit card and receive their own invoices, or you can add yours and fold the cost into your existing billing structure.

*Client Organizations* suit clients with their own budgets and content creators. A separate *Subscription Plan* means they have their own limits for *Build Time*, *Hosting Bandwidth*, *Custom Domains*, and *Seats*, so a client with heavy traffic, or one that needs constant work from your agency, never draws down the limits you rely on for everyone else, and the people they invite land on their invoice rather than yours. Your own team is the exception: everyone in your *Partner Organization* can work in every *Client Organization* you manage without using one of your client's *Seats*.

Access to a *Client Organization* works the same way as access to any other *Organization* in CloudCannon. A *Permission Group* defines a set of <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/permissions.yml">Permissions</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each of its members. Every *Organization Member* in an *Organization* must be a member of at least one *Permission Group*.

When you create a *Client Organization*, CloudCannon adds a dedicated *Partner Permission Group* to it that mirrors the membership of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/owners.yml">Owners Permission Group</comp.GlossaryTerm> in your *Partner Organization*. This group has full *Permissions*, so *Owners* can reach the settings, content, and *Organization Members* for every *Client Organization*, and give other members of your agency access by adding them to other *Permission Groups* in each *Client Organization*.

We'll cover how your clients access their content later in this step of the guide.

### Keep client Sites in your own Organization

You can have as many *Sites* and *Projects* in your own *Organization* as you require, which can be your agency's marketing website, or the content of your clients. Storing client work on your own *Organization* means they come under a single *Subscription Plan* and draw from the same pool of *Seats*, *Custom Domains*, *Build Time*, and *Hosting Bandwidth*.

You can organize your clients' *Sites* into one *Project* for each *Git Repository*.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Projects-Browser" alt="A screenshot of the Projects Browser shows a Project card for each client, with the Project's name, description, and status." title="The Projects Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Because every client you manage this way comes under your *Organization's* *Subscription Plan*, you always receive the CloudCannon invoice. You can fold the cost into your existing billing structure alongside maintenance or other services (e.g., monthly development or content creation hours), so your client receives a single invoice from you rather than one from you and one from CloudCannon.

Keeping client *Sites* in your own *Organization* suits smaller clients, or clients with light traffic or development needs. Your agency team members will automatically have access to all your client work, with what they can access determined by the *Permission Groups* they belong to.

This method is also the cheapest way to set up your agency on CloudCannon, using the *Standard Plan*, or the *Lite Plan* available through the *Partner Program*. We'll look at what a minimal agency setup looks like in the next step of this guide.

## How do your clients access their content?

Whichever method you choose to store their *Sites*, you can give your clients access to their content in one of three ways: inviting them as a full member of an *Organization*, usually their own *Client Organization*; inviting them to a specific *Site* in an *Organization*; or enabling *Client Sharing* on a specific *Site* for people without CloudCannon accounts. These differ in how much you can control your clients' permissions and which *Subscription Plan* pays for the *Seat*.

You only need to use one of these methods for each client. For example, if you invite someone to be an *Organization Member* of their *Client Organization*, you do not need to also give them access via <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-sharing.yml">Site Sharing</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

<comp.OptionsTable>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Organization Member" type_markdown="An entire Organization">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to an *Organization*. As an *Organization Member*, clients are members of at least one *Permission Group* which determines what actions they are allowed to take within the *Organization*.

    You can use <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/default-permission-groups.yml">Default Permission Groups</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which provide broad permission categories designed for types of users, or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-permission-group.yml">Custom Permission Groups</comp.GlossaryTerm> for fine-grained permission control if the *Organization* uses the *Team Plan* or *Enterprise Plan*.

    <comp.Notice info_type="important">
      We do not recommend inviting clients as *Organization Members* of your *Partner Organization* unless you use *Custom Permission Groups*, as even the lowest level *Default Permission Group* would allow your client to see and interact with every *Site* you store there, including content that belongs to other clients.
    </comp.Notice>

    * They count as an *Organization Member* in that *Organization*, and contribute to the number of *Seats* on that *Subscription Plan*.
    * If it is a *Client Organization*, you can choose whether the client pays for their *Subscription Plan* directly.
    * Depending on the *Permission Group* of your client, they can invite their own team to the *Organization* without your intervention. Anyone they invite contributes to the number of *Seats* on their *Subscription Plan*.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Organization Member via Site Sharing" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client gets their own CloudCannon account and signs in to an *Organization*, but is limited to interacting with a single *Site*. As an *Organization Member* through *Site Sharing*, clients are members of one of the following *Default Permission Groups*: *[Site Name] Editors* or *[Site Name] Technical Editors*. Only two *Default Permission Groups* are available using this method; you cannot add a client to the *Owners*, *Developers*, or *Billing Permission Groups* this way.

    This method is designed for agencies that keep their clients' content in their agency's *Organization*, rather than a dedicated *Client Organization*. Clients cannot see or interact with *Sites* that belong to other clients.

    If your *Organization* uses the *Team Plan* or *Enterprise Plan*, we recommend using *Custom Permission Groups* instead of *Site Sharing* for finer control over what each client can reach.

    * They count as an *Organization Member* in the *Organization* that owns the *Site*, and contribute to the number of *Seats* on that *Subscription Plan*.
    * You cannot use this method to let clients receive and pay their own invoices directly from CloudCannon.
    * You can share multiple *Sites* using this method without increasing the number of *Seats* used. One *Organization Member* uses one *Seat* on your *Subscription Plan*.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
  <comp.OptionsRow label="Client Sharing" type_markdown="One Site">
    Your client does not need a CloudCannon account. Instead, you enable the *Client Sharing* feature on one *Site* and configure a password. Anyone can log in to CloudCannon with that password and see a simplified *Site Dashboard*. You can only have one password per *Site*.

    * Each *Site* with *Client Sharing* enabled contributes to the number of *Seats* on that *Organization's* *Subscription Plan*, regardless of how many people use the password.
    * You cannot use this method to let clients receive and pay their own invoices directly from CloudCannon.
    * You cannot separate activity on your *Site* between users if you give the *Client Sharing* password to multiple people.
    * If you need to change who has access to your *Site*, you must update the password for every person who uses it.
  </comp.OptionsRow>
</comp.OptionsTable>

## Which method should you use?

Which way you store your clients' content and give them access depends on your agency's priorities.

Join the *Partner Program*, set up a *Client Organization*, and invite your clients as *Organization Members* if your clients:

* Require specific features
* Will use a large amount of resources
* Want multiple members of their own team to contribute to their content
* Want to pay for their CloudCannon invoice directly

Create client *Sites* inside your own *Organization*, and invite your clients using *Site Sharing* or *Client Sharing* if:

* You want to cover the cost of CloudCannon yourself
* Clients only want to edit their content occasionally
* An engagement is small or a proof of concept

You are not locked into these choices, and if your clients' needs change in the future, you can switch your setup whenever you like.
