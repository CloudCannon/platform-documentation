Web hosting is where, after a successful [build](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/), all your files are stored on a web server and made available to the public on the Internet. You can host your <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> through CloudCannon or an external service.

To serve your *Site* on your own address, you need a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-name.yml">domain name</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which you can purchase from any <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/domain-registrar.yml">domain registrar</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

* **Domain name** — The address used to access a website (e.g., <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/">cloudcannon.com</a>).
* **Domain registrar** — A third-party company that sells and renews domain names. The registrar records who owns each domain, and which nameservers answer DNS questions about it (e.g., <a href="https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Cloudflare Registrar</a>, <a href="https://www.namecheap.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Namecheap</a>).

The domain name of your website appears in the URL.

<comp.DocsImage path="assets/diagrams/CloudCannon-Documentation-URL-Structure-Diagram.png" alt="A diagram of a URL shows labels for protocol, subdomain, domain, top-level domain, subdirectory, slug, and path." title="The parts of a URL" type="ui-snippet" />

CloudCannon automatically assigns a free domain name to each *Site*, called a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which you can use to preview your *Site* content live and share it with <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization-member.yml">Organization Members</comp.GlossaryTerm>. To serve your *Site* on a personal or business domain name, you connect it as a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Testing Domain is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/) and [what a Custom Domain is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-custom-domain/).

## Hosting on CloudCannon

A developer, or anyone in your *Organization* with permission to manage *Custom Domains*, sets up hosting on your own domain. To host your *Site* on your own domain through CloudCannon, they:

* Connect a *Custom Domain* to your *Organization*.
* Choose a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/d/dns.yml">DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> provider for it: <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/cloudcannon-dns.yml">CloudCannon DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm> or <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/e/external-dns.yml">external DNS</comp.GlossaryTerm>.
* Add the *Custom Domain* to your *Site*, so CloudCannon serves your *Site* on one of its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subdomain.yml">Subdomains</comp.GlossaryTerm> (e.g., example.com or docs.example.com).

For more information, please read our documentation on [connecting a Custom Domain to your Organization](/documentation/developer-articles/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-organization/) and [adding a Custom Domain to your Site](/documentation/developer-articles/add-a-custom-domain-to-your-site/).

CloudCannon issues and renews an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/ssl-certificate.yml">SSL Certificate</comp.GlossaryTerm> for each *Subdomain* automatically, unless you upload your own. For more information, please read our documentation on [what an SSL Certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/).

For more information on how these pieces fit together, please read our documentation on [how hosting works on CloudCannon](/documentation/developer-articles/how-does-hosting-work-on-cloudcannon/).

### Do you want to host on CloudCannon?

Hosting your *Site* on CloudCannon might be right for you if you want to keep your hosting settings on the same platform you use to edit your content.

By default, CloudCannon serves *Sites* hosted on CloudCannon through a Cloudflare Content Delivery Network (CDN). Some *Subdomains* skip CloudCannon's CDN, depending on the hosting option chosen for each *Subdomain* and on your *DNS Provider*. A CDN is a globally distributed network of servers that improves your *Site's* loading time by caching your content. Visitors to your *Site* can then access a cached copy from a geographically closer server.

Whenever you update your *Site*, your hosting provider must also update these caches to prevent visitors from seeing out-of-date content. CloudCannon uses cache busting so that every time you update the content on your *Site*, CloudCannon will refresh all the cached copies as well.

If you don't want to host your *Site* on CloudCannon, you can use an external provider without interrupting your ability to edit your content in CloudCannon. For more information, please read our documentation on [what external hosting is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-external-hosting/).

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  If you want to host your *Site* externally, you can also disable builds on CloudCannon. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Headless Mode is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-headless-mode/).
</comp.Notice>

### Which combination of DNS Provider and SSL Certificate is right for your Site?

Unless you are familiar with web hosting, we recommend reading our documentation on [what DNS is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-dns/) and [what an SSL Certificate is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-an-ssl-certificate/) before selecting a *DNS Provider* or *SSL Certificate* type.

DNS and SSL often work hand-in-hand. The correct combination of *DNS Provider* and *SSL Certificate* will depend on your desired *Site* configuration. Here are a few common examples:

* **CloudCannon DNS and an auto-generated SSL Certificate** (recommended) — Manage your domain settings and website security in the same place as your content. CloudCannon keeps your DNS records up to date, and issues and renews a certificate for each of your *Subdomains*.
* **External DNS and an auto-generated SSL Certificate** — Manage your domain settings through a third-party service (e.g., Cloudflare), and let CloudCannon issue your *SSL Certificates*. This option is good if you have existing DNS infrastructure.
* **CloudCannon DNS and a Custom SSL Certificate** — Manage your domain settings through CloudCannon, and get your *SSL Certificate* from a third-party service.
* **External DNS and a Custom SSL Certificate** — Manage your domain settings and get your *SSL Certificate* through third-party services. This option is good if you have existing DNS infrastructure and security policies that require you to have an *SSL Certificate* from an internally approved authority.
