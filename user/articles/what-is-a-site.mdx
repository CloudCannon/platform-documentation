A <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site.yml">Site</comp.GlossaryTerm> in CloudCannon is one version of a website, containing all the files, content, configuration, and settings needed to edit, build, and host it. *Sites* are where you do the majority of work in CloudCannon.

Every *Site* belongs to an <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/o/organization.yml">Organization</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which is the workspace your team shares. *Sites* draw on the resources included in your *Organization's* <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/subscription-plan.yml">Subscription Plan</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what an Organization is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-an-organization/).

Each *Site* has its own:

* **Files and content** — The files that make up the website, such as pages, posts, images, code, and data. Related files are grouped into <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/collection.yml">Collections</comp.GlossaryTerm> in the *Site Navigation*, such as all your blog posts or all your staff profiles.
* **A connection to your Git Repository** — *Sites* sync with a branch of the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/g/git-repository.yml">Git Repository</comp.GlossaryTerm> you use to safely store your website. Because all the content is synced from this location, your *Site* and your *Git Repository* stay in step no matter where a change was made, so everyone on your team is working from the same files.
* **Editing interfaces** — The *Visual Editor*, *Content Editor*, *Data Editor*, and *Source Editor* for editing files in your *Collections*. Your developer chooses which of these are available on each *Collection*, so you may not see all four everywhere.
* **Settings** — Everything specific to this version of the website, including its build configuration, domains, and who can reach it.

## The relationship between websites and CloudCannon Sites

One website can have multiple *Sites* on CloudCannon, with each *Site* representing one version of that website. This way you can keep multiple copies for different purposes, such as your live *Site*, a staging *Site* for reviewing changes before they go public, or draft *Sites* for individual members of your team or projects that your team is working on.

Smaller teams may only need one *Site*. Keeping several copies of the same website becomes useful when your team wants to draft and review changes before your visitors see them. Moving changes from one *Site* to another like this is called a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/publishing-workflow.yml">Publishing Workflow</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which is available on our Team or Enterprise plan. *Sites* on our Standard plan can still publish to a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/publish-branch.yml">Publish Branch</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what publishing is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-publishing/).

*Sites* for the same website are independent of each other. Each one keeps its own files, settings, and connection to your *Git Repository*, so changing a setting on your staging *Site* leaves your live *Site* as it was.

<comp.Notice info_type="info">
  Each *Site* connects to one branch of a *Git Repository*, and normally that relationship is one-to-one: one branch is one *Site*. Your developer can also connect several *Sites* to the same branch. For more information, please read our documentation on [how Sites connect to Git branches](/documentation/developer-articles/how-do-sites-connect-to-git-branches/).
</comp.Notice>

Where one website has several *Sites*, you can group them into a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/p/project.yml">Project</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Project is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-project/).

## Where to find your Sites

You can find all the *Sites* in your *Organization* in the *Sites Browser*, which you open from the *Sites* link in the <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/a/app-sidebar.yml">App Sidebar</comp.GlossaryTerm>.

<comp.DocShot docshot_key="CloudCannon-Documentation-Sites-Browser-One-Site" alt="A screenshot of the Sites Browser shows a Site as a Card with its Testing Domain and build status, the Create a Site button above the list, and the Sites link in the App Sidebar." title="The Sites Browser" type="screenshot"/>

Each *Site* in the *Sites Browser* is represented by a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/card.yml">Card</comp.GlossaryTerm>, showing the *Site's* name and a screenshot of the website from its most recent build. The *Card* also names the branch of your *Git Repository* the *Site* syncs with, the *Publish Branch* it publishes to if it has one, and the domain visitors use to reach the website. Finally, it shows when the *Site* last built and synced, and flags either one that failed.

A *Card* also shows a badge for anything that sets the *Site* apart: *Branched* for a *Site* branched from another, *Main* for the main *Site* in a *Project*, *Edited* when someone has unsaved changes open, *Edit locked* or *Build locked* when editing or building is turned off, and *Pending transfer* when the *Site* is on its way to another *Organization*.

If you cannot see a *Site* you expect, you may not have the correct permissions to view it, or it may live in a different *Organization*. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Permission Groups are](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-permission-groups/) and [switching between your Organizations](/documentation/user-articles/switch-between-your-organizations/).

Click on a *Site* in the *Sites Browser* to open its <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/s/site-dashboard.yml">Site Dashboard</comp.GlossaryTerm>, which summarizes recent activity, unsaved changes, and builds for that *Site*. The *Site Header* at the top of the screen shows which *Site* you are viewing. For more information, please read our documentation on [what the Site Header is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-the-site-header/) and [what the Site Navigation is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-the-site-navigation/).

## Creating a Site

There are three ways to create a *Site* in CloudCannon: branch an existing *Site*, connect a branch from your *Git Repository* to a new *Site*, or let CloudCannon create *Sites* for you with *Deploy Previews*. Which ones you can use will depend on your permissions and *Project* configuration.

### Branching an existing Site

If the website is already in CloudCannon and you want your own copy to work on, you can create a branched *Site* yourself. Members of the *Editors* and *Technical Editors* [Default Permission Groups](/documentation/user-articles/what-are-default-permission-groups/) can do this without help from a developer, as long as your developer has already set up a *Project* for the website. Branching is available on our <a href="https://cloudcannon.com/pricing/">Team or Enterprise plan</a>. For more information, please read our documentation on [creating a branched Site](/documentation/user-articles/create-a-branched-site/).

### Connecting a new Site to your Git Repository

Creating a *Site* from scratch means connecting a *Git Repository* and configuring how CloudCannon builds the website. Members of the *Owners* and *Developers* *Default Permission Groups* can create a *Site* this way. For more information, please read our documentation on [getting started with CloudCannon](/documentation/developer-guides/getting-started-with-cloudcannon/), which covers the process from an empty *Organization* to a working *Site*.

### Creating Sites automatically with Deploy Previews

If your developer has turned on *Deploy Previews*, CloudCannon creates a *Site* for each *Pull Request* in your *Git Repository*. CloudCannon names each one after the *Pull Request* it was created for, such as "PR #12: Add a new blog post". *Sites* can appear and disappear from your *Organization* based on your *Deploy Previews* configuration. For more information, please read our documentation on [what Deploy Previews are](/documentation/developer-articles/what-are-deploy-previews/).

## Working on your Sites

Most of your work in CloudCannon happens inside a *Site*.

You write and update your files in the *Visual Editor*, *Content Editor*, *Data Editor*, or *Source Editor*, depending on which of them your developer has turned on for each *Collection*. For more information, please read our documentation on [editing in CloudCannon](/documentation/user-articles/introduction-to-editing/).

CloudCannon builds your *Site* when its content or configuration changes, turning your files into the pages your visitors load. This is separate from publishing, which moves your changes from one *Site* to another. Every *Site* gets a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/t/testing-domain.yml">Testing Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm> for previewing the website privately, and a *Site* serving a live website also has a <comp.GlossaryTerm term="/user/glossary/c/custom-domain.yml">Custom Domain</comp.GlossaryTerm>. For more information, please read our documentation on [what a Site build is](/documentation/developer-articles/what-is-a-site-build/) and [what a Testing Domain is](/documentation/user-articles/what-is-a-testing-domain/).
